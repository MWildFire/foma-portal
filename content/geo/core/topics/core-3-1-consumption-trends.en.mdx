## Big idea

Global material use has grown strongly since 1970, and energy use per person has risen since 2000, while access to food and water remains uneven. Use per person differs widely between income groups. Population, affluence and technology drive consumption, and the <Term id="core-ecological-footprint"/> shows whether that consumption fits within what the planet can regenerate.

## Explanation

Material use shows a long-run rise. The UN International Resource Panel (IRP) reports that global material extraction rose from 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to about 106 billion tonnes in the report’s latest estimate (IRP, Global Resources Outlook 2024). Energy use per person rose by 19.0%, from 1,590 to 1,892 kg of oil equivalent, between 2000 and 2024 (World Bank).

Food and water show that access matters as much as supply. In 2024, 673 million people faced chronic undernourishment; the <Term id="core-undernourishment"/> was 8.2% of the world population, down from 8.7% in 2022 (FAO, SOFI 2025). In the same year, 2.6 billion people could not afford a healthy diet (FAO, 2025). Agriculture took about 71% of freshwater withdrawals in 2022 (World Bank indicator ER.H2O.FWAG.ZS). <Case id="core-3-1-case-brazil-soy"/>

**Drivers.** The <Term id="core-ipat-identity"/> (Ehrlich and Holdren, 1971) states that impact = population × affluence × technology:

- **Population:** more people need more food and water. The world had nearly 8.2 billion people in mid-2024 and is projected to peak at about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s (UN DESA, World Population Prospects 2024).
- **Affluence:** rising <Term id="core-affluence"/> raises demand for energy, transport and goods. The Malthus–Boserup model in topic 1.1 links population to food supply, but it does not explain why use per person rises.
- **Technology:** <Term id="core-resource-productivity"/> rises when technology gives more output per unit of resource, but new products add demand. Lithium-ion batteries are the leading global use of cobalt (USGS, 2026).

**Inequality.** In 2024, energy use per person was 4,454 kg of oil equivalent in high-income countries and 362 kg in low-income countries, about 12 times as much (World Bank). The IRP reports that high-income countries use six times more materials per person than low-income countries (IRP, 2024). Between 2000 and 2024, middle-income energy use per person rose by 89.5%, from 759 to 1,438 kg, while high-income use fell by 8.6% and low-income use fell by 9.0% (World Bank). Income groups are reviewed each year, so group membership can change. A consumption view, the <Term id="core-material-footprint"/>, also counts materials embedded in imports.

**Ecological footprint and overshoot.** The <Term id="core-ecological-footprint"/> converts consumption into the land and sea needed to supply it and absorb its waste. The unit is <Term id="core-global-hectare"/> (gha); totals are in gha and per-person figures in gha/person. It includes cropland, grazing land, forest products, fishing grounds, built-up land and the forest needed to absorb CO₂ from fossil fuels. <Term id="core-biocapacity"/> is ecosystems’ annual capacity to regenerate biological materials and absorb specified wastes, expressed in productivity-weighted gha, not land recreated each year. A footprint larger than biocapacity is <Term id="core-ecological-overshoot"/>: demand draws down natural capital. Global Footprint Network calculates Earth Overshoot Day as

$$
\text{Earth Overshoot Day} = \frac{\text{biocapacity}}{\text{footprint}} \times 365 \text{ days}
$$

In 2026 it fell on 30 July, equivalent to using 1.73 Earths (Global Footprint Network, June 2026).

**Resource security.** <Term id="core-resource-security"/> means reliable access to enough food, water and energy at an affordable price, and the ability to withstand shocks. Its three dimensions, <Term id="core-food-security"/>, <Term id="core-water-security"/> and <Term id="core-energy-security"/>, are linked: agriculture accounted for about 71% of global freshwater withdrawals in 2022, and pumping and treating water needs energy. A disruption in one can spread to the others. Policy can change supply quickly: Indonesia announced in 2019 that its raw nickel ore export ban would start on 1 January 2020 (Al Jazeera, 2019). <Case id="core-3-1-case-indonesia-nickel"/>

**Resource or reserve?** A <Term id="core-natural-resource"/> is a natural material that people use or value. Cobalt is a <Term id="core-non-renewable-resource"/>: its stock falls as it is mined, unlike a <Term id="core-renewable-resource"/> such as wind. A <Term id="core-reserve"/> is the part of a known resource that can be extracted economically and technically now, so it changes with prices, technology and law. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), USGS puts cobalt reserves at 6,000,000 tonnes against 230,000 tonnes of mine output in 2025, about 26 years at that rate (USGS, 2026; calculated). <Case id="core-3-1-case-drc-cobalt"/>

**Poverty reduction and new consumers.** The World Bank’s **June 2025 vintage** estimates 1.5 billion fewer people in extreme poverty between 1990 and 2022, using the revised 3.00 international dollars/day line in **2021 PPP international dollars**. This is a dated comparable-series estimate, not a fall measured by comparing headcounts at the old 2.15 and new 3.00 international-dollar daily lines. Leaving extreme poverty does not automatically make a household middle class. “Middle class” needs an explicit definition: for a classroom scenario we may call households at 75–200% of their national median disposable income, adjusted for household size middle-income; this relative band is not a universal global-dollar threshold. OECD’s 2019 overview reports that, between 1988 and 2011, income growth was strongest around the 40th–60th percentiles of the world distribution, driven substantially by China, India and Indonesia. This describes a changing global consumer base; it is not the same as a national relative middle-income membership count. More households with discretionary spending can increase demand for appliances, mobility and varied diets, while within-group inequality and prices matter.

FAO’s **State of Food and Agriculture 2020**, section “Increased water demand”, links rising income and urbanisation to higher demand for water-intensive foods such as meat and dairy. This is a broad tendency, not a prediction that every person changes diet identically. **Embedded or virtual water** is the water used along a product’s production chain, not just the liquid still inside it. Importing food can spare domestic irrigation water but links the importer to the exporter’s rainfall, aquifers and production practices; water volumes are not environmentally equivalent in wet and scarce locations.

**Compare energy pathways.** Hydrocarbons such as oil and gas provide storable fuels but combustion adds CO₂, and extraction can damage land/water. Nuclear power has low operational CO₂ and can provide steady electricity, but needs capital, cooling, safety management and waste storage. Wind and solar PV have low operational CO₂ and water needs, but variable output requires grids, storage or other flexibility. Hydropower is renewable yet depends on flow and reservoirs and can change habitats and displace people. Compare the same service and full life cycle; **electricity share is not total energy use**, and a target or scenario is not achieved generation. A hypothetical equal service requires 100 units: pathway A supplies 70 from hydrocarbons and 30 from low-carbon sources; B supplies 40 and 60. B’s low-carbon share is 30 percentage points higher, not 30% more total service. This arithmetic alone gives no precise life-cycle emissions saving.

## Worked example

**Example 1: percentage change in energy use per person.** Middle-income countries used 759 kg of oil equivalent per person in 2000 and 1,438 kg in 2024 (World Bank). Calculate the percentage change.

<Steps>
<Step>Change = 1,438 − 759 = 679 kg.</Step>
<Step>Percentage change = 679 ÷ 759 × 100 = 89.5%.</Step>
<Step>Use per person rose by 89.5%, close to a doubling in 24 years. A fall needs a minus sign: for high-income countries, (4,454 − 4,873) ÷ 4,873 × 100 = −8.6%.</Step>
</Steps>

**Example 2: from overshoot day to Earths of demand.** Earth Overshoot Day 2026 fell on 30 July (Global Footprint Network). Calculate the number of Earths of demand that this date implies.

<Steps>
<Step>Day number of 30 July 2026: 31 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 30 = 211.</Step>
<Step>Footprint ÷ biocapacity = 365 ÷ 211 = 1.73.</Step>
<Step>Demand in 2026 equals 1.73 Earths: it would take 1.73 years for the planet to regenerate one year of human demand.</Step>
</Steps>

The definition above concerns the **consumption footprint**. The chart uses the **production footprint** from National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts 2026: demand generated by production within a country. Consumption footprint = production footprint + footprint of imports − footprint of exports. A country that imports many resource-intensive goods can therefore have a larger consumption footprint than its production footprint. Do not interchange the two in comparisons.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing countries by total consumption rather than use per person, so a large population looks like a heavy user.
- Confusing a <Term id="core-reserve"/> with a resource: a reserve is only the part that can be extracted economically now, and it changes with price.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original value when working out a percentage change.
- Treating the ecological footprint as carbon only; carbon is one of six components.
- Using a ranking word without checking the whole data set; quote the figure and the comparison instead.

## Summary

- Global material extraction rose from 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to about 106 billion tonnes in the report’s latest estimate (IRP, 2024).
- Energy use per person rose by 19.0% between 2000 and 2024, from 1,590 to 1,892 kg of oil equivalent (World Bank).
- In 2024, energy use per person in high-income countries was about 12 times that in low-income countries (4,454 and 362 kg; World Bank).
- Impact = population × affluence × technology; the world had nearly 8.2 billion people in mid-2024 (UN DESA).
- Ecological overshoot means the footprint exceeds biocapacity; Earth Overshoot Day 2026 fell on 30 July, equivalent to 1.73 Earths (Global Footprint Network).
- A reserve is the economically extractable part of a resource; DRC cobalt reserves are estimated at 6,000,000 tonnes against 230,000 tonnes of 2025 output (USGS, 2026).
