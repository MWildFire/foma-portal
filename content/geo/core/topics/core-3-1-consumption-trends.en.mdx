## Big idea

Global material use has grown strongly since 1970, and energy use per person has risen since 2000, while access to food and water remains uneven. Use per person differs widely between income groups. Population, affluence and technology drive consumption, and the <Term id="core-ecological-footprint"/> shows whether that consumption fits within what the planet can regenerate.

## Explanation

Material use shows a long-run rise. The UN International Resource Panel (IRP) reports that global material extraction rose from 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to about 106 billion tonnes in the report’s latest estimate (IRP, Global Resources Outlook 2024). Energy use per person rose by 19.0%, from 1,590 to 1,892 kg of oil equivalent, between 2000 and 2024 (World Bank).

Food and water show that access matters as much as supply. In 2024, 673 million people faced chronic undernourishment; the <Term id="core-undernourishment"/> was 8.2% of the world population, down from 8.7% in 2022 (FAO, SOFI 2025). In the same year, 2.6 billion people could not afford a healthy diet (FAO, 2025). Agriculture took about 71% of freshwater withdrawals in 2022 (FAO AQUASTAT, via Our World in Data). <Case id="core-3-1-case-brazil-soy"/>

**Drivers.** The <Term id="core-ipat-identity"/> (Ehrlich and Holdren, 1971) states that impact = population × affluence × technology:

- **Population:** more people need more food and water. The world had nearly 8.2 billion people in mid-2024 and is projected to peak at about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s (UN DESA, World Population Prospects 2024).
- **Affluence:** rising <Term id="core-affluence"/> raises demand for energy, transport and goods. The Malthus–Boserup model in topic 1.1 links population to food supply, but it does not explain why use per person rises.
- **Technology:** <Term id="core-resource-productivity"/> rises when technology gives more output per unit of resource, but new products add demand. Lithium-ion batteries are the leading global use of cobalt (USGS, 2026).

**Inequality.** In 2024, energy use per person was 4,454 kg of oil equivalent in high-income countries and 362 kg in low-income countries, about 12 times as much (World Bank). The IRP reports that high-income countries use six times more materials per person than low-income countries (IRP, 2024). Between 2000 and 2024, middle-income energy use per person rose by 89.5%, from 759 to 1,438 kg, while high-income use fell by 8.6% and low-income use fell by 9.0% (World Bank). Income groups are reviewed each year, so group membership can change. A consumption view, the <Term id="core-material-footprint"/>, also counts materials embedded in imports.

**Ecological footprint and overshoot.** The <Term id="core-ecological-footprint"/> converts consumption into the land and sea needed to supply it and absorb its waste. The unit is <Term id="core-global-hectare"/>, counted per person. It includes cropland, grazing land, forest products, fishing grounds, built-up land and the forest needed to absorb CO₂ from fossil fuels. <Term id="core-biocapacity"/> is the area the planet can regenerate in one year, in the same units. A footprint larger than biocapacity is <Term id="core-ecological-overshoot"/>: demand draws down natural capital. Global Footprint Network calculates Earth Overshoot Day as

$$
\text{Earth Overshoot Day} = \frac{\text{biocapacity}}{\text{footprint}} \times 365 \text{ days}
$$

In 2026 it fell on 30 July, equivalent to using 1.73 Earths (Global Footprint Network, June 2026).

**Resource security.** <Term id="core-resource-security"/> means reliable access to enough food, water and energy at an affordable price, and the ability to withstand shocks. Its three dimensions, <Term id="core-food-security"/>, <Term id="core-water-security"/> and <Term id="core-energy-security"/>, are linked: farming takes about 71% of freshwater withdrawals, and pumping and treating water needs energy. A disruption in one can spread to the others. Policy can change supply quickly: Indonesia announced in 2019 that its raw nickel ore export ban would start on 1 January 2020 (Al Jazeera, 2019). <Case id="core-3-1-case-indonesia-nickel"/>

**Resource or reserve?** A <Term id="core-natural-resource"/> is a natural material that people use or value. Cobalt is a <Term id="core-non-renewable-resource"/>: its stock falls as it is mined, unlike a <Term id="core-renewable-resource"/> such as wind. A <Term id="core-reserve"/> is the part of a known resource that can be extracted economically and technically now, so it changes with prices, technology and law. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), USGS puts cobalt reserves at 6,000,000 tonnes against 230,000 tonnes of mine output in 2025, about 26 years at that rate (USGS, 2026; calculated). <Case id="core-3-1-case-drc-cobalt"/>

## Worked example

**Example 1: percentage change in energy use per person.** Middle-income countries used 759 kg of oil equivalent per person in 2000 and 1,438 kg in 2024 (World Bank). Calculate the percentage change.

<Steps>
<Step>Change = 1,438 − 759 = 679 kg.</Step>
<Step>Percentage change = 679 ÷ 759 × 100 = 89.5%.</Step>
<Step>Use per person rose by 89.5%, close to a doubling in 24 years. A fall needs a minus sign: for high-income countries, (4,454 − 4,873) ÷ 4,873 × 100 = −8.6%.</Step>
</Steps>

**Example 2: from overshoot day to Earths of demand.** Earth Overshoot Day 2026 fell on 30 July (Global Footprint Network). Calculate the number of Earths of demand that this date implies.

<Steps>
<Step>Day number of 30 July 2026: 31 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 30 = 211.</Step>
<Step>Footprint ÷ biocapacity = 365 ÷ 211 = 1.73.</Step>
<Step>Demand in 2026 equals 1.73 Earths: it would take 1.73 years for the planet to regenerate one year of human demand.</Step>
</Steps>

## How it is examined

Section A questions carry 2 to 6 marks each. Section B (10 marks) uses an infographic and has parts (a) to (c). The command terms reward different things:

- **Outline**: one correct driver per mark, with a brief description or example.
- **Calculate**: each step of the working, the answer and its unit. Method marks are given even when the final answer is wrong.
- **Explain**: a cause, the link to resource use and the effect. A definition alone earns no Explain marks.
- **Using the infographic** and **Describe**: quote values, years and units from the figure.
- **Evaluate**: a point for and a point against the claim, the limits of the data, and a judgement.

<Callout kind="exam">Quote a year and a source with every figure. Compare the numbers themselves, and use a ranking word only when the whole data set supports it.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Comparing countries by total consumption rather than use per person, so a large population looks like a heavy user.
- Confusing a <Term id="core-reserve"/> with a resource: a reserve is only the part that can be extracted economically now, and it changes with price.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original value when working out a percentage change.
- Treating the ecological footprint as carbon only; carbon is one of six components.
- Using a ranking word without checking the whole data set; quote the figure and the comparison instead.

## Summary

- Global material extraction rose from 30 billion tonnes in 1970 to about 106 billion tonnes in the report’s latest estimate (IRP, 2024).
- Energy use per person rose by 19.0% between 2000 and 2024, from 1,590 to 1,892 kg of oil equivalent (World Bank).
- In 2024, energy use per person in high-income countries was about 12 times that in low-income countries (4,454 and 362 kg; World Bank).
- Impact = population × affluence × technology; the world had nearly 8.2 billion people in mid-2024 (UN DESA).
- Ecological overshoot means the footprint exceeds biocapacity; Earth Overshoot Day 2026 fell on 30 July, equivalent to 1.73 Earths (Global Footprint Network).
- A reserve is the economically extractable part of a resource; DRC cobalt reserves are estimated at 6,000,000 tonnes against 230,000 tonnes of 2025 output (USGS, 2026).
