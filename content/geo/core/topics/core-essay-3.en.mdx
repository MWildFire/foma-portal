## Big idea

A 10-mark Section C essay on resource consumption is one argument, not a list of resources. You answer the question in a clear thesis, support it with developed analytical paragraphs using relevant, accurate evidence, weigh the other side, and finish with a direct answer. Marks follow the judgement and the evidence, not the amount of description.

## Explanation

A Section C essay is worth 10 marks. It tests four things: accurate knowledge, application of that knowledge to named places, analysis of causes and effects, and a judgement that answers the question. Clear communication, with a logical structure and precise terms, supports all four.

Unit 3 asks four kinds of question, and the command term shows which one:

- Security: is rising consumption a threat to food, water and energy supplies? (To what extent)
- Impacts: who bears the costs of resource use, the country that buys the goods or the country that produces them? (Discuss)
- Management: do stewardship methods such as quotas, certification, recycling and governance work in a named case? (Evaluate)
- Links: how do water, food and energy trade off against each other, and against conservation? (Examine)

One possible practice structure is:

- Introduction (3 to 4 sentences): define the key term briefly, then state a thesis that answers the question and says how far it is true.
- Three developed points: each makes a claim, explains the mechanism, supports it with relevant evidence and links back to the question; use accurate dates, units and figures where they help.
- Evaluation: weigh the other side, state the conditions under which your view holds, and say which factor matters most.
- Conclusion (1 to 2 sentences): answer the question directly. Do not add new evidence.

<Callout kind="define">An ecological footprint expresses demand for biologically productive area in global hectares (gha), including resources and absorption of carbon emissions. Biocapacity measures regeneration. Global demand above global biocapacity is ecological overshoot. A national consumption footprint above national biocapacity is an ecological deficit; trade and emissions to the global atmosphere mean this does not alone prove overuse of local ecosystems.</Callout>

Two accounts matter in Unit 3. A production footprint counts the land and sea used to make goods inside a country. A consumption footprint adds the footprint embodied in imports and subtracts that embodied in exports from the production footprint. The two can differ, so always say which one you mean.

Method: [Global Footprint Network glossary](https://footprintnetwork.org/resources/glossary/), entries EFC, EFP, ecological deficit and overshoot.

Some terms need care in this unit:

- Resource security is reliable access to enough of a resource, at a price people can pay, without serious disruption.
- A reserve is the part of a known deposit that can be extracted at current prices and with current technology. It is not the total amount in the ground.
- Stewardship is managing a resource so that it is used efficiently and kept for future use.
- A quota limits how much may be taken, sold or exported. Certification is an independent check that a product meets set standards. Governance is the set of rules and institutions that decide who uses a shared resource and how.

Evidence separates a weak essay from a strong one. A named place with a figure, a year and a source is worth far more than a general claim such as "in many countries". Use each figure as its source gives it, with "about" for rounded numbers. If you calculate something, show the inputs: for example, the world footprint of production (2.56 global hectares per person) divided by biocapacity (1.50) gives 1.7 (National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts, 2026 edition). Do not set a stock (reserves) against an annual flow (output) as if they were the same kind of number.

Balance is essential. Discuss, Evaluate and To what extent all reward the other side: the case for, the case against and the conditions under which your view holds. A conclusion that says "both matter" earns little without a reason.

Time and planning: the practice essay has 20 minutes. Spend about three minutes on the plan before the first sentence.

## Worked example

**Question:** To what extent does rising consumption threaten global resource security in food, water and energy? (10 marks)

The plan covers all three sectors. Use the linked Unit 3 cases and the dated source values below; the figures are evidence to evaluate, not a direct estimate of consumption’s causal effect.

**Plan**

1. Thesis: rising consumption can threaten food, water and energy security, but the extent depends on local access, infrastructure, substitution and supply governance; a single global indicator cannot rank all three.
2. Energy: DRC cobalt concentration and export quotas can disrupt battery-material supply. Link this to energy storage and transport electrification, while distinguishing a material bottleneck from a measured interruption to electricity supply.
3. Food: FAO SOFI 2025 reports hunger falling from 8.5% in 2023 to 8.2% in 2024, but 2.6 billion people unable to afford a healthy diet. Explain how demand and input costs can affect price and access; the aggregate improvement does not prove every region is secure.
4. Water: Reclamation reported Colorado system storage around 36% of capacity in April 2026; its basin overview assigns about 70% of river water to agriculture. Explain competition between irrigation, cities and energy, without attributing the storage figure to demand alone: drought and supply matter too.
5. Judgement: quantify each case on its own terms. Distinguish snapshots from trends and mechanisms from measured causal effects; evaluate conservation, substitution and access policies.

**Model paragraph**

The threat is conditional rather than uniform. For energy, the USGS 2026 cobalt summary describes the DRC as supplying about 73% of world mined cobalt in 2025 and records export quotas for 2026–2027. Because lithium-ion batteries are cobalt’s leading global use, concentrated supply can expose parts of the energy transition to price and availability shocks. This is not evidence of a particular electricity outage, and cobalt-free substitutes can change demand. Food security concerns access as well as output: FAO SOFI 2025 reports 8.2% of people facing hunger in 2024, down from 8.5% in 2023, while 2.6 billion could not afford a healthy diet. A global improvement does not remove local price and distribution risks. In water, Colorado system storage was around 36% of capacity in April 2026, while agriculture accounts for about 70% of river water in the basin overview. Growing demand can intensify competition, but drought and allocation rules also shape the outcome. Rising consumption therefore poses a substantial risk where supply and access cannot adjust; these different indicators cannot by themselves establish a universal ranking or measure consumption’s causal contribution.

<Reveal title="What the footprint cannot show">The 2023 global footprint of 2.56 gha per person divided by biocapacity of 1.50 gives about 1.71 Earth equivalents in the stated accounting model. It is an aggregate demand for biological area, with carbon about 60% of the footprint. It does not measure water storage, mineral reserves or the security of every food and energy supply. A separate mechanism and evidence are needed for each of those claims.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

Use the school’s printed Section C instructions and timing. The five 10-mark bands in the actual May 2019 Paper 2 markscheme (printed pp.3–4) assess the whole response, not a fixed number of countries, statistics or paragraphs. A 7–8 response can remain unbalanced; the 9–10 band requires well-developed evaluation and a justified, balanced conclusion where appropriate. Our question-specific checklists are revision guidance, not fixed marks per bullet. See the [Unit 1 essay method](#/s/geo/t/core-essay-1) for a fuller paraphrase.

Answer the exact command, explain mechanisms, integrate relevant evidence and assess its limits. Use a named case when the question requests one. Do not invent a figure or assume every point requires a number.

## Common mistakes

- Describing resources and figures without answering the question. A list of minerals, percentages or products earns few marks unless it is linked to a judgement.
- Mixing up the two footprint accounts. Say whether a figure is a production footprint or a consumption footprint, and do not compare them as if they were the same.
- Treating reserves as the total supply, or as the year the resource runs out. A reserve is a known deposit that can be extracted economically; the ratio of reserves to annual output is only a rough guide.
- Giving a figure without its year or source, or with the wrong year. Footprint figures are for 2023, cobalt output for 2025 and Amazon clearing for the year from August 2024 to July 2025.
- Writing about stewardship in general terms, without testing a method against a named case and its result.

## Summary

- A Unit 3 essay is one argument: a thesis, developed analytical points, a judgement and a direct conclusion; three points are one workable practice structure, not a markband quota.
- Name the resource (food, water, energy or a mineral) and the account (production or consumption) for every figure.
- Use the Unit 3 cases and figures with their years and sources, for example cobalt and nickel, the Colorado River, the Aral Sea and the footprint accounts.
- Discuss asks for both sides and a view; Evaluate asks for a weighed judgement on a method; To what extent asks how far; Examine asks how and why, with the links explained.
- Stewardship methods such as quotas, certification, recycling and governance are judged by their results in a named case, not by their description.
- Keep the judgement in the thesis, test it in the evaluation and give the answer in the conclusion, without new evidence.
