## Explanation

### 1.1, 1.2 — PPC and circular flow

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/what-is-economics) · Tragakes pp. 16, 20–21

Explain points on, inside and outside a PPC; movement along it shows scarcity and opportunity cost. Trace goods, factors and money in two-sector and five-sector circular flow models. Low revision priority.

### 2.1 — Demand

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/demand) · Tragakes pp. 43, 46, 48–49

Define demand and explain ceteris paribus. Distinguish a price movement from shifts caused by income (normal/inferior goods), tastes, substitutes, complements and buyer numbers. HL: diminishing marginal utility, income and substitution effects.

### 2.2 — Supply

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/supply) · Tragakes pp. 50, 52, 55

Define supply. Explain movements and shifts: factor costs, technology, competitive and joint supply, expectations, taxes, subsidies, firm numbers and shocks. HL: diminishing marginal returns.

### 2.3 — Equilibrium and surplus

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/competitive-market-equilibrium) · Tragakes pp. 59, 64, 67, 69

Predict changes in P and Q after demand/supply shifts. Define signalling and incentive functions. Explain D = MB, S = MC and allocative efficiency at MB = MC. Define and calculate consumer and producer surplus. Firm cost/revenue curves are outside this mock.

### 2.4 — Consumer behaviour (HL)

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/critique-maximizing-behaviour) · Tragakes pp. 73–78

Define rational choice. Explain rules of thumb, anchoring, framing and availability; bounded rationality, self-control and selfishness; imperfect information. Explain nudge theory and choice architecture. Business objectives are outside the checklist.

### 2.5 — PED and YED

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/price-elasticity-of-demand) · Tragakes pp. 88, 90, 96, 99, 103

Define and calculate PED for price increases and decreases; interpret steep/flat curves with their scale held constant. Explain substitutes, necessities/luxuries, time and budget share; all four price–revenue cases. HL: changing PED along a straight demand curve and primary/manufactured goods. Calculate YED; distinguish inferior, income-inelastic normal and income-elastic normal goods. HL: changing sectoral structure. XED is outside the mock.

### 2.6 — PES

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/elasticity-of-supply) · Tragakes pp. 106, 109

Define and calculate PES in both directions; interpret curves. Explain time, factor mobility, spare capacity, stocks and rising costs. HL: compare primary and manufactured goods after demand shifts.

### 2.7 — Government intervention

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/government-taxes-subsidies-price-controls) · Tragakes pp. 116, 120–125, 130, 133, 136, 140

Explain revenue, support for firms/low-income households and changes in production/consumption. Define ceilings, floors, indirect taxes and subsidies; draw outcomes and evaluate stakeholders. HL: calculate prices, quantities, spending, revenue, surplus and welfare loss (TYU 4.2, 4.4, 4.7, 4.9).

### 2.8 — Externalities and common access resources

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/externalities) · Tragakes pp. 151–152, 165–167, 175–180

Define externality, positive and negative effects; distinguish MPB/MSB and MPC/MSC. For all four production/consumption cases explain the market outcome, social optimum and welfare between them; calculate the loss triangle. Evaluate Pigouvian/carbon taxes, cap-and-trade, regulation, education, subsidies, direct provision and HL nudges. Carbon tax is per tonne of emissions. Explain overfishing/dirty energy and costs for future generations; licences, permits and taxes as remedies.

### 2.9 — Public goods

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/public-goods) · Tragakes pp. 144–145, 182–186

Define non-rivalry, non-excludability and free riding. Explain why non-excludability discourages payment. Compare direct government provision with contracting private producers. Vertical summation calculations are outside this checklist.

### 2.10 — Asymmetric information (HL)

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/asymmetric-information) · Tragakes pp. 187–192

Define asymmetric information, adverse selection and moral hazard. Explain selection when sellers know more and when buyers know more; evaluate disclosure, screening, signalling and regulation. Explain hidden risk-taking after agreement and responses such as monitoring, deductibles and co-payments.

### 2.12 — Market inequality (HL)

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/equity-and-market-failure) · Tragakes pp. 193–194

Explain why different ownership of resources and different market rewards generate unequal incomes in a free market. Detailed redistribution belongs to 3.4.

### 3.1 — Measuring economic activity

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/measuring-economic-activity) · Tragakes pp. 248–249, 258, 261

Draw two-/five-sector circular flow. Use expenditure GDP; distinguish GDP/GNI, nominal/real, total/per capita and PPP-adjusted GDP. Calculate GDP deflator and real GDP (TYU 8.4). Explain expansion, peak, contraction, trough and potential output. GDP limitations and HDI are outside this mock.

### 3.2 — AD/AS

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/ad-as-model-and-business-cycle) · Tragakes pp. 272, 278–280, 283, 285, 288, 291

Define AD and AS. Explain components C, I, G, X−M and their determinants; upward SRAS and wage/input/tax/subsidy/shock shifts. Draw short-run equilibrium and changes. Compare vertical LRAS and flexible-price self-correction with Keynesian AS and downward wage/price rigidity. Explain gaps, potential output, factor quantity/quality, technology, efficiency, institutions and natural unemployment. Compare policy roles and conditions for growth without inflation.

### 3.3 — Macro objectives

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/macroeconomic-objectives) · Tragakes pp. 302, 304, 314–315, 324–326, 335–336

Calculate unemployment using the labour force. Explain structural, frictional, seasonal and cyclical unemployment in both models and its costs. Define inflation, deflation and disinflation; HL price basket index (TYU 10.3); CPI bias, demand-pull/cost-push, costs and causes of deflation. HL: SRPC, stagflation, LRPC/NRU. Calculate real growth (TYU 11.1); AD-driven short-run and AS-driven long-run growth, benefits and trade-offs. HL: debt/deficit/surplus, debt-to-GDP and servicing, ratings, future budgets, inequality, crowding out and debt traps.

### 3.4 — Inequality and poverty

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/inequality-and-poverty) · Tragakes pp. 353–382

Distinguish equality/equity; define inequality, Lorenz curve, Gini, absolute/relative poverty. HL: plot cumulative income quintiles. Explain poverty measurement and difficulties; eight causes: opportunity, resources, human capital, discrimination, power, tax/benefits, globalisation/technology, market-based supply policies. Assess growth, living standards and stability. Define direct/indirect, proportional/progressive/regressive taxes and transfers. HL: indirect tax from expenditure, total tax, average and marginal rates. Evaluate other poverty policies.

### 3.5 — Monetary policy

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/monetary-policy) · Tragakes pp. 388–397

Define policy, commercial/central banks and inflation targeting; explain policy goals. HL: bank money creation, OMO, reserve requirement, minimum lending rate, QE; money demand/supply and equilibrium interest rate diagram. Distinguish real/nominal interest. Draw expansion/contraction closing gaps. Evaluate low rates/confidence limits against incremental, flexible, reversible action and short lags.

### 3.6 — Fiscal policy and multiplier

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/fiscal-policy) · Tragakes pp. 398–411

Define fiscal policy and six goals. Draw expansion/contraction. HL: define MPC/MPS/MPT/MPM, calculate multiplier and GDP effect of I/G/X injections (p.410 diagram). Evaluate politics, lags and debt; HL crowding out (p.403), automatic stabilizers. Explain targeting and effectiveness in deep recession.

### 3.7 — Supply-side policies

[Open topic](#/s/econ/t/supply-side-policies) · Tragakes pp. 413–421, 424–430

Explain five goals: capacity/growth, competition/efficiency, labour flexibility, inflation/competitiveness and investment incentives. Compare market-based competition, labour and tax incentives with interventionist education/training, health, R&D, infrastructure and industrial policies. Explain demand-side effects and fiscal supply-side effects. Evaluate equity, lags, vested interests, environment, costs and benefits. Debate potential output, incentives and minimum wages; choose policies for unemployment, inflation and growth.

## How it is examined

P1: choose one of two Unit 3 questions; 25 marks, 75 minutes plus 5 minutes reading. Explain (10 marks, about 30 minutes), then discuss/evaluate (15 marks, about 45 minutes). Weight 2/8.

P2: one compulsory data response; 40 marks, 105 minutes, no reading period. Parts a–f: define, calculate, draw, show, explain. Part g: discuss (15 marks). Weight 3/8.

P3: one compulsory policy question; 32 marks, 60 minutes. Calculate, outline, explain, comment on, draw, recommend. Reserve about 20–25 minutes for the final recommendation. Weight 3/8. Type your answers.

AO1 recalls meaning: define, describe, list, outline, state. AO2 develops reasoning: analyse, apply, comment, distinguish, explain, suggest. AO3 weighs arguments: compare, compare and contrast, contrast, discuss, evaluate, examine, justify, recommend, to what extent. AO4 performs a skill: calculate, construct, derive, determine, draw, identify, label, measure, plot, show, show that, sketch, solve.

## Common mistakes
