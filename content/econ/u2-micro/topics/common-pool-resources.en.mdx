## Big idea

A common access resource is rivalrous but difficult to exclude people from. An individual user may ignore damage to other users and future generations, so private decisions can lead to overuse.

## Explanation

<Term id="common-pool-resource"/> combines <Term id="rivalry"/> with <Term id="non-excludability"/>. Catching a fish removes it from the stock available to others; without effective access rules the user need not pay the full cost of depletion. Dirty energy also uses the environment’s capacity to absorb pollution, imposing costs on future generations.

In the school model, demand is MPB = MSB, while MSC lies above MPC because extraction imposes an external cost. The market chooses Qm where MPB = MPC. The social optimum Qopt is where MSB = MSC. Between Qopt and Qm, extra social costs exceed extra social benefits: reducing this overuse increases welfare. This is the externality diagram in Tragakes ch.5, p.152, Fig.5.3.

Licences restrict who can use the resource; permits or catch limits restrict how much. Tradable permits allow rights to move between users, while a Pigouvian tax makes extraction more costly to the user. A carbon tax targets each tonne of emissions. Compare monitoring, enforcement, ecological information, distribution and cross-border cooperation. A rule set using poor stock estimates can still allow depletion.

## Worked example

In an illustrative externality diagram, Qm = 12 and Qopt = 9 units. The vertical marginal-cost gap at Qm is 3 currency units per unit of output, and the curves are straight. Welfare loss = 0.5 × (12 − 9) × 3 = 4.5 currency units. Cutting use to 9 removes the units whose social cost exceeds benefit; it does not mean all fishing should stop.

## How it is examined

In P2 data response and P3, explain rivalry and non-excludability, then connect private decisions to social depletion costs. Draw the MSC/MPC model or calculate a quota or welfare area when asked. For a 15-mark P2(g) discussion, weigh the effectiveness and limits of licences, permits, taxes or caps using the data; for P3, justify a recommendation. Include future generations among affected stakeholders. School P1 is macroeconomics only; the TR/TC fishing-effort extension is outside this mock.

## Common mistakes

- A shared resource is rivalrous; a public good is not.
- Sustainable catch is not automatically the welfare-maximising catch.
- Rules require monitoring and enforcement.
- Do not substitute the optional TR/TC model for the checklist externality diagram.

## Summary

- Rivalry makes another user’s extraction costly to others.
- Non-excludability weakens control of access.
- MPC can omit depletion and pollution costs.
- Qm exceeds Qopt in the negative-production-externality model.
- Policies must change incentives or restrict access, with enforcement.
