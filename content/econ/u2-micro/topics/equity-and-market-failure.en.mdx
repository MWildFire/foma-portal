## Big idea

The 2.12 checklist asks why free markets generate income inequality (HL). Detailed redistribution and tax calculations belong to the included macro topic 3.4.

## Explanation

Market income reflects ownership of factors and the prices paid for their services. People inherit unequal amounts of land, property and financial wealth; those with assets receive rent, interest and profit as well as wages. Differences in education, skills and experience affect productivity and earnings. Opportunity is unequal when some households cannot finance training. Discrimination and unequal bargaining power can also change rewards. Therefore market efficiency does not guarantee equal or fair incomes. <Term id="equity"/> concerns fairness, which is a judgement, not simply numerical equality. Tragakes ch.6, pp.193–194; ch.12, pp.365–369.

## Worked example

Two equally diligent workers earn different total incomes: one owns a rented flat and has specialist training, while the other has no assets and could not afford the course. Explain the resource-ownership and human-capital channels separately; do not assume effort is the sole cause.

## Visual

Write a chain: inherited assets/education → factor ownership or productivity → market reward → household income. Add discrimination as a separate possible channel.

## How it is examined

For P2 data response and P3, explain why market outcomes can produce unequal incomes, using differences in skills, assets, market power or opportunity in the data. Distinguish equality of income from fairness. Taxation and redistribution are taught in [3.4 inequality and poverty](#/s/econ/t/inequality-and-poverty), where you can develop evaluation for P2(g) or a P3 recommendation. School P1 is Unit 3 only.

## Common mistakes

- Unequal income does not prove unequal effort.
- Efficient allocation does not imply fairness.
- Equality and equity have different meanings.

## Check yourself

Give two mechanisms producing unequal market incomes, then explain why equal treatment today may not remove unequal starting opportunities.

## Summary

- Resources are unequally owned.
- Skills and access to education differ.
- Market rewards differ across factors and jobs.
- Discrimination and bargaining power can widen gaps.
- Equity is a judgement about fairness.
