## Big idea

Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how strongly quantity demanded responds to a change in price. It is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price, so it can be compared across goods. It shows a firm what a price change does to revenue, and a government how much revenue a tax will raise.

Demand slopes downwards, so PED is negative. Keep the minus sign in your working, because it shows that price and quantity move in opposite directions. Quote the absolute value only when the question asks for it.

<Callout kind="define">Price elasticity of demand: the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price.</Callout>

The size of PED sorts demand into three groups. <Term id="elastic-demand"/> means the absolute value is greater than 1, so quantity changes by a larger percentage than price. <Term id="inelastic-demand"/> means the absolute value is less than 1, so quantity changes by a smaller percentage. <Term id="unit-elastic-demand"/> means the absolute value is exactly 1, so the two percentage changes are equal. Several factors decide how elastic demand is. Goods with many close substitutes, such as one brand of cola among many, have elastic demand, because buyers can switch. A good that takes a large share of income, such as a car, is more elastic than one that takes a small share, such as salt. A <Term id="necessity-good"/>, such as basic medicine, tends to be inelastic, while a <Term id="luxury-good"/>, such as a designer watch, tends to be elastic. Demand is more elastic over a longer period, because buyers have time to find alternatives. Addictive goods, such as cigarettes, tend to be inelastic.

PED shows what a price change does to <Term id="total-revenue"/>, which is the price multiplied by the quantity sold:

<Formula id="total-revenue"/>

If demand is inelastic, quantity falls by a smaller percentage than the price rises, so total revenue rises. If demand is elastic, quantity falls by a larger percentage, so total revenue falls, and a price cut raises it. If demand is unit elastic, total revenue is at its maximum on a straight-line demand curve. Firms use PED when they set prices: a price rise can raise revenue if demand is inelastic, but costs and rivals' reactions still matter. Governments use PED when they tax goods. A tax on a good with inelastic demand, such as fuel or tobacco, raises more revenue because quantity falls only a little. A tax on a good with elastic demand raises less revenue and changes buyers' behaviour more.

HL — Tragakes ch.3, p.96: primary products often have price-inelastic demand because they are necessities with few close substitutes. A supply shock can therefore cause a large price change and volatile producer revenue. Manufactured products often have more substitutes and more elastic demand; the same supply shift causes a smaller price movement, other things equal. A steep/flat comparison only works with consistent units and scale; along a straight demand curve slope is constant while PED changes.

## Worked example

Demand for a product is Q = 24 - 2P, where Q is units per week and P is the price in AED. The price rises from AED 4 to AED 5.

<Steps>
<Step>Quantity at AED 4: Q = 24 - 2 x 4 = 16 units. Quantity at AED 5: Q = 24 - 2 x 5 = 14 units.</Step>
<Step>Percentage change in quantity = (14 - 16) / 16 x 100 = -12.5%.</Step>
<Step>Percentage change in price = (5 - 4) / 4 x 100 = +25%.</Step>
<Step>PED = -12.5 / 25 = -0.5. The absolute value is below 1, so demand is inelastic.</Step>
<Step>Total revenue: 4 x 16 = AED 64 before, and 5 x 14 = AED 70 after: revenue rises, as inelastic demand predicts.</Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by the new value instead of the original value, which gives a different percentage change.
- Dropping the minus sign and then writing the answer as if demand rose with price.
- Calling a good inelastic only because it is expensive; elasticity compares percentage changes, not price levels.
- Saying that a price rise always increases revenue; it depends on whether demand is elastic or inelastic.

## Summary

- PED is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price; for a normal demand curve it is negative.
- An absolute value above 1 means elastic demand, below 1 inelastic, and exactly 1 unit elastic.
- With elastic demand a price rise lowers total revenue; with inelastic demand it raises total revenue.
- Demand is more elastic with close substitutes, a large share of income, a luxury good, or a longer period.
- Firms use PED to set prices; governments use it to predict revenue from taxes on goods.
- In every calculation, show both percentage changes, the sign and the conclusion.
