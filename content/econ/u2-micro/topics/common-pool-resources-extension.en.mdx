## Big idea

Common pool resources are rivalrous and non-excludable, so each user gains from using more while paying only a small share of the cost. Without rules or prices the resource is overused, and open access leads to a different outcome from the socially optimal use.

## Explanation

Goods differ in two features. A rival good is used up or congested when one person uses it, and an excludable good can be withheld from people who do not pay. A <Term id="common-pool-resource"/> is both rivalrous and non-excludable, as in a fishing bay, groundwater or a shared pasture.

<Callout kind="define">Common pool resource: a resource that is rivalrous and non-excludable, so each user's extraction reduces what is left for others and no one can easily be kept out.</Callout>

Two features cause overuse. <Term id="rivalry"/> means that one boat's catch leaves fewer fish for the other boats. <Term id="non-excludability"/> means that no owner can charge a price or keep boats out, so entry is free. Each boat compares only its own benefit with its own cost, while the cost of a smaller future stock is shared among all users.

In the simplified fishing-effort model, unrestricted entry continues until total revenue equals total cost, so economic profit is zero. A smaller effort maximises the revenue–cost gap where MR = MC. This represents socially optimal use only if the model includes all relevant social benefits and costs; the main textbook diagram instead locates the optimum at MSB = MSC. The result is the <Term id="tragedy-of-the-commons"/>: each user acts rationally, but the resource is overexploited and total profit is lower than it could be.

Governments can limit use. A <Term id="harvest-quota"/> caps the total catch and shares it out. <Term id="property-rights"/> give an owner a reason to conserve, and <Term id="tradable-permits"/> let fishing rights move to the most efficient boats. A tax on each tonne caught works like a <Term id="pigouvian-tax"/>. Each measure needs monitoring and reliable data on the stock, which are costly.

The Atlantic cod case shows what happens when open access is not controlled.

## Worked example

A bay has a sustainable catch of 1200 tonnes a year and is open to any boat. Each boat costs AED 15000 a year, and fish sells for AED 600 per tonne.

<Steps>
<Step>Open access: 60 boats catch 1500 tonnes, which is above the sustainable 1200 tonnes. Total revenue is $600 \times 1500 = 900000$ AED and total cost is $60 \times 15000 = 900000$ AED. Economic profit is zero, so no more boats enter, and the stock keeps falling.</Step>
<Step>A sustainable alternative: 40 boats catch 1200 tonnes, the sustainable level. Total revenue is $600 \times 1200 = 720000$ AED and total cost is $40 \times 15000 = 600000$ AED, so economic profit is AED 120000.</Step>
<Step>A quota of 1200 tonnes shared equally among 40 boats gives each boat $1200 / 40 = 30$ tonnes a year.</Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

- Calling a common pool resource a public good. It is rivalrous: one user's catch reduces what is left for others.
- Saying open access maximizes profit. It ends where economic profit is zero; a smaller effort can give higher profit, but social optimality needs social costs and benefits.
- Ignoring non-excludability. Quotas and permits work because they set rules on who may use the resource.
- Saying a quota ends overfishing without evaluation. A quota set too high on poor data still depletes the stock.

## Summary

- A common pool resource is rivalrous and non-excludable.
- Rivalry means one user's catch reduces what is left for others; non-excludability means no owner can easily keep others out.
- Under open access, entry continues until total revenue equals total cost, so economic profit is zero.
- In the simplified effort model, MR = MC maximises net revenue; social optimality requires all social costs and benefits to be included.
- The tragedy of the commons is the result of rational individual choices under open access.
- Quotas, property rights, tradable permits and taxes limit use, but each needs monitoring, enforcement and reliable data.
