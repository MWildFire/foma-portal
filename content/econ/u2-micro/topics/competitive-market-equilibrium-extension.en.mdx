## Big idea

In a competitive market the price adjusts until quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. At that equilibrium the market clears, consumer and producer surplus measure the gains from trade, and a perfectly competitive firm is a price taker that can earn supernormal profit only in the short run.

## Explanation

<Term id="market-equilibrium"/> is the price and quantity at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so there is no pressure for the price to change. With demand $Q_d = a - bP$ and supply $Q_s = c + dP$, setting the two equal gives the equilibrium price:

$$
P^{*} = \frac{a - c}{b + d}
$$

<Formula id="linear-equilibrium"/>

Substitute the equilibrium price into either equation to find the equilibrium quantity.

If the price is below equilibrium, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied. This <Term id="excess-demand"/> pushes buyers to compete and sellers to raise the price. If the price is above equilibrium, there is <Term id="excess-supply"/>: sellers cannot sell all they produce, so they cut the price. This adjustment is the <Term id="price-mechanism"/>, which coordinates buyers and sellers without central direction.

Shifts move the equilibrium. An increase in demand (a shift right) raises both the equilibrium price and quantity; a decrease lowers both. An increase in supply lowers the price and raises the quantity; a decrease raises the price and lowers the quantity. When both curves move at once, one of the two effects is uncertain: if demand and supply both increase, quantity rises, but the price effect depends on the size of each shift.

Welfare is measured by surplus. The <Term id="consumer-surplus"/> is the gap between the maximum price buyers would pay and the price they actually pay, summed over the units bought: the triangle above the price and below demand. The <Term id="producer-surplus"/> is the gap between the price received and the minimum price producers would accept: the triangle below the price and above supply. The area of a triangle is:

$$
\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}
$$

<Formula id="triangle-area"/>

<Term id="perfect-competition"/> has four main conditions: many buyers and sellers, so that no single firm affects the price; identical products; perfect information; and free entry and exit. Each firm is a <Term id="price-taker"/>. Its demand curve is horizontal at the market price, so price equals marginal revenue and average revenue (P = MR = AR).

In the short run a firm produces where marginal cost equals marginal revenue (MC = MR = P). If price is above average total cost, the firm earns <Term id="supernormal-profit"/>, because total revenue exceeds total cost. If price is below average total cost, it makes a loss. In the long run, supernormal profit attracts new firms, which shifts market supply right and pushes the price down; losses make firms leave, which shifts supply left. Long-run equilibrium is reached where price equals minimum average total cost, so the firm earns only <Term id="normal-profit"/>. At this point P = MC, so the market is allocatively efficient, and output is at minimum average total cost, so it is productively efficient.

## Worked example

Take demand $Q_d = 40 - 2P$ and supply $Q_s = 4 + 3P$, with Q in thousand units and P in AED per unit.

<Steps>
<Step>Set quantity demanded equal to quantity supplied: $40 - 2P = 4 + 3P$.</Step>
<Step>Rearrange: $36 = 5P$, so $P^{*} = 7.20$ AED.</Step>
<Step>Quantity: $Q^{*} = 40 - 2 \times 7.20 = 25.6$ thousand units. Check with supply: $4 + 3 \times 7.20 = 25.6$.</Step>
</Steps>

At AED 6, which is below equilibrium, quantity demanded is 28 and quantity supplied is 22. At AED 9, which is above equilibrium, quantity demanded is 22 and quantity supplied is 31.

<Steps>
<Step>At AED 6: $Q_d = 40 - 12 = 28$ and $Q_s = 4 + 18 = 22$, so excess demand = 28 - 22 = 6 thousand units.</Step>
<Step>At AED 9: $Q_d = 40 - 18 = 22$ and $Q_s = 4 + 27 = 31$, so excess supply = 31 - 22 = 9 thousand units.</Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

- Solving the equations but forgetting to substitute the equilibrium price back to find the quantity.
- Swapping excess demand and excess supply: excess demand (a shortage) happens below equilibrium, excess supply above it.
- Treating a high price as supernormal profit. Profit is supernormal only when price is above average total cost.
- Forgetting that in the long run a perfectly competitive firm earns only normal profit.

## Summary

- Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied; with linear equations, set them equal, solve for P, then find Q.
- Below equilibrium there is excess demand (a shortage); above it there is excess supply; the price mechanism moves the market back.
- Shifts of demand or supply move the equilibrium; when both move, one of the price or quantity effects is uncertain.
- Consumer surplus lies above the price and below demand; producer surplus lies below the price and above supply.
- A perfectly competitive firm is a price taker, with P = MR = AR; in the short run it can earn supernormal profit or make a loss.
- In the long run, entry and exit drive profit down to normal profit, where P equals minimum average total cost.
