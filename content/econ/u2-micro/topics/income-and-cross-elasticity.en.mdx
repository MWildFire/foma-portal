## Big idea

For this mock, study income elasticity of demand (YED). It measures how strongly purchases change when income changes. The sign identifies normal or inferior goods; the size distinguishes income-elastic and income-inelastic normal goods. XED is retained in the extension below.

## Explanation

<Term id="income-elasticity-of-demand"/>

<Formula id="yed"/>

Use percentage changes relative to the starting values. Positive YED means a <Term id="normal-good"/>; negative YED means an <Term id="inferior-good"/>. For normal goods, YED between zero and one means demand grows less than proportionately with income; above one means it grows more than proportionately. A <Term id="necessity-good"/> usually has the former pattern and a <Term id="luxury-good"/> the latter. These classifications depend on the consumer and income range.

HL: as income rises, low-YED primary products tend to take a smaller share of spending, while higher-YED manufactured goods and services can take larger shares. This helps explain changes in the sectoral structure of an economy; technology and productivity also matter. Source: Tragakes ch.3, pp.99–103.

## Worked example

Income rises from AED 3000 to 3600 (+20%); salmon purchases rise from 10 to 13 kg (+30%). YED = 30/20 = 1.5: normal and income elastic. If income instead falls 10% while noodle purchases rise 15%, YED = 15/(−10) = −1.5: inferior.

## Visual

The graph uses price and quantity axes. An income rise shifts demand right for normal goods and left for inferior goods; it is not a movement along demand. Tragakes Fig.3.9, p.99.

## How it is examined

For P2 data response and P3, calculate YED with its sign, classify the good and explain the effect of income changes on demand. Use the data to discuss implications for producers or the changing sectoral structure. Where relevant, apply that reasoning to the 15-mark P2(g) discussion or a P3 recommendation. The school P1 covers Unit 3 only. XED is excluded from this mock.

## Common mistakes

- Do not use a price change in the denominator of YED.
- Inferior describes the income response, not physical quality.
- Positive does not automatically mean income elastic.

## Summary

- YED compares percentage changes in quantity demanded and income.
- Retain the sign.
- Negative: inferior; positive: normal.
- Normal goods may be income inelastic or elastic.
- HL: link income growth to changing sector shares.
