## Big idea

The mock examines rational consumer choice and its behavioural limits (HL). Business objectives are outside the checklist.

Rational choice means comparing affordable options consistently according to preferences. <Term id="utility-maximization"/> is the standard consumer objective. <Term id="bounded-rationality"/> recognises limits on information, time and processing. Imperfect information prevents complete comparisons. Bounded self-control means immediate temptations can defeat a longer-term intention; bounded selfishness recognises concern for other people.

Rules of thumb simplify a decision but can produce systematic errors. <Term id="anchoring-bias"/> gives excessive influence to an initial reference point. Framing changes choices through presentation of equivalent options. Availability gives excessive weight to recent or readily recalled information.

Choice architecture designs the environment in which choices are made. A <Term id="nudge"/> changes that setting while leaving alternatives available: defaults, information and the position of options can affect behaviour. Evaluate whether it helps the target group, preserves meaningful choice and avoids manipulation. Source: Tragakes ch.2.6, pp.72–78.

## Worked example

A worker intends to save but repeatedly postpones enrolment. Automatic pension enrolment with an opt-out changes the default. It can overcome inertia and limited self-control without banning spending. However, the default contribution may be unsuitable for a worker with urgent debts.

## How it is examined

This HL topic can support P2 data response and P3, while the school P1 is macroeconomics only. Identify the bias or limit to rational choice shown in the extract and explain how it affects the decision. Explain the design and mechanism of a nudge. In a 15-mark P2(g) discussion or P3 recommendation, assess effectiveness, freedom of choice, information and possible unintended effects using the context. Business objectives are outside this mock.

## Common mistakes

- A nudge preserves options; a ban does not.
- Bounded rationality is a limit on decision-making, not stupidity.
- Name the mechanism, not just the bias.

## Summary

- Rational choice uses consistent comparisons.
- Information, processing and self-control are limited.
- Self-interest is not the only motive.
- Rules of thumb, anchoring, framing and availability can bias decisions.
- Choice architecture and nudges change the setting of choice.
