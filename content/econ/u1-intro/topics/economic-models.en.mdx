## Big idea

The school mock uses circular flow to connect households, firms and the wider economy. Unit 1 has low priority; the general methodology of economics is retained below for later study.

## Explanation

In the two-sector model, households supply factors to firms through resource markets. Firms pay wages, rent, interest and profit. Firms supply goods and services through product markets; household spending returns to firms as revenue. Real flows and money flows travel in opposite directions.

The five-sector model adds financial institutions, government and the foreign sector. <Term id="withdrawals"/> are saving, taxes and imports. <Term id="injections"/> are investment, government spending and exports. Injections equal to withdrawals keep the level of income stable; higher injections tend to expand the flow. Tragakes ch.1, pp.20–21; ch.8, pp.248–249.

## Worked example

Saving is 20, taxes 15 and imports 10 billion; investment is 25, government spending 12 and exports 8 billion. Withdrawals = 20 + 15 + 10 = 45; injections = 25 + 12 + 8 = 45. Income is in equilibrium in this simplified model.

## Common mistakes

- Saving is a withdrawal; investment is an injection.
- Imports pay foreign producers; exports bring foreign spending to domestic firms.
- Money and real flows are not the same arrow.

## Summary

- Households supply factors; firms supply output.
- Factor incomes return to households.
- Spending is firms’ revenue.
- Saving, taxes and imports withdraw income.
- Investment, government spending and exports inject it.
