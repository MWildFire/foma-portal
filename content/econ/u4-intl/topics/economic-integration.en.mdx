## Big idea

Economic integration means countries reduce barriers between themselves, moving from a free trade area to a customs union, a common market and an economic union. Integration can create trade where members are efficient suppliers, but it can also divert trade away from cheaper non-members, so the welfare effect depends on which effect is larger.

## Explanation

<Term id="economic-integration"/> is an agreement between countries that reduces or removes barriers to trade, and sometimes to the movement of factors, between the members. Members give each other preferential treatment, so these are also called preferential trading arrangements. The four main forms go from the lightest to the deepest.

A <Term id="free-trade-area"/> removes tariffs and quotas on trade among its members, but each member keeps its own tariffs on imports from non-members. Because those tariffs can differ, a free trade area needs <Term id="rules-of-origin"/> to decide which goods qualify for duty-free entry.

A <Term id="customs-union"/> is a free trade area with a <Term id="common-external-tariff"/>: every member charges the same tariff on imports from outside. One tariff at the border removes the need for rules of origin on those goods. The GCC customs union in the case below shows this in practice.

A <Term id="common-market"/> is a customs union in which factors of production, such as labour and capital, can also move freely between members. An <Term id="economic-union"/> goes further: members harmonise economic policies, such as tax and competition policy, and may share one currency. Each step gives up more national control over policy.

Integration affects trade in two ways. <Term id="trade-creation"/> happens when cheaper imports from a member replace higher-cost domestic production, or when lower prices raise consumption; it raises welfare. <Term id="trade-diversion"/> happens when imports switch from a lower-cost non-member to a higher-cost member, because the member is now cheaper; it can reduce welfare.

Supporters argue that a larger market allows <Term id="economies-of-scale"/>, increases competition and attracts investment. Critics point to trade diversion, the loss of tariff revenue and policy independence, uneven gains between large and small members, and the risk that regional deals weaken global trade liberalisation. The worked example shows how the balance between creation and diversion decides the result.

## Worked example

Country A is forming a customs union with partner B. Its domestic demand is 200 - 10P units and its domestic supply is 10P - 100 units, where P is the price in AED. Non-member C supplies the good at AED 8 per unit and partner B at AED 11 per unit. Before the union, A applies a tariff of AED 6 per unit to all imports. After the union, the tariff on B is removed, and the common external tariff of AED 6 still applies to C.

<Steps>
<Step>Before the union, the price of imports from C is AED 8 + 6 = AED 14 and from B is AED 11 + 6 = AED 17, so C sets the price at AED 14. Demand = 200 - 140 = 60 units; domestic supply = 140 - 100 = 40 units; imports from C = 20 units.</Step>
<Step>After the union, B supplies at AED 11 with no tariff and C costs AED 14, so B sets the price at AED 11. Demand = 200 - 110 = 90 units; domestic supply = 110 - 100 = 10 units; imports from B = 80 units.</Step>
<Step>Trade diversion: 20 units switch from C (AED 8 per unit) to B (AED 11 per unit), so each of those units costs AED 3 more.</Step>
<Step>Trade creation: 30 units of domestic output are replaced by imports from B (output falls from 40 to 10), and 30 extra units are consumed (from 60 to 90). That is 60 units of new trade.</Step>
</Steps>

The gains from the trade created (cheaper supply and lower prices for buyers) outweigh the extra cost of the trade diverted (AED 3 on each of 20 units), so welfare rises in this example. If diversion were the larger effect, welfare would fall.

## Common mistakes

- Giving a customs union a common currency or free movement of labour. Those features belong to a common market or an economic union.
- Saying trade diversion is always bad or trade creation always good. The welfare effect depends on the size of each.
- Forgetting the common external tariff. A free trade area does not have one, so its members can keep different tariffs on outsiders.
- Confusing trade diversion with lost trade. Diverted trade still happens, but from a less efficient source.

## Summary

- A free trade area removes tariffs and quotas among members; each member keeps its own external tariffs and needs rules of origin.
- A customs union adds a common external tariff on imports from non-members.
- A common market adds free movement of factors of production; an economic union adds harmonised economic policy.
- Trade creation raises welfare when cheaper member supply replaces higher-cost supply or raises consumption.
- Trade diversion moves imports from a lower-cost non-member to a higher-cost member and can reduce welfare.
- The net welfare effect depends on the size of creation compared with diversion.
- Integration gains come from larger markets, economies of scale and competition; the costs include lost policy control and uneven gains.
