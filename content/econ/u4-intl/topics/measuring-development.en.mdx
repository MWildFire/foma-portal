## Big idea

Development is wider than growth: it means better health, education and living standards, not only more output. Economists therefore combine several indicators, because no single average shows how people actually live.

## Explanation

<Term id="economic-development"/> means a broader improvement in living standards than economic growth alone. A country can grow fast while most people stay poor, so development is measured with indicators that describe people as well as output.

A widely used income measure is <Term id="gross-national-income"/> (GNI), which is GDP plus net primary income from abroad. Dividing GNI by the population gives <Term id="gni-per-capita"/>, the average income per person:

<Formula id="gni-per-capita"/>

<Callout kind="define">GNI per capita: gross national income divided by the population, the average income per person. For comparisons between countries it is converted at PPP exchange rates.</Callout>

Market exchange rates do not show what money buys at home. If a basket of goods costs more in one country, the same income buys less there. <Term id="purchasing-power-parity"/> (PPP) is the exchange rate at which the same basket costs the same in two countries, so incomes converted at PPP compare living standards more closely. Such figures are often quoted in <Term id="international-dollar"/> units.

GNI per capita has clear limits. It is an average, so it hides how income is shared. Most unpaid household services are excluded from the national-accounts production boundary. Subsistence crops and informal production within that boundary should be included, but incomplete coverage can understate them. It says nothing about health, education, the environment or freedom, and figures for poorer countries are often estimates.

The <Term id="human-development-index"/> (HDI) covers more. It is a composite index from 0 to 1 that gives equal weight to three dimensions: health, measured by <Term id="life-expectancy-at-birth"/>; education, measured by <Term id="mean-years-of-schooling"/> and the expected years of schooling of children; and living standards, measured by GNI per capita at PPP. Scores of 0.800 and above are very high, 0.700 to 0.799 high, 0.550 to 0.699 medium, and below 0.550 low.

The HDI has its own limits. Equal weights are a value judgement, and the basic index does not show inequality within a country, political freedom or environmental sustainability. Years of schooling say nothing about what pupils learn. Any average can hide distribution, so economists pair income with the <Term id="gini-coefficient"/>, which runs from 0 (everyone has the same income) to 1 (one person has all the income). Two countries can have the same GNI per capita and very different Gini values, so the choice of indicator should fit the question.

## Worked example

Country A has a GNI of USD 45000 million and a population of 3.1 million. Country B has a GNI per capita of USD 2350. Both figures are illustrative and converted at PPP.

<Steps>
<Step>GNI per capita of country A = GNI / population = 45000 / 3.1 = 14516 (rounded). The million cancels, so the answer is USD 14516 per person.</Step>
<Step>Ratio of the two averages: 14516 / 2350 = 6.18, so the average income in country A is about 6.2 times that in country B.</Step>
<Step>Evaluation: the ratio says nothing about inequality inside each country. Check the Gini coefficient before saying that most people in A are richer than most people in B.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why convert at PPP?">If a basket costs AED 90 in country X and USD 25 in the USA, the PPP exchange rate is 3.6 AED per USD. Converting income at a market rate that differs from this rate would misstate what people can buy.</Reveal>

## Common mistakes

- Treating GNI per capita as the income of the typical citizen; an average hides inequality.
- Comparing living standards at market exchange rates instead of PPP figures.
- Calling the HDI a measure of happiness or freedom; it covers health, education and income only.
- Confusing GNI with GDP: GNI adds net primary income from abroad, so it can be above or below GDP.

## Summary

- GNI per capita is GNI divided by population: the average income per person.
- PPP converts incomes at a rate that equalises the cost of the same basket, so living standards can be compared.
- The HDI combines life expectancy, years of schooling and GNI per capita at PPP into an index from 0 to 1.
- The Gini coefficient shows how unequal incomes are; an average hides that distribution.
- GNI and the HDI miss the environment, unpaid work and freedom, so choose indicators to fit the question.
