## Big idea

An injection of spending raises national income by more than its original amount, because the money spent becomes income for someone else, who spends part of it again. The multiplier shows how much income rises for each unit of injection. The larger the leakages of saving, taxes and imports at each round, the smaller the multiplier.

## Explanation

When income rises, households spend part of the extra income. The rest is saved, paid in tax or spent on imports. The <Term id="marginal-propensity"/> is the share of each extra unit of income that goes to a particular use. The <Term id="marginal-propensity-to-consume"/> (MPC) is the share spent on consumption, and the <Term id="marginal-propensity-to-save"/> (MPS) is the share saved. In a closed economy with no taxes, all extra income is either consumed or saved, so MPS = 1 - MPC:

<Formula id="mps"/>

Two more leakages take money out of each round of spending. The <Term id="marginal-propensity-to-tax"/> (MPT) is the share of extra income paid in tax, and the <Term id="marginal-propensity-to-import"/> (MPM) is the share spent on imports. Saving, taxes and imports are all <Term id="withdrawals"/>, so the spending that stays in the country is what is left after them.

The <Term id="multiplier"/> is the number that converts an injection into a change in national income:

<Formula id="multiplier"/>

With no tax and no imports, this reduces to the closed-economy form:

<Formula id="multiplier-mpc"/>

So with an MPC of 0.8 the multiplier is 5. The change in national income equals the multiplier times the change in <Term id="injections"/> (investment, government spending and exports).

The <Term id="multiplier-effect"/> works in rounds. Suppose the injection is AED 100 bn and the MPC is 0.8. The first round adds AED 100 bn of income, the second round AED 80 bn, the third AED 64 bn, and so on. Each round is smaller because part of it leaks out, and the total converges to AED 500 bn, which is 5 times the injection. Each extra leakage makes the rounds smaller and the total smaller.

The multiplier has limits. It is smaller when there is no <Term id="spare-capacity"/>, because extra spending then raises prices rather than output. It is also smaller when imports are high, when households and firms become cautious and save more in a recession, and when spending takes time to work through the economy. These limits matter for fiscal policy, because a government cannot assume that each AED of extra spending adds the full multiple to income.

## Worked example

The government builds a road that costs AED 100 bn. The economy is closed, there are no taxes and the MPC is 0.8. Find the change in national income.

<Steps>
<Step>MPS = 1 - MPC = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2.</Step>
<Step>k = 1 / MPS = 1 / 0.2 = 5.</Step>
<Step>Change in national income = k x change in government spending = 5 x AED 100 bn = AED 500 bn.</Step>
</Steps>

An open economy has MPS 0.1, MPT 0.1 and MPM 0.2. Exports rise by AED 20 bn. Find the change in national income.

<Steps>
<Step>Sum of the leakages = MPS + MPT + MPM = 0.1 + 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.4.</Step>
<Step>k = 1 / 0.4 = 2.5.</Step>
<Step>Change in national income = 2.5 x AED 20 bn = AED 50 bn.</Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

- Using the MPC in the formula k = 1 / (MPS + MPT + MPM) for an economy with taxes and imports, instead of the three leakages.
- Forgetting MPT or MPM in an open economy with taxes and imports.
- Treating the multiplier as the sum of the leakages; k = 1 / (MPS + MPT + MPM), so more leakages mean a smaller k.
- Saying that a multiplier of 5 means income rises by 5 per cent; it means income rises by 5 times the injection.

## Summary

- The MPC is the share of extra income spent on consumption; in a closed economy with no taxes, MPS = 1 - MPC.
- The leakages are MPS, MPT and MPM; the multiplier is k = 1 / (MPS + MPT + MPM).
- With no taxes or imports, k = 1 / (1 - MPC), so an MPC of 0.8 gives a multiplier of 5.
- The change in national income = k x change in injections (investment, government spending, exports).
- Bigger leakages mean smaller rounds of spending and a smaller multiplier.
- The multiplier is limited by spare capacity, imports, confidence and time lags.
