## Big idea

French has three ways to look forward. The futur proche (near future) is for plans and predictions. The futur simple (future tense) is for later events. The conditionnel présent (conditional tense) is for politeness, advice and hypotheses. Si-clauses link a condition to its result. After « si », French never uses the future or the conditional.

## Explanation

The futur proche is the present of aller plus an infinitive: Je vais partir demain. It is used for plans and for predictions based on what you can see now: Regarde le ciel : il va pleuvoir.

The futur simple takes a future stem and the endings -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. For -er and -ir verbs the stem is the whole infinitive; for -re verbs drop the final e. Some very common verbs have irregular stems, but the endings stay the same:

| Infinitive | Future stem | je | nous |
|---|---|---|---|
| être | ser- | serai | serons |
| avoir | aur- | aurai | aurons |
| aller | ir- | irai | irons |
| faire | fer- | ferai | ferons |
| pouvoir | pourr- | pourrai | pourrons |
| vouloir | voudr- | voudrai | voudrons |

After quand with a future meaning, French uses the future where English uses the present: Quand tu arriveras, on mangera (When you arrive, we will eat).

The conditionnel présent takes the future stem and the imparfait endings -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient: parlerais, serais, aurais, irais. It expresses politeness (Je voudrais un renseignement, s'il vous plaît.), advice (Tu devrais réviser ce soir.) and hypotheses (Nous voyagerions en Asie.).

Si-clauses come in two forms:

- Real future: si with the present; the main clause takes the future, the present or an imperative: Si tu as le temps, on pourra se voir demain.
- Unreal present: si with the imparfait; the main clause takes the conditionnel présent: Si Léa avait plus d'argent, elle voyagerait en Asie.

Never use the future or the conditional after si. Write s'il, never si il: s'il pleut.

## Worked example

Read this short text. The future and conditional forms are in bold.

« Demain, **je vais passer** l'examen de français. **Si je me repose** ce soir, **je réussirai**. **Si j'avais** plus de temps, **je réviserais** encore. **Pourriez-vous** me donner un conseil ? »

- **je vais passer** is the futur proche (aller and an infinitive), for a near plan.
- **Si je me repose** is si with the present, a real future; me repose is reflexive.
- **je réussirai** is the futur simple of réussir.
- **Si j'avais** is si with the imparfait, an unreal present.
- **je réviserais** is the conditionnel présent: what the speaker would do.
- **Pourriez-vous** is the conditional of pouvoir, a polite request.

## Visual

| Pronoun | futur simple: parler | futur simple: être | futur simple: aller | conditionnel présent: parler |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| je | parlerai | serai | irai | parlerais |
| tu | parleras | seras | iras | parlerais |
| il / elle | parlera | sera | ira | parlerait |
| nous | parlerons | serons | irons | parlerions |
| vous | parlerez | serez | irez | parleriez |
| ils / elles | parleront | seront | iront | parleraient |

The endings are the same for all verbs in a tense; only the stem changes.

## How it is examined

On Paper 1, future and conditional forms earn Criterion A marks, and a polite conditional in a formal letter supports Criterion B. On Paper 2, texts about plans, predictions and hypotheses use these tenses. Si with the imparfait describes an imagined situation, not a real plan, and questions often test that difference.

## Common mistakes

- A conditional or future after si: « Si j'aurais le temps » is wrong; « Si j'avais le temps » is right.
- An English-style present after quand: « Quand tu arrives » is wrong; « Quand tu arriveras » is right.
- A wrong future stem: « je faierai » is wrong; « je ferai » is right.
- Keeping the -e of a -re verb: « je vendrerai » is wrong; « je vendrai » is right.

## Summary

- Futur proche: present of aller plus an infinitive, for plans and near predictions.
- Futur simple: future stem plus -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont; learn the irregular stems.
- Conditionnel présent: future stem plus -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient; for politeness, advice and hypotheses.
- Si with the present for a real future; si with the imparfait and the conditional for an unreal present. Never the future or the conditional after si.
- The futur proche is common in speech; the futur simple is common in formal writing.
- Write s'il, never si il: s'il pleut.
