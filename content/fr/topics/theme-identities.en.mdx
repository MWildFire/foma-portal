## Big idea

Identities is the first of the five IB French B themes. It asks who you are and how you present yourself: personality, family and origins, values, languages, style, hobbies, social roles and life online. In Paper 1 you write about yourself, a friend or a character, so you need words for describing people. In Paper 2 the texts often show people who discover, change or defend their identity, so you must understand this vocabulary in context.

## Explanation

**The present for who you are.** Describe traits with *être* and *avoir*: *Il est curieux. Elle a du caractère.* With *depuis*, the present shows something that started earlier and continues: *Je joue du piano depuis six ans* means "I have been playing the piano for six years".

**Reflexive verbs.** Identity verbs such as *se définir* (to define oneself) and *s'intéresser à* (to be interested in) need a pronoun that matches the subject: *Je me reconnais dans ce personnage* (I identify with this character).

**Relative clauses.** Use *qui*, *que*, *dont* and *où* to define people, things and places: *une culture dont je suis fier*, *un pays où l'on se sent chez soi*.

**The imparfait for a past identity.** Use the imparfait for how you used to be, and the passé composé for single events: *Quand j'étais petit, j'étais timide. Un jour, j'ai changé de ville.*

## Worked example

A student wrote this paragraph about her identity.

Je m'appelle Inès, j'ai dix-sept ans et je suis née à Lyon, dans une famille franco-russe. Ma mère parle russe à la maison, alors que mon père est né à Lyon et ne parle que français. Pendant longtemps, je me suis sentie un peu perdue entre les deux cultures. Aujourd'hui, je me considère comme bilingue et je suis fière de mes origines. Je m'intéresse à la musique et je joue du piano depuis l'âge de six ans. Mes amis disent que je suis curieuse et sincère, et c'est vrai : j'aime comprendre les gens avant de les juger.

**Analysis**

- Vocabulary: *une famille franco-russe*, *mes origines*, *bilingue*, *sincère*, *s'intéresser à*.
- *Depuis* with the present: *je joue du piano depuis l'âge de six ans* describes an activity that continues now.
- Reflexive verbs and agreement: in *je me suis sentie*, the passé composé takes *être*, so *sentie* agrees with the feminine subject. *Perdue*, *fière* and *curieuse* also end in -e.
- Connectors: *alors que* contrasts the mother and the father; *avant de* with an infinitive links two actions.

## How it is examined

Identities can appear in any Paper 1 task or Paper 2 text, so do not wait for a task called "family". Paper 1 tasks might be a blog post about your hobbies, a diary entry about a new school or an e-mail about your family. Your answer is 250 to 400 words, and three criteria apply:

- Criterion A, Language (12 marks): tenses, reflexive verbs, agreements and vocabulary.
- Criterion B, Message (12 marks): a clear purpose, all content points and a suitable tone.
- Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks): you show that you understand identity, for example how you have changed or how culture shapes a person.

In Paper 2 you find words in context, explain phrases, or decide whether a statement is true, false or not mentioned. Learn fixed expressions as whole units.

## Common mistakes

- *actuellement* means "currently", not "actually" (use *en fait*).
- *une identité* is an identity; *un identifiant* is a login name. An ID card is *une carte d'identité*.
- Forgetting the reflexive pronoun: *il s'intéresse*, not *il intéresse*.
- *une personne* is always feminine in grammar, even for a man: *c'est une personne timide*.
- With *être*, the participle agrees with the subject: *elle s'est sentie*, not *elle s'est senti*.
- *sensible* means "sensitive", not "reasonable" (use *raisonnable*).
- *assister à* means "to attend", not "to assist".

## Summary

- Identity vocabulary covers personality, family, values, languages, style, hobbies, social roles, digital life and multicultural life.
- Use the present for who you are now, and *depuis* with the present for what continues.
- Reflexive verbs need the matching pronoun.
- Use *qui*, *que*, *dont* and *où* to define people, things and places.
- Use the imparfait for a past identity, and the passé composé for single events.
- Learn fixed expressions such as *avoir du caractère* as whole units.
