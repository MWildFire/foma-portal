## Big idea

Paper 1 tests whether you can write a real French text for a real purpose and reader. Marks go to a text that works: correct language, the right message and developed ideas.

## Explanation

Paper 1 lasts 75 minutes, or 94 minutes with [redacted]'s 25% extra time. You get three writing tasks, each linked to a different theme of the course, and you choose one. In the practice set, the task names its one text type (for example a blog post, a formal letter or a speech) and asks for 250 to 400 words. In the exam, you choose the text type from those the task offers.

Each answer is marked against three criteria, and each criterion is assessed separately:

- **Criterion A, Language (12 marks):** the accuracy, range and complexity of your grammar and vocabulary. Correct tenses, agreements and accents matter, as do varied structures such as the subjunctive.
- **Criterion B, Message (12 marks):** whether the text does its job. Consider the purpose (to inform, persuade or invite), the reader and the conventions of the text type: a letter has a greeting and a sign-off.
- **Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks):** whether the theme is developed through clear ideas, examples and detail, not just named.

These are our summary of the criteria; check the exact wording in the official IB guide.

Plan before you write: how you will cover what the task asks for, whether you use tu or vous, and the layout. Use tu for friends, classmates and personal blogs; use vous for a formal letter or an unknown reader.

## Worked example

Sample task: you write a blog post for your classmates about a trip that changed your view of a place. Say what you saw, what surprised you and what you recommend.

The plan: a blog post needs a title and a personal tone; a classmate reader means tu; the layout is a title, an opening, one paragraph per point the task asks for and a closing recommendation.

Opening sentence:

« Salut à tous ! Le mois dernier, j'ai passé une semaine à Marrakech, et cette ville m'a beaucoup plu. »

It works: the greeting suits a blog, tu matches the reader, and the second sentence answers the task at once. Plaire takes an indirect object, so plu does not agree with m' and is correct whatever the writer's gender.

## How it is examined

In the practice set, Paper 1 is marked out of 30. The examiner reads the whole text against all three criteria; each criterion has its own mark and the marks are added, so a weak message costs marks even when the language is strong. The 250 to 400 words are part of the task.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing tu and vous in one text, for example « Tu pouvez venir » instead of « Tu peux venir » or « Vous pouvez venir ».
- Writing too few words, so that what the task asks for is only touched on.
- Choosing a text type that does not match the task, such as a formal letter written as a blog post.
- Reusing a memorised template without answering the task. The same paragraphs for every topic earn few marks for Criterion C.
- Writing without a plan, so that the text repeats itself.
- Skipping the check: missing accents, as in « ecole » instead of « école », and past participles that do not agree.
