## Big idea

A proposal asks someone to approve a plan. It must make the plan attractive, realistic and easy to decide on. The writer states the aims, explains how the project works step by step, lists the benefits and gives a rough budget, then asks clearly for approval.

## Explanation

A proposal is organised like a short project plan:

- **Title.** It names the project, for example « Proposition : un café des réparations intergénérationnel ».
- **Objectives** (les objectifs). What the project wants to achieve, in one or two sentences.
- **Description.** What the project is and who takes part: residents, volunteers, young people.
- **Implementation.** The steps in order. Use the futur simple for the steps (« Nous obtiendrons un local. ») or the infinitive for a list of actions (« Obtenir un local, recruter des bénévoles… »).
- **Benefits.** Why residents gain from the project, with concrete examples.
- **Rough costs.** A realistic estimate, such as « environ 1 500 euros », with what it covers.
- **Conclusion.** A clear request for approval, with a polite closing.

The register is formal. Use vous when you address the council (« Nous vous demandons d'approuver… ») and nous when you speak for the group. For suggestions, use the conditional (le conditionnel): « Nous pourrions organiser une séance par mois. » After il faut que and il est important que, use the subjunctive (le subjonctif): « Il faut que le conseil accepte le projet avant l'hiver. »

## Worked example

Task: an intergenerational project for the district council.

Objective:
« Ce projet vise à réduire le gaspillage et à créer des liens entre les générations. »

Step, with the futur simple:
« Nous recruterons une dizaine de bénévoles. »

Benefit, with an example:
« Les habitants économisent de l'argent, car un appareil réparé dure plus longtemps. »

Request for approval, with the subjunctive:
« Il est important que le conseil accepte cette proposition avant l'hiver. »

Each sentence has a clear function: the aim, the plan, the benefit and the request. The council can decide quickly.

## How it is examined

Paper 1 is worth 30 marks: 12 for Language (Criterion A), 12 for Message (Criterion B) and 6 for Conceptual understanding (Criterion C). For a proposal, Criterion B checks the three content points: the aims, how the project works step by step, and the benefits for residents with a reason why the council should accept. Criterion A checks the futur simple, the conditional for suggestions and the subjunctive after il faut que. Criterion C looks for an understanding of how a project can improve social life in a district.

Keep to 250 to 400 words. Plan for about 5 minutes, write for about 25 minutes and check for about 10 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- **A vague plan.** « On va faire quelque chose pour le quartier » does not persuade. Write concrete steps: « Nous recruterons une dizaine de bénévoles. »
- **Wrong tense for future steps.** Use the futur simple or the infinitive for steps still to come, not the passé composé.
- **No budget.** A proposal without a rough estimate is hard to approve. Add a figure and say what it covers.
- **Indicative after il faut que.** « Il faut que vous signez le formulaire » is wrong. The subjunctive is « Il faut que vous signiez le formulaire. »
- **No request.** End with a clear request, such as « Nous vous demandons d'approuver ce projet. »

## Summary

- A proposal has a title, objectives, a description, the steps, the benefits, a rough budget and a conclusion that asks for approval.
- Describe the steps still to come with the futur simple or the infinitive.
- Make suggestions with the conditional, such as « Nous pourrions… ».
- After il faut que and il est important que, use the subjunctive.
- Use vous for the council and nous for your group, and keep the register formal.
