## Big idea

Instructions tell a reader how to do something safely and in the right order. Good instructions are short, use one verb form for the steps and warn the reader before a danger, not after it.

## Explanation

A user guide has a clear layout:

- **Title:** a line that begins with *Comment…*, for example *Comment utiliser…*
- **Introduction:** what the device is for, what the reader needs and what they must know before starting.
- **Materials:** a short list of the parts or items needed.
- **Numbered steps:** one action per step, in the imperative: *Placez le socle.*, *Versez les ingrédients.*
- **Warnings:** *Attention !* for a danger, *Important :* for a key point.
- **General rules:** in the infinitive: *Ne pas plonger le socle dans l'eau.*
- **Troubleshooting:** what to do if something goes wrong, with a clear next step.

Choose *vous* or *tu* at the start and keep it. *Vous* is the usual choice for a formal guide.

## Worked example

Here are two versions of the same step.

*Vous devez ouvrir le couvercle pour pouvoir ajouter les ingrédients.*

*Ouvrez le couvercle. Ajoutez les ingrédients.*

The first version is correct, but it is wordy: *vous devez* adds nothing the reader needs. The second version gives two short actions, each starting with the imperative. A warning comes before the action it protects: *Attention ! Les lames sont très tranchantes.* In a real guide, the warning goes above the step, not below it.

## Visual

| Part | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Step | imperative, vous | *Placez le socle sur une surface plane.* |
| General rule | infinitive | *Ne pas faire fonctionner le robot à vide.* |
| Danger | Attention ! | *Attention ! Les lames sont tranchantes.* |
| Key point | Important : | *Important : fermez le couvercle avant le démarrage.* |
| Problem | si + present, then imperative | *Si l'appareil s'arrête, débranchez-le.* |

## How it is examined

The P1 writing task asks for 250 to 400 words in one of the offered text types, and instructions are one of them. Criterion A Language (12) looks at the imperative in the steps, the infinitive in the general rules, the subjunctive after *il faut que* and accurate agreements. Criterion B Message (12) looks at the three content points in order: the purpose of the device, the steps of use, and the safety notes with what to do if something goes wrong. Criterion C Conceptual understanding (6) rewards an invented device that is clearly described and that takes the user's needs and risks into account.

## Common mistakes

- Writing *vous devez* before every step. It is correct, but the imperative is more direct.
- Mixing *tu* and *vous*: *Ouvrez le couvercle*, then *Mets les ingrédients*.
- Putting a warning after the step it should prevent.
- Using the past tense for the steps: *Il ouvrait le couvercle* tells a story; it does not give an instruction.
- Writing *il faut que* with the indicative: *il faut que le couvercle est fermé* should be *il faut que le couvercle soit fermé*.
- Leaving out what to do when something goes wrong.

## Summary

Instructions have a title, an introduction, a short materials list, numbered steps in the imperative, warnings marked *Attention !* or *Important :*, general rules in the infinitive and a short troubleshooting section. Keep one register from start to finish, and use the subjunctive after *il faut que*.
