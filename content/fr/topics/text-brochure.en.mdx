## Big idea

A brochure has one job: to make the reader want to go, to buy or to take part. It combines an attractive presentation with clear practical information. The tone is warm and persuasive, the sentences are short, and the reader is addressed directly, with vous or the imperative.

## Explanation

A tourist brochure is built from short blocks that the reader can scan quickly:

- **Title and slogan.** The title names the place or the offer, and the slogan sums up the promise in a few words: « Une semaine entre falaises, rivières et villages de pierre ! »
- **Subtitles.** Each subtitle names the topic of its block: « Ce que vous allez découvrir », « Informations pratiques », « Pourquoi venir ? »
- **Short paragraphs and bullet lists.** Lists make activities easy to scan, for example « Descendez la rivière en canoë. »
- **A direct address.** Speak to the reader with vous and the imperative: « Venez découvrir… », « Profitez de… », « Réservez dès aujourd'hui. » The imperative is the mood that most often pushes the reader to act.
- **Practical information.** Give opening times, the best season, a rough price and the address. State prices in general terms, such as « à partir de 240 euros » or « environ 60 euros par personne ».
- **Contact details.** End with an address, a telephone number or an e-mail address.

Use the présent for general facts (« L'abbaye date du XIIe siècle. ») and the futur simple to promise an experience (« Vous verrez la vallée depuis le sommet. »). Vary the sentence structure, but keep it simple, because a brochure is read quickly.

## Worked example

Task: a brochure for an adventure and culture stay in a valley.

Slogan:
« Une semaine entre falaises, rivières et patrimoine. »

Activities, in the imperative, as a bullet list:
- « Descendez la rivière en canoë, entre falaises et forêts. »
- « Visitez l'abbaye, fondée au XIIe siècle. »

Practical information, short and factual:
« Le séjour est ouvert d'avril à octobre. Comptez environ 240 euros pour cinq jours, hébergement et repas compris. »

Closing argument, with a call to act:
« Ce séjour vaut le détour. Réservez dès aujourd'hui ! »

The text answers three questions in order: what can I do, how do I get there and pay, and why should I come?

## How it is examined

Paper 1 is worth 30 marks: 12 for Language (Criterion A), 12 for Message (Criterion B) and 6 for Conceptual understanding (Criterion C). For a brochure, Criterion B checks that all three content points are covered: what to see and do, practical information (how to get there, when to go and roughly what it costs), and why the experience is worth it. Criterion A checks the imperative, the futur simple, adjectives and a consistent register. Criterion C looks for a clear appeal: the brochure shows what makes the experience valuable.

Keep to 250 to 400 words. Plan for about 5 minutes, write for about 25 minutes and check for about 10 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- **A catalogue without a reader.** Activities listed without vous or an imperative do not persuade. Write « Vous descendrez la rivière… » or « Descendez la rivière… ».
- **Vague prices.** « C'est pas cher » is not a price. Write « à partir de 240 euros ».
- **Missing practical information.** A brochure without opening times or an address does not help the reader act. Include at least one time, one price and one address.
- **Sentences that are too long.** A long, complicated sentence is hard to scan. Split it: « La vallée est belle. Elle est aussi très calme. »
- **Wrong register.** « Trop bien, allez-y ! » is too casual for a tourist office. Write « Venez découvrir une vallée unique ! »

## Summary

- A brochure has a title and a slogan, clear subtitles, short paragraphs and bullet lists.
- It speaks to the reader with vous and the imperative, the mood that most often pushes the reader to act.
- Practical information covers the season, the way to get there and a rough price; contact details close the text.
- The best brochures answer three questions: what can I do, how do I get there, and why should I come?
- Keep sentences short and the register warm and persuasive.
