## Big idea

Paper 2 reading tests whether you can find information in a text. Almost every item points to one sentence or paragraph. Find that place first, then answer. You do not need to understand every word.

## Explanation

The reading items come in four kinds. Each kind has its own answer format, and the format counts for the mark.

- **vrai, faux or non mentionné** (Identify). Choose vrai when the text says the same thing in other words, faux when it says the opposite, and non mentionné when it does not say it at all. For vrai and faux, quote the sentence that proves it.
- **Short answer in English** (State). The prompt says "Answer in English". Write the key words, in any order. A short phrase is enough.
- **Matching** (Match). Find the paragraph (A to E) or the speaker with the same idea. The question paraphrases the text, so look for the meaning, not the same words.
- **Quote or gap fill** (Quote, State). Write the French word exactly as the text has it, in the same form. The practice page accepts an answer without accents when they do not change the word (ecole for école), and it shows the spelling with accents; ou and où are different words. The exam expects the accents.

The most common trap is non mentionné. If the text is silent, the answer is not faux.

## Worked example

Read this short text, an invented example written for this topic.

« Depuis septembre, le bus de la ligne 4 ne passe plus après vingt heures. Les habitants ont signé une pétition, mais la mairie n'a pas encore répondu. Le maire promet une nouvelle ligne pour l'hiver. »

- "The bus no longer runs after 8 pm." is **vrai**. Quote: « ne passe plus après vingt heures ». Vingt heures is 8 pm.
- "The town hall has already replied." is **faux**. Quote: « la mairie n'a pas encore répondu ».
- "The bus company has agreed to a survey." is **non mentionné**. The text is silent, so there is nothing to quote.
- "In which month did the change start?" needs an English answer: **September**, from « Depuis septembre ».
- "Which word means 'the people who live in a place'?" needs a French word: **habitants**, with its final -s.

## Visual

| Item | You write | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| vrai, faux, non mentionné | one word and a quotation | faux, because the text does not mention the idea |
| short answer in English | the key words | a long sentence without a key word |
| matching | a letter, A to E | a shared word instead of the meaning |
| vocabulary | the French word as written | your own translation |

## How it is examined

The portal's practice set uses three texts, ten items and 45 minutes; check the exam format in the guide before you rely on it. Each item has one fixed answer: a choice, a letter, a key word or a French word from the text.

## Common mistakes

- Writing faux when the text does not say the thing. The answer is non mentionné.
- Quoting a sentence about another person or paragraph.
- Giving the wrong form: « habitant » instead of « habitants ».
- Translating a French word in a vocabulary item.

## Summary

- Find the place in the text first, then answer.
- vrai and faux need a quotation; non mentionné means the text is silent.
- Short answers in English: key words, in any order.
- Vocabulary: copy the French word exactly, in its form.
- Practise a set of three texts in 45 minutes, then read the markscheme.
