## Big idea

The présent (present tense) describes what happens now, what happens regularly and what is generally true. It appears in almost every text, so a mistake in it is noticed at once. Most verbs follow three simple patterns; a few very common verbs are irregular and must be learned by heart.

## Explanation

Regular verbs keep the stem of the infinitive and take one set of endings for each person. There are three groups:

| Group | Example | Stem | je | nous | ils |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -er | parler | parl- | parle | parlons | parlent |
| -ir | finir | fin- | finis | finissons | finissent |
| -re | vendre | vend- | vends | vendons | vendent |

The -ir verbs add -iss- in the nous, vous and ils forms. The il form of a -re verb has no ending: il vend, not il vends. Before a vowel, je becomes j': j'habite.

Spelling changes keep the sound right: manger gives nous mangeons (the e keeps the g soft), and commencer gives nous commençons (the c becomes ç before o).

The most common irregular verbs are être, avoir, aller and faire, then prendre, pouvoir and vouloir. Learn them whole: je suis, tu es, il est, nous sommes, vous êtes, ils sont; j'ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont.

Reflexive verbs (verbes pronominaux) take an extra pronoun before the verb: je me lève, tu te lèves, il se lève. In a negative sentence, ne and pas go around the pronoun and the verb: je ne me lève pas.

The present has four main uses:

- now: Je travaille maintenant.
- habits: Je prends le bus tous les matins.
- general truths: L'eau bout à cent degrés.
- an action that started in the past and continues, with depuis: J'habite à Genève depuis deux ans.

The future and conditional topic covers aller with an infinitive and si in a real condition.

## Worked example

Read this short text. The present forms are in bold.

« **Je m'appelle** Léa et **j'habite** à Lyon. Chaque matin, **je me lève** à sept heures, **je prends** le bus et **j'arrive** au lycée vers huit heures. **J'ai** cours de français le mardi. »

- **je m'appelle** is a reflexive verb: the pronoun m' comes before the verb.
- **j'habite** is a regular -er verb: stem habit-.
- **je me lève** is a reflexive verb, ending -e.
- **je prends** is the irregular prendre: stem prend-, ending -s.
- **j'arrive** is a regular -er verb.
- **J'ai** is the irregular avoir: je ai becomes j'ai before a vowel.

The whole text is in the present, because it describes a routine.

| je | parle | finis | vends | suis | ai (j'ai) |
| tu | parles | finis | vends | es | as |
| il / elle | parle | finit | vend | est | a |
| nous | parlons | finissons | vendons | sommes | avons |
| vous | parlez | finissez | vendez | êtes | avez |
| ils / elles | parlent | finissent | vendent | sont | ont |

Compare the tu forms: -es, -is and -s. Être and avoir have their own forms.

## How it is examined

On Paper 1, Criterion A (Language) rewards accurate verb forms. Wrong endings such as il vends, a missing -iss- or a misplaced reflexive pronoun cost marks, even when the ideas are good. On Paper 2, the present is the commonest tense in articles and dialogues; recognise habits, current situations and depuis with the present.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting -iss- in the plural of -ir verbs: « nous finons » is wrong; « nous finissons » is right.
- Adding -s to the il form of a -re verb: « il vends » is wrong; « il vend » is right.
- Putting the reflexive pronoun after the verb: « je lève me » is wrong; « je me lève » is right.
- Using être for age: « Je suis seize ans » is wrong; « J'ai seize ans » is right.

## Summary

- Regular endings: -er (-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent), -ir (-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent), -re (-s, -s, none, -ons, -ez, -ent).
- Learn être, avoir, aller, faire, prendre, pouvoir and vouloir as irregular forms.
- In reflexive verbs the pronoun goes before the verb.
- Use the present for now, habits, general truths and depuis with an ongoing action.
- Check the -iss- plural and the spelling changes (ç, and the e of mangeons).
- Watch the il form of -re verbs: il vend, with no -s.
