## Big idea

Sharing the planet is the fifth IB French B theme. It covers the environment and climate, biodiversity, waste and recycling, clean energy and transport, water, food, sustainable cities, responsible consumption and international solidarity. In Paper 1 you might write an article, a report or a proposal about a local environmental action, so you need words for problems, causes and solutions. In Paper 2 the texts often describe what communities, companies and governments do for the planet, so you must understand the vocabulary and the tenses in context.

## Explanation

**The passive.** Use *être* with a past participle when the action or its result matters more than the person who does it: *Les déchets sont triés sur place.* (The waste is sorted on site.) The participle agrees with the subject: *la rivière est nettoyée*, *les bouteilles sont recyclées*. In writing, you can also use *on* with an active verb: *On trie les déchets sur place.*

**The conditionnel présent.** Use it for polite suggestions, for hypotheses and for a result that depends on a condition: *Il faudrait réduire la consommation d'eau.* (We should reduce water consumption.) In a *si* sentence, the imparfait follows *si* and the conditionnel goes in the main clause: *Si la mairie soutenait ces projets, la ville deviendrait un modèle.* The conditionnel does not follow *si* in a *si* condition, but it is possible after *si* in an indirect question, for example *je me demande si tu viendrais* (I wonder if you would come).

**The subjunctive after *il faut que*.** Use the subjunctive after *il faut que* and similar expressions: *Il faut que nous réduisions nos déchets.* (We have to reduce our waste.) The form *nous réduisions* looks like the imparfait, but here it is the subjunctive, because *il faut que* requires it.

**Purpose, cause and means.** *Grâce à* gives the cause: *grâce à cette initiative* (thanks to this initiative). *Pour* and *afin de* with an infinitive give the purpose: *afin de protéger les oiseaux*. *En* + present participle is the French *gérondif*; it shows how something is done: *en triant ses déchets* (by sorting one's waste).

## Worked example

A student wrote this paragraph about a local environmental action.

Dans notre quartier, un groupe de voisins a décidé de nettoyer le parc chaque samedi matin. Au début, peu de personnes participaient ; aujourd'hui, plus de trente habitants se joignent à eux. Les déchets sont triés sur place, et le compost est utilisé pour les jardins partagés. Grâce à cette initiative, le nombre d'oiseaux a augmenté et les enfants apprennent à respecter la nature. Si la mairie soutenait davantage ces projets, la ville deviendrait un modèle pour les quartiers voisins. Il faut que chacun s'engage, mais la solution commence par un geste simple : trier ses déchets chaque jour.

**Analysis**

- *Les déchets sont triés* and *le compost est utilisé* are passives. The participles agree with the subject: *triés* (masculine plural) and *utilisé* (masculine singular). The writer focuses on the action, not on the people who do it.
- *Peu de personnes participaient* is in the imparfait, because it describes how things were at the start.
- *Si la mairie soutenait davantage ces projets, la ville deviendrait un modèle* combines an imparfait in the *si* clause with a conditionnel in the main clause.
- *Il faut que chacun s'engage* uses the subjunctive after *il faut que*.
- *Grâce à cette initiative* gives the cause, and *trier ses déchets* is a fixed expression to learn as a whole.

## How it is examined

Sharing the planet can appear in any Paper 1 task. Articles, reports, proposals, speeches and formal letters to a local council often use this theme. Your answer is 250 to 400 words, and three criteria apply:

- Criterion A, Language (12 marks): tenses, the passive and the conditionnel, agreements and a range of vocabulary.
- Criterion B, Message (12 marks): a clear purpose, all content points and a tone suited to the reader.
- Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks): you show that you understand the issue, for example why a local action matters for the wider planet.

In Paper 2 you find words in context, explain phrases, and decide whether a statement is true, false or not mentioned. Learn fixed expressions such as *mettre fin à* and *trier ses déchets* as whole units.

## Common mistakes

- *L'environnement* (the environment) is masculine. *Environ* means "about", as in *environ dix espèces* (about ten species). Do not mix them up.
- *Le climat* is masculine: *le climat change*, not *la climat*.
- *Sensible* means "sensitive", as in *sensible à la pollution* (sensitive to pollution). For the English sense of "reasonable", use *raisonnable*.
- After *il faut que*, use the subjunctive: *il faut que nous réduisions*, not *il faut que nous réduisons*.
- After a negation, *du*, *de la*, *des*, *un* and *une* all become *de* (*d'* before a vowel): *il n'y a pas d'eau*, *pas de voitures*.
- *Réaliser* means "to carry out" (*réaliser un projet*). For "I realise that", use *je me rends compte que*.
- With the passive, the participle agrees with the subject: *les déchets sont triés*, not *sont trié*.

## Summary

- The fifth theme covers the environment, biodiversity, waste, energy and transport, water, food, cities, consumption and solidarity.
- Use the passive to focus on the action or its result: *les déchets sont triés*.
- Use the conditionnel for suggestions and hypotheses; after *si* with the imparfait, the main clause takes the conditionnel.
- Use the subjunctive after *il faut que*.
- *Grâce à* gives a cause; *en* with a present participle shows how something is done.
- Learn fixed expressions such as *mettre fin à* and *trier ses déchets* as whole units.
- Watch the traps *environnement* and *environ*, *le climat* (masculine) and *sensible*.
