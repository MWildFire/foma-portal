## Big idea

A review tells the reader what an object is like, how good it is and whether they should try it. A good review gives an honest judgement with clear reasons, and it leaves the best discoveries to the reader. In Paper 1 you write in a lively, balanced voice, in a neutral to elevated register (registre soutenu).

## Explanation

A review has five parts, and each one has its own job:

- **The title** names the object and gives a rating, for example « 4 étoiles sur 5 » or « 8/10 ».
- **The introduction** presents the object and says why you are writing about it. Do not reveal the plot of a film or the key features of an app: the reader should discover them.
- **The evaluation** gives strengths (points forts) and weaknesses (points faibles). Each point needs an example, otherwise it is only an opinion.
- **The opinion** states your overall view and the reasons for it.
- **The recommendation** says for whom the object is suitable and why.

The grammar follows the same logic. Use the présent to describe the object and to make judgements: « Le rythme est lent. » Use the passé composé for your own experience: « J'ai utilisé l'application pendant six mois. » Choose precise evaluative adjectives such as captivant (gripping), convaincant (convincing), décevant (disappointing) and fade (bland), and avoid general words such as bien or cool. Comparisons make a judgement easier to understand: « Elle est plus efficace que… », « moins… que », « aussi… que ».

## Worked example

Task: a review of a note-taking app that changed how you study.

Introduction (no spoilers; it names the object and gives the time frame):
« Depuis six mois, j'utilise une application de prise de notes qui a transformé ma façon de réviser. »

Strength, with an example:
« Son point fort est la recherche : en tapant un mot, on retrouve une note écrite il y a plusieurs mois. »

Weakness, with an example:
« En revanche, la synchronisation est parfois lente, ce qui est gênant quand on change d'appareil. »

Recommendation, with a named audience:
« Je la recommande surtout aux élèves qui travaillent sur plusieurs appareils. »

Each sentence has one job. The reader learns what the object is, what is good about it, what is not, and who should try it.

## How it is examined

Paper 1 is worth 30 marks. Criterion A (Language, 12 marks) checks accuracy and range: tenses, agreements, evaluative vocabulary and comparisons. Criterion B (Message, 12 marks) checks that you cover the three content points: the object presented without spoilers, strengths and weaknesses with examples, and a recommendation for a named audience. Criterion C (Conceptual understanding, 6 marks) rewards a review that links the object to how people create or communicate, rather than a list of features.

Keep to 250 to 400 words. Plan for about 5 minutes, write for about 25 minutes and check for about 10 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- **Spoilers.** Giving away the ending of a film or the key discovery in an app spoils the reader's experience. Keep the best details for the reader.
- **Opinion without evidence.** « C'est bien. » is not a judgement. Write « La présentation est claire et agréable à lire. »
- **Spoken register.** « C'est un truc trop cool » is too informal. Write « C'est un outil très utile. »
- **No recommendation or no audience.** End with a clear recommendation for a named group of readers.
- **Description only.** A list of features is not a review. Add your evaluation to each point.

## Summary

- A review has a title with a rating, a spoiler-free introduction, strengths and weaknesses with examples, an opinion with reasons and a recommendation.
- Use the présent for the object and the judgements, and the passé composé for your own experience.
- Precise evaluative adjectives and comparisons make the judgement clear.
- Keep a neutral to elevated register, with no spoken expressions.
- Plan for about 5 minutes, write for about 25 and check for about 10.
