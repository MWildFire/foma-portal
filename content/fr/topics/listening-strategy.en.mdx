## Big idea

Paper 2 plays each recording twice. You do not need every word. You need the answer to each question, proved with the right words and given in the form the question asks.

## Explanation

Before the first listening, read every question and underline one key word in each. It tells you what to listen for.

In the first listening, get the gist: who speaks, where, and about what. Answer what you are sure of.

In the second listening, check the doubtful answers and find the evidence.

The recording almost never repeats the question's words. « Le bus est supprimé » may be answered by « il n'y aura pas de bus ». Listen for the idea.

Each item type has its own rule:

- **vrai, faux or non mentionné.** Write vrai when the recording says the same thing, faux when it says the opposite, and non mentionné when it says nothing about the point. Faux is not non mentionné: a false statement is contradicted, while a point that is not mentioned is absent.
- **Quotation.** Copy the exact words that prove your answer.
- **Multiple choice.** Cross out the options the recording contradicts. A wrong option often repeats a word in a different meaning.
- **Short answer or note.** Write exactly what is asked: one word for one word. Do not write a sentence.
- **Matching.** Match the idea, not the word. Follow the instruction on letters.

## Worked example

Read this announcement:

« Le bus de vingt heures est supprimé ce soir. Les voyageurs peuvent prendre le train de vingt et une heures à la place. »

- « Le bus de vingt heures circule comme d'habitude ce soir. » is **faux**: the recording says the bus is cancelled.
- « Le bus est conduit par une femme. » is **non mentionné**: the driver is not mentioned.
- « Les voyageurs peuvent prendre le train. » is **vrai**. The quotation is « peuvent prendre le train de vingt et une heures à la place ».

## How it is examined

The items in this course come in five forms: vrai, faux or non mentionné with a quotation; multiple choice (A, B, C); short answers; note completion; and matching. The instruction tells you the form. The form is part of the answer.

## Common mistakes

- Writing faux when the point is simply not mentioned.
- Quoting a sentence about another point, or only part of the evidence.
- Writing 2 when a word is asked, or a sentence when one word is asked.
- Choosing an option because it repeats a word from the recording.
- Leaving a blank. Make an informed guess instead.
- Writing a word without its accents, such as « ecole » for « école » or « mer » for « mère ». The practice page accepts « ecole » because the accent does not change the word; « mer » for « mère » is a different word and is marked wrong, and so is « ou » for « où ».

## Summary

- Read every question first, and underline one key word in each.
- First listening: the gist. Second listening: the doubtful answers and the evidence.
- The recording uses other words for the same idea.
- vrai means the same, faux the opposite, and non mentionné means not said.
- When asked, quote exactly. Follow the form the question asks for.
- Never leave a blank.
