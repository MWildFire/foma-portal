## Big idea

A social media post is a short personal text for readers who scroll quickly. It must catch attention in the first line, be easy to read on a phone and invite a reaction. In the P1 task the post shares an experience and explains what it changed for the author.

## Explanation

A good post has five elements:

- **Hook:** a title or a first line that makes the reader want to continue, for example a striking statement or a question.
- **Short paragraphs:** two to four sentences each, so that the text is easy to read on a screen.
- **Emojis (optional):** up to three. They show mood and never replace words.
- **Hashtags:** a few at the very end, so that people can find the post by its topic.
- **Call to action:** a sentence that asks readers to respond, for example a question or an invitation to comment.

Choose *tu* for friends and classmates, or a neutral tone with *vous* for a wider public. Use *je* for yourself. Keep the same choice for the whole post.

A long post can also be a short series. Mark the parts 1/3, 2/3 and 3/3, and give each part its own hook, so that readers come back for the next one.

## Worked example

The title and the first two sentences of a post about a stay in Bordeaux:

*Trois mois à Bordeaux, et je ne parle plus comme avant ! ✈️*

*Au début, je traduisais tout dans ma tête. Puis, peu à peu, j'ai commencé à comprendre les blagues.*

The title is the hook: it names the place and the change in one line. The next sentences show a before and an after, with the linking words *au début* and *puis*. Notice the tenses: the story is told in the passé composé (*j'ai commencé*) and the imperfect (*je traduisais*), not in the present.

## How it is examined

The P1 writing task asks for 250 to 400 words in one of the offered text types, and a social media post is one of them. Criterion A Language (12) looks at the tenses of a personal story (passé composé and imperfect), linking words and accurate agreements. Criterion B Message (12) looks at the layout and at the three content points, in order: the experience (what and where), what changed for you, and a call to the readers. Criterion C Conceptual understanding (6) rewards reflection on what the experience taught you, not only a list of events.

## Common mistakes

- Opening with a flat sentence such as *Je vais vous parler de mon voyage*, which gives the reader no reason to keep reading.
- Writing one long block of text without paragraphs, which is hard to read on a phone.
- Mixing *tu* and *vous* in the same post.
- Using too many hashtags: two to five at the end is enough.
- Ending without a call to action, so the readers have nothing to do.
- Telling the whole story in the present tense, with no passé composé.

## Summary

A social media post has a hook, short paragraphs, three content points in order (the experience, what changed, a call to the readers), optional emojis and a few hashtags at the end. Keep one register from the first line to the last, and use the passé composé and the imperfect for the story.
