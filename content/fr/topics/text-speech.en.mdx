## Big idea

A speech is heard, not read, so the listener cannot go back to a sentence they missed. The speech must be easy to follow: an opening that catches attention, a plan that is announced in advance, a few strong arguments with examples, and a call to action at the end. The register is formal (soutenu), and the speaker addresses the audience directly with vous.

## Explanation

A formal speech usually has six parts:

1. the greeting to the audience, for example « Mesdames et Messieurs, » for an assembly;
2. an opening that catches attention: a question, an anecdote or a striking fact;
3. the topic, stated in one sentence;
4. the announced plan: « Premièrement… Deuxièmement… Enfin… »;
5. the arguments, each with an example, and a call to action;
6. the closing thanks: « Je vous remercie de votre attention. »

Use rhetorical questions (« Combien d'entre vous ont déjà attendu vingt minutes à la cantine ? ») and repetition for emphasis. Address the listeners with vous, and use « nous » when you speak for the whole group. For a proposal, use the conditional (« je proposerais », « cela profiterait à… »); for the call to action, use the imperative (« soutenez », « venez »).

Connectors help the listener: « d'abord », « ensuite », « en effet » (indeed) and « bref » (in short) when you sum up. Keep the sentences short enough to say aloud without losing breath.

## Worked example

Weak opening: « Salut à tous, je suis là pour parler de la cantine. »

Stronger opening: « Mesdames et Messieurs, combien d'entre vous ont déjà fait la queue vingt minutes à la cantine sans avoir le temps de manger ? »

Then the plan: « Aujourd'hui, je viens vous proposer une idée simple. Premièrement, je vais expliquer ma proposition. Deuxièmement, je montrerai qui en profite. Enfin, je dirai ce que chacun peut faire. »

The steps:

1. change the greeting to a formal form that suits an audience;
2. start with a question that the listeners can answer in their heads;
3. announce the plan with clear markers, so that the audience can follow;
4. use « Enfin » for the last point, then move on to the call to action.

## Visual

A speech can be read as a route. Each stop has a marker that the listener hears.

| Stop | Marker | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | Mesdames et Messieurs | Mesdames et Messieurs, |
| Hook | a question | Combien d'entre vous… ? |
| Topic | Aujourd'hui, je viens vous proposer… | an idea in one sentence |
| Plan | Premièrement, Deuxièmement, Enfin | three announced points |
| Arguments | Prenez l'exemple de… | a named example |
| Call to action | Alors, je vous le demande… | soutenez, venez |
| Thanks | Je vous remercie de votre attention. | the last line |

## How it is examined

The P1 speech is marked with the same three criteria. Examiners look for a clear audience, a hook, an announced plan that is followed, three content points with examples, a call to action and closing thanks. For criterion A, the register must stay formal and the speech markers must be correct. For criterion C, the speaker must show that the proposal matters to the audience. A text that reads like an essay, with no direct address and no plan, loses marks under criteria B and C.

## Common mistakes

- Opening with « Salut » or « Cher journal », which suit a friend or a diary, not an audience.
- Forgetting the plan, so that the listener cannot follow the arguments.
- Writing sentences that are too long to say aloud. Use shorter sentences and clear connectors.
- Getting the subjunctive wrong after « je propose que ». The correct form is « je propose que la bibliothèque reste ouverte ».
- Ending without a call to action, or with « Voilà, c'est tout » (too casual for a speech).
- Using « En somme » for the first point. « En somme » introduces a conclusion, not the start of a plan.

## Summary

- A speech has a greeting, a hook, a topic, an announced plan, arguments with examples, a call to action and thanks.
- Use « Mesdames et Messieurs » for an assembly, vous for the audience, and rhetorical questions to get attention.
- Announce each point with Premièrement, Deuxièmement and Enfin. Use Enfin only for the last point.
- Keep the sentences short enough to say aloud, and finish with a clear call to action.
