## Big idea

A personal blog post is a public text in which the writer shares an experience and an opinion with readers who follow them. The post has to be personal, clear and engaging from the title onwards.

## Explanation

A blog post is written in the first person and speaks to the readers directly. Here the readers follow the writer's life, so address them with tu (the informal "you"). The register is informal to neutral: short sentences and a few exclamations are natural, but avoid slang.

Three kinds of language do most of the work:

- Tenses. Use the passé composé for the events of the story (« Nous sommes allés dans le Finistère »), the imparfait for the background such as the weather or a mood (« Il faisait beau »), and the présent for the opinion and the call to react.
- Opinion phrases: à mon avis (in my opinion), selon moi (for me), je pense que (I think that). Always give a reason after them.
- Engagement and links: rhetorical questions, such as « Qui n'a jamais ressenti ça ? », and linking words such as d'abord (first), ensuite (then), enfin (finally) and finalement (in the end).

A blog post usually follows this layout:

```text
Title (the hook)
Date line
Intro: what the post is about
Anecdote: one experience (passé composé, imparfait)
Reflection: what changed (présent, opinion phrases)
Question or call to react
Signature (first name or pseudonym)
```

## Worked example

Three lines from the model post:

- « Je me suis sentie exclue, comme si une partie de ma famille m'échappait. » The passé composé reports the event. « Sentie » and « exclue » are feminine because the writer is a woman, so the past participle and the adjective agree with her.
- « Selon moi, notre identité ne vient pas seulement de nos origines. » The phrase « selon moi » marks the writer's opinion.
- « Qui n'a jamais eu l'impression de changer en changeant de lieu ? » This rhetorical question expects the reader to agree.

## How it is examined

The blog task is one writing task of 250 to 400 words, worth 30 marks. Criterion A, Language (12 marks), rewards accurate tenses, agreements and accents, and a range of structures. Criterion B, Message (12 marks), rewards the development of all three content points, a clear blog form and a consistent tu register for the readers. Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks), rewards a real understanding of identity as shaped by people and experience, with a reasoned opinion. Plan for about 40 minutes: 5 minutes to plan the three points, 30 minutes to write and 5 minutes to check agreements and accents.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a letter instead of a post: opening with « Madame, Monsieur, » and closing with « Cordialement ». Fix: start with a title and « Salut à tous ! », and sign with a first name.
- Mixing vous and tu for the readers. Fix: choose tu, then check every verb and pronoun to the end of the text.
- Using only the passé composé. Fix: put the weather, the moods and the habits of the background in the imparfait.
- Giving an opinion with no reason. Fix: after « À mon avis » or « Selon moi », add a reason or an example from your story.

## Summary

- A blog post is personal and public: a title, a date, a story, an opinion and a call to react.
- Use the passé composé for events and the imparfait for the background.
- Give opinions with à mon avis, selon moi or je pense que, and always add a reason.
- Link the paragraphs with d'abord, ensuite, enfin and finalement.
- Finish with a question or an invitation, and sign with a first name or a pseudonym.
- Address the readers with tu and keep it to the end.
