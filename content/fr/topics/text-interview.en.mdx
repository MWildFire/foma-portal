## Big idea

A written interview is a conversation put on paper. The reader must understand who is speaking, why this person is interesting and what they think, without hearing their voice. A good interview has a short introduction, clearly marked questions and answers, open questions that invite explanations, and answers that sound like a real person.

## Explanation

A written interview usually has these parts:

1. the introduction: who the person is, what they have done and why the interview matters;
2. the questions and answers, with each speaker marked by a dash (—) or by the name in bold;
3. open questions, which start with Pourquoi, Comment or Qu'est-ce que and need more than yes or no;
4. answers in the first person (je, nous), which give facts, examples and feelings;
5. a closing question, for example about the future or about advice.

The register depends on the person. With adults, the interviewer uses vous. A tu is possible only if the context makes it natural, for example between peers in a youth magazine. In the P1 task, use vous unless the context says otherwise.

Inversion is used in questions with vous and with il or elle: « Pourquoi avez-vous choisi ce métier ? », « Comment fonctionne-t-il ? ». Use the conditional for hopes and plans (« je voudrais », « j'aimerais ») and the subjunctive after « j'aimerais que » and « je souhaite que »: « j'aimerais qu'il soit installé partout ».

## Worked example

Here is a weak exchange:

— Tu l'as fait toi ? C'est cool ?

— Oui, c'est bien.

Here is an improved version:

— Comment vous est venue l'idée de cet appareil ?

— Mon voisin a subi un dégât des eaux. Personne ne l'avait prévenu à temps, alors j'ai voulu créer un appareil qui alerte avant la catastrophe.

The steps:

1. use vous with an adult, and the polite form of the question;
2. ask an open question with Comment, which invites an explanation;
3. answer in the first person and give a concrete reason (the neighbour's flood);
4. mark each line with a dash, so that the reader sees who speaks.

## Visual

A written interview is easiest to read when every speaker has the same marker. The table shows the layout.

| Element | Marker | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | a short paragraph before the first line | Sophie Morel, vingt-quatre ans, présente un détecteur de fuites. |
| Question | a dash at the start of the line | — Comment fonctionne-t-il ? |
| Answer | a dash, then the answer in the first person | — Il se place sous un évier. |
| Closing question | a question about the future | — Comment imaginez-vous l'avenir de votre appareil ? |

## How it is examined

The P1 interview is marked with the same three criteria. Examiners look for an introduction that says who the person is and why the interview matters; all three content points in order (the invention and how it works, the difficulties, the advice or vision); open questions; first-person answers; and a clear marking of the speakers. For criterion A, the questions must use the correct inversion (« Comment fonctionne-t-il ? ») and the register must match vous. The task asks for 10 to 14 lines of questions and answers within 250 to 400 words, so count both before you finish.

## Common mistakes

- Writing questions that can only be answered with yes or no (« Aimez-vous… ? ») when an open question is needed.
- Mixing vous and tu in the same interview.
- Forgetting the dash or the name of the speaker, so that the reader cannot follow who speaks.
- Writing the answers in the third person (« Elle a dit que… ») instead of in the speaker's own voice.
- Forgetting the inversion: write « Comment fonctionne-t-il ? », not « Comment il fonctionne ? ».
- Getting the subjunctive wrong after « j'aimerais que »: « j'aimerais qu'il soit installé », not « j'aimerais qu'il est installé ».

## Summary

- An interview has a short introduction, clearly marked questions and answers, and a closing question.
- Ask open questions with Pourquoi, Comment and Qu'est-ce que, and use inversion: « Comment fonctionne-t-il ? ».
- Mark every line with a dash or the speaker's name, and keep the register: vous with adults.
- Answers are in the first person, with facts, examples and feelings.
