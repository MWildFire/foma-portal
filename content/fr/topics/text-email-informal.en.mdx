## Big idea

An informal e-mail is a message to a friend or to someone you know well. Its job is to keep the relationship alive: to share news, to invite, to apologise or to give advice, in a warm and direct style.

## Explanation

An informal e-mail follows a clear layout. It has a subject line (« Objet : … »), a greeting (« Salut Léa, »), an opening with news, thanks or an apology for a late answer, two to four short paragraphs, a closing (« À bientôt, », « Bises, » or « Je t'embrasse, ») and a first-name signature. The register is informal: tu, short sentences, a few exclamations and no formal phrases such as « Je vous prie d'agréer… ».

Three grammar points carry most of the message:

- Impératif for invitations and advice, as in « Dis-moi… » or « N'oublie pas ton appareil photo ! ».
- Futur proche for plans, as in « On va se voir samedi ? ».
- Conditionnel for a polite proposal, as in « Tu pourrais venir ? ».

The passé composé reports the news (« Tu as enfin déménagé ! »), and the présent gives facts about yourself and your opinions.

An informal e-mail usually follows this layout:

```text
Objet : subject line
Salut Léa,
Opening: news, thanks or an apology
Two to four short paragraphs: who you are, what you think, the invitation
Closing: Je t'embrasse,
First name
```

## Worked example

Here are three lines from the model e-mail. Each one shows a feature of the informal register.

- « Alors, tu as enfin déménagé ! Je suis tellement contente pour toi. » The passé composé reports the news, and « contente » agrees with the writer, who is a woman.
- « Dis-moi si ça te va, et n'oublie pas ton appareil photo ! » Two imperatives give direct instructions, and the exclamation mark keeps the tone warm.
- « Tu pourrais venir vers dix heures ? » The conditionnel makes the invitation polite and leaves room for an answer.

## Visual

| Element | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject line | tells the reader what the e-mail is about | « Objet : Bienvenue dans notre ville ! » |
| Greeting | sets the friendly tone | « Salut Léa, » |
| Opening | gives the news or an apology | « Désolée pour ma réponse tardive » |
| Body paragraphs | develop the content points | « Je suis née à Lyon, mais ma grand-mère vient de Porto » |
| Invitation | gives a concrete plan | « on va se voir samedi ? » |
| Closing | ends the message warmly | « Je t'embrasse, » |
| Signature | shows who wrote the message | « Inès » |

The subject line is one short line. The rest follows the order of a letter, but in a relaxed way.

## How it is examined

The e-mail task is worth 30 marks, with the same three criteria. Criterion A, Language (12 marks), rewards tu throughout, the futur proche, the impératif and the conditionnel, correct agreements (for example « contente » and « désolée ») and connectors such as alors, bref and honnêtement. Criterion B, Message (12 marks), rewards the three content points (who you are, what you think, a concrete invitation with a day, a time and a place) and a complete e-mail layout. Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks), rewards a view of identity that goes beyond origins, for example one that gives weight to choices and relationships. The text is 250 to 400 words. Plan for about 40 minutes: 5 minutes for the three points, 30 minutes to write and 5 minutes to check the tu forms and the agreements.

## Common mistakes

- Using vous with a friend, as in « Je vous invite à dîner. ». Fix: use tu and the tu forms of verbs and pronouns, as in « Tu veux venir ? ».
- Writing a vague invitation, such as « On devrait se voir un jour. ». Fix: give a day, a time and a place.
- Closing a letter to a friend with « Cordialement, ». Fix: use « Je t'embrasse, » or « Bises, ».
- Leaving out the subject line or the first-name signature. Fix: start with « Objet : » and finish with your first name.

## Summary

- An informal e-mail keeps a friendship alive: a subject line, a greeting, news, a few paragraphs, a warm closing and a first name.
- Use tu throughout, with the matching verb forms.
- Use the futur proche for plans, the impératif for invitations and advice, and the conditionnel for polite proposals.
- Give a concrete invitation with a day, a time and a place.
- Show who you are: your origins, languages, passions and your view of what defines a person.
- Close with « Je t'embrasse, » or « Bises, » and sign with your first name.
