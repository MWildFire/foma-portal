## Big idea

The papers repeat their patterns. Paper 1 offers the same few text types again and again, and reading and listening use the same handful of question types. If you know the pattern, you know what to practise first.

## Explanation

**Paper 1.** Each session gives three writing tasks, each on a different theme. You write one of them in 250 to 400 words. Every task offers two or three text types. Across the papers, the blog, the informal e-mail and the speech come up most often (eight of the 21 different tasks each), followed by the article, the brochure and the proposal (six each). The essay and the report appear only once each. Social organization and sharing the planet are the most frequent themes of the tasks (six each).

**Reading.** Each paper has three texts, A, B and C, with 36 to 40 questions for 40 marks. Four types of question appear. Identify asks for true, false or not mentioned, or a choice. Quote asks for the exact French words from the text. Match asks for the paragraph or text that goes with each statement. State asks for a short answer, in English or French as the question says. Identify, Quote and Match are about equally common; State is the least common.

**Listening.** Each paper has three recordings, 16 to 21 questions and 25 marks. The types are Identify, Note (one word or a number, written as you hear it), Match and State. Identify is the most common, then Note.

## Worked example

The table on this page counts different tasks, not papers. "Blog post: 8 / 21" means that the blog was offered in eight of the 21 different tasks. That is more than one task in three, so a blog is a sensible type to practise well. Read a count as a guide to what to prepare, not as a promise about the exam on 28 October.

## How it is examined

- Paper 1: the task is marked on criteria A (Language, 12 marks), B (Message, 12 marks) and C (Conceptual understanding, 6 marks). You choose one task, and the text type comes from those the task offers.
- Reading: 40 marks in the real paper. Quote answers must be exact.
- Listening: 25 marks. Each recording is played twice. The mock gives 60 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- Preparing only the text types you like. Practise every type in the list, because the task decides which types you can choose from.
- Changing or translating a Quote answer. Copy the French words exactly as printed.
- Writing a full sentence where a Note answer needs one word or a number.

## Summary

- Paper 1: one task out of three, 250 to 400 words, with the text types the task offers.
- The blog, the informal e-mail and the speech are the most common Paper 1 types; social organization and sharing the planet are the most common themes.
- Reading: three texts, four question types, Quote answers copied exactly.
- Listening: three recordings, 25 marks.
- The counts are built from the papers of 2020 to 2025. They show patterns, not the 2026 paper.
