## Big idea

A diary entry is a private record in which the writer looks back at an event and thinks about what it meant. The only reader is the writer, so the text can be personal and honest.

## Explanation

A diary entry opens with a date and time line, then an optional address to the journal, such as « Cher journal, ». The paragraphs follow the order of time, and the entry ends with a reflection. There is no signature, because the writer is the only reader. The register is informal, personal and emotional: the writer uses je, and tu only when talking to themselves.

The tenses carry the meaning:

- Imparfait for the background: the weather, the setting, moods and habits, as in « Il pleuvait depuis le matin » or « J'étais très fatiguée ».
- Passé composé for the events, in order: « Nous sommes arrivés à la gare. », « J'ai présenté ma ville natale. »
- Plus-que-parfait for an event that happened before the main story: « J'avais préparé des phrases pendant des jours. »
- Présent for feelings now and for the reflection: « Aujourd'hui, je suis fière de moi. »
- Time markers such as ce matin (this morning), le lendemain (the next day), ensuite (then) and finalement (in the end).

A diary entry usually follows this layout:

```text
Mardi 14 avril, 22 h 15
Cher journal,
Setting: the day, the place and the mood (imparfait)
Events in order, one paragraph per stage (passé composé)
Reflection: what you feel now and what you learned (présent)
(no signature)
```

## Worked example

Three lines from the model entry:

- « Il faisait froid, j'étais très fatiguée et je me demandais si j'allais tenir une semaine entière avec une famille que je ne connaissais pas. » The imparfait sets the scene: the weather, the writer's mood and her doubts.
- « J'avais préparé des phrases pendant des jours, mais je les ai toutes oubliées. » The plus-que-parfait shows the earlier preparation. In the passé composé, « oubliées » agrees with the feminine plural « phrases », because the direct object « les » comes before the verb.
- « Finalement, j'ai compris : apprendre une langue, ce n'est pas seulement connaître des mots… » The time marker « Finalement » introduces the lesson.

## How it is examined

The diary task is worth 30 marks, with the same three criteria. Criterion A, Language (12 marks), rewards accurate imparfait, passé composé and plus-que-parfait, agreements after être and after a direct object, and time markers. Criterion B, Message (12 marks), rewards a clear setting, events in order, explained feelings and a stated lesson, and the diary form (date line, address, no signature). Criterion C, Conceptual understanding (6 marks), rewards a reflection on how the experience changed the writer's view of themselves or of others. Plan for about 40 minutes: 5 minutes to plan, 30 minutes to write and 5 minutes to check the past tenses.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the setting in the passé composé, so that the weather and the mood sound like events. Fix: use the imparfait for the background.
- Telling the events without order or time markers. Fix: one paragraph per stage, with le lendemain, ensuite or finalement.
- Ending with a list of events and no feelings. Fix: finish with a reflection in the présent: what you felt and what you learned.
- Forgetting the agreement after être. Fix: « Je suis rentrée » for a woman and « Je suis rentré » for a man.

## Summary

- A diary entry is private: a date and time line, optionally « Cher journal, », the events in order and a reflection.
- Use the imparfait for the setting, the weather, moods and habits.
- Use the passé composé for the events and the plus-que-parfait for what happened before them.
- Use the présent for feelings now and for what you have learned.
- Make the agreements after être and after a direct object placed before the verb.
- Finish with the reflection, and do not sign the entry.
