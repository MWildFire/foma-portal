## Big idea

A vector has both a size and a direction. Writing it in components lets you find its length, compare directions and rescale it to any length you need.

## Explanation

A <Term id="vector-magnitude-direction"/> is written as a column of components, $\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \\ v_3 \end{pmatrix}$, or in unit-vector form $\mathbf{v} = v_1\mathbf{i} + v_2\mathbf{j} + v_3\mathbf{k}$, where $\mathbf{i}$, $\mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ are the unit vectors along the axes. A scalar is described by a number (possibly signed), without a spatial direction.

The length (magnitude) of $\mathbf{v}$ is $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{v_1^{2} + v_2^{2} + v_3^{2}}$ (<Formula id="vector-length-3d"/>). In two dimensions, drop the $v_3$ term.

The <Term id="position-vector-from-origin"/> of a point P is $\overrightarrow{OP}$, so its components are the coordinates of P. The vector from A to B is found by subtracting position vectors: $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ (<Formula id="vector-ab-points"/>). Its magnitude is the distance AB.

Scalar multiples act component by component. The vector $k\mathbf{v}$ has magnitude $|k||\mathbf{v}|$. For nonzero $\mathbf{v}$, it points the same way when $k > 0$ and the opposite way when $k < 0$. The vector $-\mathbf{v}$ has the same length as $\mathbf{v}$ and the opposite direction. The zero vector $\mathbf{0} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has zero length and no direction.

A <Term id="unit-vector-in-direction"/> has magnitude 1. Divide a non-zero vector by its magnitude to get a unit vector in the same direction: $\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}/|\mathbf{v}|$ (<Formula id="unit-vector-normalise"/>). Multiply that unit vector by $L>0$ to get length $L$ in the same direction; $L=0$ gives the zero vector, with no direction.

The midpoint M of AB has position vector $\mathbf{m} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}}{2}$ (<Formula id="midpoint-position-vector"/>).

<Callout kind="define">Vector: a quantity with both magnitude and direction. Its magnitude is written $|\mathbf{v}|$; a unit vector has magnitude 1.</Callout>

<Callout kind="gdc">Create a vector as a one-column matrix with the `templates` key and the matrix icon, store it as `v` with `ctrl+var`, then type `norm(v)` for its length and `unitV(v)` for its unit vector. For a vector stored as a list, type `sqrt(dotP(u,u))` for the length.</Callout>

## Worked example

Let $A(1, -2, 4)$ and $B(5, 1, -2)$.

<Steps>
<Step>$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 - 1 \\ 1 - (-2) \\ -2 - 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}$.</Step>
<Step>$|\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{4^{2} + 3^{2} + (-6)^{2}} = \sqrt{61} = 7.810$ (to 3 decimal places), so the distance AB is 7.810 units.</Step>
<Step>The unit vector in the direction of AB is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{61}} \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.512 \\ 0.384 \\ -0.768 \end{pmatrix}$ (to 3 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why not a minus b?">Going from A to B means finishing at B and starting at A. The displacement is the end position vector minus the start position vector. The vector $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ points from B back to A.</Reveal>

## Visual

Sketch the triangle O, A, B with arrows from O to A and from O to B. The arrow from A to B closes the triangle, so $\mathbf{a} + \overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b}$, which gives $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$.

<Callout kind="tip">A quick three-arrow sketch shows why subtraction is correct before you calculate anything.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Subtracting in the wrong order: $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ points from B to A.</Callout>

- Dropping the square root in the magnitude: $\sqrt{61}$ is the length, not 61.
- Dividing a vector by one of its components to get a unit vector. Divide by the magnitude.
- Rounding early, for example using 7.8 instead of 7.810, which changes the third decimal place of the unit vector.

## Summary

- A vector has magnitude and direction. Write it as components, or as $v_1\mathbf{i} + v_2\mathbf{j} + v_3\mathbf{k}$.
- The magnitude is $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{v_1^{2} + v_2^{2} + v_3^{2}}$.
- $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$: the end position vector minus the start position vector.
- $k\mathbf{v}$ multiplies the length by $|k|$ and reverses the direction when $k < 0$; $-\mathbf{v}$ is the reverse of $\mathbf{v}$.
- For nonzero $\mathbf{v}$, a unit vector in its direction is $\mathbf{v}/|\mathbf{v}|$; the zero vector has no direction.
- The midpoint of AB has position vector $(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})/2$.
