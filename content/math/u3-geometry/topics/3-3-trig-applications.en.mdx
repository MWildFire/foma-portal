## Big idea

Many lengths and angles cannot be measured directly: the height of a tower, the distance to a boat, the slope of a ramp. Turn the words into a labelled diagram, find the triangle that contains the unknown, and pick the tool. Right-angled triangles use Pythagoras and SOH CAH TOA; other triangles use the sine rule or the cosine rule.

## Explanation

<Term id="line-of-sight"/>

Angles of elevation and depression are always measured from a horizontal line through the observer, not from the vertical. The line of sight is the straight line from the observer's eye to the object.

<Term id="angle-of-elevation"/>

<Term id="angle-of-depression"/>

Horizontal lines are parallel, so the angle of depression from one point equals the angle of elevation from the other point (alternate angles). Heights are vertical and ground distances are horizontal, so a height and a ground distance always meet at a right angle.

<Callout kind="define">Angle of elevation: measured upward from the horizontal to the line of sight to an object above. Angle of depression: measured downward from the horizontal to the line of sight to an object below.</Callout>

For a right-angled triangle with a height $h$, a horizontal distance $d$ and an angle $\theta$ at the observer, the tangent links them:

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}
$$

A method that works every time:

- Draw a labelled diagram from the words: heights vertical, distances on the ground horizontal, angles marked at the observer.
- Mark the horizontal lines and any right angles.
- Choose the triangle that contains the unknown and the known values.
- Calculate with full precision, round only at the end, and give units.

## Worked example

<Steps>
<Step>An observer stands on level ground 80 m from the foot of a vertical tower. The angle of elevation of the top of the tower is $28^{\circ}$. Let the height of the tower be $h$. Then $\tan 28^{\circ} = \dfrac{h}{80}$, so $h = 80\tan 28^{\circ} = 42.5$ m (1 decimal place).</Step>
</Steps>

<Steps>
<Step>A ladder of length 5 m leans against a vertical wall, with its foot 1.5 m from the wall. Let the height of the top of the ladder be $h$. Then $h^2 = 5^2 - 1.5^2 = 22.75$, so $h = 4.77$ m (3 significant figures).</Step>
<Step>The ladder makes an angle $\theta$ with the ground. Then $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1.5}{5} = 0.3$, so $\theta = 72.5^{\circ}$ (1 decimal place).</Step>
</Steps>

## Visual

Draw a horizontal line for the ground and a vertical line for the tower standing on it. The observer stands on the ground at a fixed distance from the foot. The line of sight rises from the observer's eye to the top of the tower, and the angle between the horizontal and that line is the angle of elevation. The right-angled triangle has the ground distance and the tower height as its two shorter sides, and the line of sight as its hypotenuse.

<Callout kind="tip">On a sketch, draw a horizontal line at both the observer and the object. The angle of depression at the top and the angle of elevation at the bottom are then equal, so one right-angled triangle is enough.</Callout>

## How it is examined

Find and Calculate questions give a method mark for a correct triangle and trigonometric ratio, a mark for the substitution, and an accuracy mark for the answer with units. Questions that ask you to draw a diagram or to show a method expect a labelled diagram and each step. Marks are lost when the angle is taken from the vertical, when the wrong side is called opposite, and when rounding happens too early. In a multi-part question, reuse the unrounded value from an earlier part.

<Callout kind="exam">State the angle you are using and why, for example "angle of depression = angle of elevation, by alternate angles". The reason earns the reasoning mark even if the calculation is wrong.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Measuring an angle of elevation from the vertical instead of from the horizontal.</Callout>

- Using the sine when the triangle needs the tangent, because the opposite and adjacent sides were confused.
- Treating a non-right triangle as right-angled and applying SOH CAH TOA directly.
- Reading an angle of depression as an angle inside the triangle at the object, without checking the alternate angle.

## Summary

- Begin with a labelled diagram: heights are vertical, ground distances are horizontal, and angles are measured from horizontal lines.
- The angle of elevation is measured upwards from the horizontal; the angle of depression is measured downwards.
- Alternate angles make the depression from one point equal to the elevation from the other.
- Right-angled triangles: use Pythagoras, SOH CAH TOA, or $\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{d}$.
- Non-right triangles: use the sine rule or the cosine rule.
- Round only at the end, and give units as the question asks.
