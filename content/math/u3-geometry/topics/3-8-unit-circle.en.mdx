## Big idea

The unit circle has radius 1, so the point at angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. This one picture gives the sign of sine, cosine and tangent in every quadrant, the identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, and the link $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. Trigonometric equations then have every solution in an interval, not only the one a calculator shows first.

## Explanation

Put the centre of the circle at the origin and measure $\theta$ anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis. The ray at angle $\theta$ meets the <Term id="unit-circle"/> at a point $P$. Because $OP = 1$, the coordinates of $P$ are $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$: the cosine is the $x$-coordinate and the sine is the $y$-coordinate.

<Callout kind="define">Unit circle: the circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. The point at angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.</Callout>

<Formula id="unit-circle-point"/>

Since $OP = 1$, Pythagoras' theorem gives $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, which is the identity for every angle:

$$
\sin^{2}\theta + \cos^{2}\theta = 1
$$

<Formula id="pythagorean-identity"/>

The point $P$ lies in one of four quadrants, and that fixes the sign of each ratio. <Term id="quadrant-sign-rule"/> In quadrant I all three ratios are positive; in II only sine; in III only tangent; in IV only cosine.

The tangent is the ratio of the two coordinates, so it is undefined when $\cos\theta = 0$, that is at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$:

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
$$

<Formula id="tan-identity"/>

Reflecting $P$ in the $y$-axis gives the angle $180^\circ - \theta$ with the same sine. This symmetry produces the <Term id="ambiguous-case-sine-rule"/>: when the sine rule gives $\sin B$, both $B$ and $180^\circ - B$ satisfy it, and each may or may not give a valid triangle.

<Formula id="sine-rule-ambiguous-case"/>

To solve a trigonometric equation in an interval, sketch both sides as functions of $x$ and find their intersection points inside the interval. For a value between $-1$ and $1$, sine or cosine takes that value twice in each full period, so an interval often holds several solutions.

## Worked example

<Steps>
<Step>Point at $\theta = 120^\circ$: on the unit circle, $\cos 120^\circ = -0.500$ and $\sin 120^\circ = 0.866$ (to 3 decimal places). So $\tan 120^\circ = \dfrac{0.866}{-0.500} = -1.732$ (to 3 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Given $\cos\theta = 0.6$ with $\theta$ in quadrant IV, $\sin^2\theta = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$. Sine is negative in quadrant IV, so $\sin\theta = -0.800$ (to 3 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Triangle with $a = 8$ cm, $b = 10$ cm and $A = 40^\circ$: $\sin B = \dfrac{10 \sin 40^\circ}{8} = 0.803$ (to 3 decimal places). So $B = 53.5^\circ$ or $B = 126.5^\circ$ (to 1 decimal place). Both are possible, because $40^\circ + 53.5^\circ < 180^\circ$ and $40^\circ + 126.5^\circ < 180^\circ$.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does a second angle appear?">Sine takes the same value at an angle and at its supplement. For $B = 53.5^\circ$, the supplement $180^\circ - 53.5^\circ = 126.5^\circ$ has the same sine, so both are candidates for angle $B$.</Reveal>

The tangent is $\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta$, so its magnitude can exceed 1 (Oxford AI HL, ch. 8.2, pp. 344–347). ## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Giving only the calculator value of $\arcsin$ or $\arccos$ and missing the second solution in the interval. For example, $\sin x = 0.5$ has $x = 30^\circ$ and $x = 150^\circ$ in $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$.</Callout>

- Choosing the wrong sign. In quadrant II $\sin\theta$ is positive, so $\cos\theta = -0.6$ gives $\sin\theta = +0.8$, not $-0.8$.
- Accepting the second triangle of the ambiguous case without checking that the angle sum is less than $180^\circ$.
- Writing $\tan 90^\circ = 0$. Since $\cos 90^\circ = 0$, the tangent is undefined there.

## Summary

- On the unit circle the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.
- $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ for every angle $\theta$.
- $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$, and it is undefined where $\cos\theta = 0$.
- Signs by quadrant: all positive in I, sine in II, tangent in III, cosine in IV.
- $\sin\theta = \sin(180^\circ - \theta)$, so the sine rule can give two triangles in the ambiguous case.
- For an equation on an interval, find every solution inside the interval and check each one.
