## Big idea

An arc is a fraction of a circle's circumference, and a sector is the same fraction of its area. That fraction is the central angle divided by 360°, so both the arc length and the sector area grow in proportion to the angle.

## Explanation

A circle of radius $r$ has circumference $2\pi r$ and area $\pi r^{2}$. An <Term id="arc-of-circle"/> is the part of the circumference between two of its points. A <Term id="sector-of-circle"/> is the region bounded by two radii and the arc between them. Both depend on the <Term id="central-angle"/> $\theta$, which is measured at the centre of the circle.

If $\theta$ is in degrees, the arc and the sector are the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the whole circle:

$$
l = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r, \qquad A = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^{2}
$$

## Worked example

A sector of a circle has radius 6 cm and central angle 50°.

<Steps>
<Step>Arc length: $l = \dfrac{50}{360} \times 2\pi \times 6 = \dfrac{5\pi}{3} = 5.24$ cm (3 s.f.).</Step>
<Step>Area of the sector: $A = \dfrac{50}{360} \times \pi \times 6^{2} = 5\pi = 15.7$ cm² (3 s.f.).</Step>
<Step>Radian check: 50 degrees is $\dfrac{5\pi}{18} \approx 0.873$ radians, so $l = r\theta = 6 \times \dfrac{5\pi}{18} = 5.24$ cm, the same as in step 1.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does the radian form have no 360?">In radians the whole circle is $2\pi$, so an angle $\theta$ is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}$ of it. The $2\pi$ cancels in the arc formula, which leaves $l = r\theta$.</Reveal>

## Visual

<Callout kind="tip">Sketch the circle, mark the centre O and the two radii, then shade the sector. A 120° arc is one third of the circumference, so it should look like one third of a full turn. A sector smaller than a semicircle must also have less than half the area of the circle.</Callout>

## How it is examined

State whether the central angle is in degrees or radians and use the matching formula. Show how you rearrange it when finding an angle or radius, retain full intermediate precision, and report the requested units and accuracy. Exact marks depend on the question.

<Callout kind="exam">Check the unit of the angle before you start. Degrees need the degree form, and radians need the radian form. Do not mix the two in one step.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using the full angle of 360° in place of the central angle, which gives the whole circumference and not the arc.</Callout>

- Using the diameter where the radius is needed. A sector area calculated with the diameter is four times too large.
- Writing $\pi r$ instead of $\pi r^{2}$ for the sector area.
- Putting an angle in degrees into the radian formula $l = r\theta$, which gives a value far too large.

## Summary

- An arc is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the circumference, and a sector is the same fraction of the area $\pi r^{2}$.
- In degrees: $l = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ and $A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^{2}$.
- In radians: $l = r\theta$ and $A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2} \theta$, as given in the formula booklet.
- Use the central angle, which is measured at the centre, not at the circumference.
- To find an angle or a radius, reverse the formula and keep full precision until the end.
- Check the angle unit first, and give answers with units and the required accuracy.
