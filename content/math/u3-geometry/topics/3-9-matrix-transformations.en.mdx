## Big idea

A 2 × 2 matrix moves every point of the plane by one fixed linear rule. Its determinant tells you two things: by what factor the transformation scales areas, and whether the transformation can be undone.

## Explanation

A point $(x, y)$ is written as a column. A <Term id="transform-matrix"/> $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ sends it to the image point $(x', y')$ by matrix multiplication:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
$$

<Formula id="transform-image-point"/>

Each of the matrices below keeps the origin O fixed. The standard matrices about O are:

- reflection in the x-axis: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$; reflection in the line $y = x$: $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$;
- stretch with scale factor $k$ parallel to the x-axis: $\begin{pmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$;
- enlargement with scale factor $k$ and centre O: $\begin{pmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{pmatrix}$;
- anticlockwise rotation through $\theta$ about O: $\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$ (<Formula id="transform-rotation-matrix"/>).

A translation moves the origin, so no 2 × 2 matrix can produce it. Write it as $\mathbf{x}' = M\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}$, where $\mathbf{t}$ is the translation vector.

If $T_1$ acts first with matrix $M_1$ and $T_2$ acts second with matrix $M_2$, the image of $\mathbf{x}$ is $M_2 M_1 \mathbf{x}$. So the matrix of the <Term id="transform-composite"/> is $M_2 M_1$. Matrix multiplication is not commutative, so $M_1 M_2$ is usually a different transformation.

For $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ the <Term id="transform-determinant"/> is $\det M = ad - bc$ (<Formula id="transform-det-2x2"/>). Every area is multiplied by $|\det M|$ (<Formula id="transform-area-scale"/>), so the determinant gives the area scale factor. Its sign shows the orientation: $\det M > 0$ keeps it (as for rotations and stretches), $\det M < 0$ reverses it, as in a mirror image. If $\det M = 0$, the transformation is <Term id="transform-singular"/>: the plane collapses onto a line or a point, and no inverse exists.

<Callout kind="define">Transformation matrix: a 2 × 2 matrix M such that the image of the point with position vector $\mathbf{x}$ is $M\mathbf{x}$. The area scale factor is $|\det M|$.</Callout>

## Worked example

Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and let triangle $ABC$ have vertices $A(1, 0)$, $B(3, 0)$ and $C(2, 1)$.

<Steps>
<Step>$\det M = 2(1) - (-1)(1) = 3$, so every area is multiplied by 3.</Step>
<Step>The image of $P(1, 2)$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 2(1) - 1(2) \\ 1(1) + 1(2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$, so $P'(0, 3)$.</Step>
<Step>Triangle $ABC$ has base 2 and height 1, so its area is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 1 = 1$ square unit. Its image has area $3 \times 1 = 3$ square units.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why take the absolute value?">The determinant can be negative, but an area cannot. A negative $\det M$ means the orientation is reversed, and the area is still multiplied by $|\det M|$.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

For Find, show the relevant working, such as a matrix product and the resulting coordinates. Write down usually asks for an answer read directly from the information; working need not be shown. For Show that, present the calculation leading to the stated result. When asked to describe a transformation fully, include the relevant axis, scale factor, centre or angle. Follow the exact question and its markscheme; the command alone does not prescribe an M1/A1/R1 split.

<Callout kind="exam">When the question says "first $T_1$, then $T_2$", write the matrix as $M_2 M_1$ before you multiply anything.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Multiplying in the wrong order: writing $M_1 M_2$ when $T_1$ acts first.</Callout>

- Using $\det M$ as the area scale factor instead of $|\det M|$. A negative determinant means reversed orientation, not negative area.
- Giving an enlargement with scale factor $k$ an area scale factor of $k$. The area scale factor is $k^{2}$.
- Writing a translation as a 2 × 2 matrix. The origin would stay fixed, so a translation vector must be added.

## Summary

- A 2 × 2 matrix $M$ sends the point $(x, y)$ to $M(x, y)$, written as a column; the origin stays fixed.
- Reflections, stretches, enlargements and rotations about O each have a standard matrix.
- A translation needs a vector added: $\mathbf{x}' = M\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}$.
- If $T_1$ acts first and $T_2$ second, the composite matrix is $M_2 M_1$, so the order matters.
- The area of the image is $|\det M|$ times the original area.
- $\det M > 0$ keeps orientation, $\det M < 0$ reverses it, and $\det M = 0$ collapses the plane onto a line (rank one) or the point $O$ (zero matrix), so no inverse exists.
- Use the GDC for determinants and inverses, and check the angle unit before using $\cos\theta$ or $\sin\theta$.
