## Big idea

A radian measures an angle by length. It is the angle at the centre of a circle that cuts off an arc as long as the radius, so arc lengths and sector areas need no extra constant.

## Explanation

Take a circle of radius $r$ and an arc of length $r$. The angle this arc subtends at the centre is one <Term id="radian-measure"/>. A full circumference of $2\pi r$ makes a full turn, so a full turn is $\dfrac{2\pi r}{r} = 2\pi$ radians. Therefore $360^\circ = 2\pi$ rad and $180^\circ = \pi$ rad.

<Callout kind="define">Radian: the angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc whose length equals the radius. One radian is about $57.3^\circ$.</Callout>

To convert between the two units, use $180^\circ = \pi$ rad:

$$
\theta_{\text{rad}} = \theta_{\text{deg}} \times \frac{\pi}{180}, \qquad \theta_{\text{deg}} = \theta_{\text{rad}} \times \frac{180}{\pi}
$$

<Formula id="radian-degree-conversion"/>

For radius $r>0$ and a sector angle $0\le\theta\le2\pi$ in radians, arc length and sector area are simple products (see <Term id="arc-length-in-radians"/> and <Term id="sector-area-in-radians"/>):

$$
s = r\theta, \qquad A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2} \theta
$$

## Worked example

Work in radians whenever a formula needs them.

<Steps>
<Step>Convert $150^\circ$ to radians: $150 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 2.618$ (to 3 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>A sector has radius 12 cm and central angle $60^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ rad. Its arc length is $s = 12 \times \dfrac{\pi}{3} = 4\pi = 12.566$ cm, and its area is $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 12^{2} \times \dfrac{\pi}{3} = 24\pi = 75.398$ cm$^2$ (both to 3 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Convert $1.5$ rad to degrees: $1.5 \times \dfrac{180}{\pi} = 85.9^\circ$ (to 1 decimal place).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why not put 60 into the arc formula?">Substituting 60 for $\theta$ gives a length about 57 times too large. The arc formula rests on $\theta = s/r$, which holds only when $\theta$ is in radians.</Reveal>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Substituting an angle in degrees into $s = r\theta$ or $A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2}\theta$. The answer is then about 57 times too large.</Callout>

- Converting in the wrong direction: $150 \times 180/\pi$ instead of $150 \times \pi/180$ gives an angle that is far too large.
- Writing the sector area as $\tfrac{1}{2} r\theta$ and forgetting to square the radius.
- Leaving the calculator in degree mode for an angle given in radians, or the reverse.

## Summary

- A radian is the angle at the centre subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius.
- A full turn is $2\pi$ rad, so $180^\circ = \pi$ rad and one radian is about $57.3^\circ$.
- Convert degrees to radians by multiplying by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$, and radians to degrees by multiplying by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$.
- The arc length $s = r\theta$ and the sector area $A = \tfrac{1}{2} r^{2}\theta$ hold only when $\theta$ is in radians.
- Give exact answers in terms of $\pi$ when they are asked for, and round only at the end.
- Check the calculator's angle mode before every trigonometric calculation.
