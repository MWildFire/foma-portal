## Big idea

Not every quantity follows a straight line or a simple exponential curve. A drug or a radioactive sample halves over a fixed time, learning slows as it nears a maximum, tides repeat, and some rules change at a threshold. Each shape has its own model. Choose the model from the shape of the data and the context, then fit its parameters.

## Explanation

Five families of extended models appear in this topic.

- Half-life. Exponential decay is written $N = N_0 e^{-kt}$ with $k > 0$. The <Term id="half-life-model"/> is the time for the amount to halve, $T = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k}$. It does not depend on the starting amount $N_0$.
- Natural logarithm model. $y = a + b\ln x$ for $x > 0$ and $b > 0$ rises quickly at first and then more slowly, because its gradient $\dfrac{b}{x}$ falls as $x$ grows. It suits diminishing returns.
- Sinusoidal model. $y = a\sin\big(b(x-c)\big) + d$ oscillates about the principal axis $y = d$. Its amplitude is $|a|$, its horizontal shift is $c$ and its period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$. The sine is taken in radians.
- Logistic model. The <Term id="logistic-model"/> $y = \dfrac{L}{1 + Ce^{-kx}}$ is S-shaped. $L$ is the <Term id="carrying-capacity"/>, the upper limit that $y$ approaches. <Formula id="logistic-formula"/>
- Piecewise model. A <Term id="piecewise-model"/> uses a different rule on each interval of its domain. At a shared boundary the two rules must give the same value, otherwise the model jumps there.

<Callout kind="define">Logistic model: a model $y = \dfrac{L}{1+Ce^{-kx}}$ whose graph is S-shaped and levels off at $L$.</Callout>

For a logistic model the key features follow from the formula. The starting value is $f(0) = \dfrac{L}{1+C}$. The inflection point, where growth changes from speeding up to slowing down, is at $x = \dfrac{\ln C}{k}$, and there $y = \dfrac{L}{2}$. Growth is fastest at that point, at the rate $\dfrac{kL}{4}$. <Formula id="logistic-key-features"/>

<Callout kind="gdc">For a logistic model, enter the data in two lists and use `Menu → Statistics → Stat Calculations → Logistic Regression (d=0)`, which fits $y = \dfrac{c}{1 + a e^{-bx}}$, so $L = c$, $C = a$ and $k = b$. To find when the model reaches a value such as $f(x) = 180$, use `Menu → Algebra → Numerical Solve` on a Calculator page, or graph $f$ and the line $y = 180$ and use `Menu → Analyze Graph → Intersection`.</Callout>

## Worked example

A radioactive tracer decays as $N = N_0 e^{-kt}$, where $N_0 = 80$ g, $k = 0.14$ per day and $t$ is in days.

<Steps>
<Step>Half-life: $T = \dfrac{\ln 2}{0.14} = 4.95$ days.</Step>
<Step>Amount after 10 days: $N(10) = 80e^{-1.4} = 19.7$ g.</Step>
</Steps>

A growth model is $y = \dfrac{100}{1 + 4e^{-0.5x}}$. Find its starting value, its inflection point and its greatest rate of growth.

<Steps>
<Step>Starting value: $y(0) = \dfrac{100}{1 + 4} = 20$.</Step>
<Step>Inflection point: $x = \dfrac{\ln 4}{0.5} = 2.77$, and there $y = 50$, which is half of $L = 100$.</Step>
<Step>Greatest rate of growth: $\dfrac{kL}{4} = \dfrac{0.5 \times 100}{4} = 12.5$ units of $y$ per unit of $x$.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why not read the starting value as L over C?">At $x = 0$ the denominator is $1 + C$, not $C$. Writing $\dfrac{L}{C}$ gives the wrong starting value.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

Questions on extended models use command terms such as Find, Show that, Sketch, Determine and Comment. Marks are given for choosing the model, for setting up the equation (M1), for the value to the required accuracy (A1) and for a reasoned comment about the context (R1). In a logistic question, saying that $L$ is the largest value the model can give often earns the reasoning mark.

<Callout kind="exam">Write the equation before you use the GDC, for example $f(x) = 180$ or $y = \dfrac{L}{2}$. A bare decimal without the equation can earn no method mark.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Treating the half-life as $k$. The half-life is $T = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k}$, so a larger $k$ gives a shorter half-life.</Callout>

- Reading the starting value of a logistic model as $\dfrac{L}{C}$. It is $\dfrac{L}{1+C}$.
- Using $b$ as the period of $a\sin\big(b(x-c)\big)+d$. The period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$.
- Finding the maximum of a sinusoid without the shift $c$, or not checking that the answer lies in the given interval.

## Summary

- Choose the model from the shape of the data and the context before you fit it.
- Exponential decay has half-life $T = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k}$.
- $y = a + b\ln x$ grows quickly and then slowly, with gradient $\dfrac{b}{x}$.
- $y = a\sin\big(b(x-c)\big)+d$ has amplitude $|a|$, period $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$ and principal axis $y = d$.
- The logistic model $\dfrac{L}{1+Ce^{-kx}}$ starts at $\dfrac{L}{1+C}$, has its inflection point at $x = \dfrac{\ln C}{k}$ where $y = \dfrac{L}{2}$, and grows fastest at $\dfrac{kL}{4}$.
- A piecewise model must agree at each boundary, and its domain must be stated.
