## Big idea

Each change to $y = f(x)$ moves, stretches or flips the graph in a predictable way. Every point $(x, y)$ of the original graph moves to a new point, and the equation tells you the rule for that move.

## Explanation

<Term id="function-graph-translation"/> keeps the shape and moves the graph. $y = f(x) + b$ moves every point up by $b$, and $y = f(x - a)$ moves every point $a$ units to the right. A negative value reverses the direction.

<Term id="function-graph-reflection"/> flips the graph over a line. $y = -f(x)$ reflects it in the $x$-axis, and $y = f(-x)$ reflects it in the $y$-axis.

<Formula id="function-graph-reflection-rules"/>

<Term id="function-graph-stretch"/> multiplies distances from an axis by a fixed factor. $y = p f(x)$ stretches the graph vertically by factor $p$, and $y = f(qx)$ stretches it horizontally by factor $\frac{1}{q}$. The factor is the reciprocal of the number multiplying $x$, so $y = f(2x)$ makes the graph half as wide.

<Formula id="function-graph-general-form"/>

<Callout kind="define">Transformation of a graph: a rule that sends each point $(x, y)$ of $y = f(x)$ to a new point. The transformed graph is the set of all the new points.</Callout>

Several transformations can be combined in one equation. Each point $(x, y)$ of $y = f(x)$ has an <Term id="function-graph-image-point"/> on $y = p\,f(q(x - a)) + b$: the $x$-coordinate is divided by $q$ and then $a$ is added, while the $y$-coordinate is multiplied by $p$ and then $b$ is added.

<Formula id="function-graph-image-rule"/>

<Callout kind="gdc">Draw the original and the transformed function on the same axes, and compare key points such as a maximum, a minimum or a zero. For the transformed function, a table of values gives the same $y$-values as the original at the image $x$-values, which checks the image rule.</Callout>

## Worked example

<Steps>
<Step>The point $(3, 8)$ lies on $y = f(x)$. For $y = 0.5f(x - 4) + 1$ the horizontal rule is $x \mapsto x + 4$, so the $x$-coordinate becomes $3 + 4 = 7$. The vertical rule is $y \mapsto 0.5y + 1$, so the $y$-coordinate becomes $0.5 \times 8 + 1 = 5$. The image is $(7, 5)$.</Step>
<Step>Factorise first: $3x + 3 = 3(x + 1)$, so the transformed curve is $y = f(3(x + 1))$ with $q = 3$ and $a = -1$. The $x$-coordinate of the image of $(3, 8)$ is $\frac{3}{3} + (-1) = 0$, and the $y$-coordinate stays $8$. The image is $(0, 8)$.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why factorising first matters">The shift is read from the bracket after the coefficient of $x$ has been factored out. Reading the shift straight from $3x + 3$ gives the wrong answer.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

Describe, Find and Write down questions reward the correct stretch factor, the correct direction of each translation, and the image of a named point. When a question asks for a sequence of transformations, state the stretch first and then the translation for a bracket $q(x - a)$, and give each step with its vector or scale factor. Show that questions may ask for the equation of the transformed graph, so practise writing $p f(q(x - a)) + b$ without losing the bracket.

<Callout kind="exam">The image rule gives a fast check: find the image of one key point of the original graph and make sure it lies on the transformed graph.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Reading $y = f(x + 2)$ as a shift 2 units to the right. The graph moves 2 units to the left.</Callout>

- Using $q$ instead of $\frac{1}{q}$ as the scale factor. $y = f(3x)$ compresses the graph horizontally to one third of its width.
- Translating by $qa$ when the bracket is $q(x - a)$. The shift is $a$; the form $f(qx - a)$ equals $f\bigl(q(x - \tfrac{a}{q})\bigr)$ and moves the graph by $\tfrac{a}{q}$.
- Dividing after the translation when finding an image: the $x$-coordinate is $a + \frac{x}{q}$, not $\frac{x + a}{q}$.

## Summary

- $y = f(x) + b$ moves the graph up by $b$; $y = f(x - a)$ moves it $a$ units to the right.
- $y = -f(x)$ reflects in the $x$-axis; $y = f(-x)$ reflects in the $y$-axis.
- $y = p f(x)$ stretches vertically by factor $p$; $y = f(qx)$ stretches horizontally by factor $\frac{1}{q}$.
- For $y = p f(q(x - a)) + b$ the point $(x, y)$ maps to $\left(a + \frac{x}{q}, py + b\right)$.
- Divide the $x$-coordinate by $q$ and then add $a$; never multiply the shift $a$ by $q$.
- Test each transformation on one key point, such as a maximum, a minimum or a zero.
