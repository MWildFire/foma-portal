## Big idea

A straight line is fixed by its gradient and one point on it. The gradient measures how steeply the line rises or falls, and each form of the equation shows a different feature of the line at a glance.

## Explanation

The <Term id="gradient-straight-line"/> is the rise divided by the run between any two points on the line:

$$
m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}, \qquad x_2 \neq x_1
$$

<Formula id="line-gradient-two-points"/>

A positive gradient means the line rises from left to right, a negative gradient means it falls, and $m = 0$ means the line is horizontal. A vertical line $x = k$ has no gradient.

The <Term id="gradient-intercept-form"/> is $y = mx + c$. Here $c$ is the y-intercept: the value of $y$ when $x = 0$, where the line crosses the y-axis. If a point $(x_1, y_1)$ on the line is known, the point–gradient form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ gives the equation directly.

<Formula id="line-gradient-intercept"/>

<Formula id="line-point-gradient"/>

<Callout kind="define">Gradient-intercept form: $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the y-intercept. The general form $ax + by + d = 0$, with $b \neq 0$, has gradient $-\dfrac{a}{b}$.</Callout>

The x-intercept is found by putting $y = 0$. For $y = mx + c$ with $m \neq 0$ this gives $x = -\dfrac{c}{m}$.

Two lines with equal gradients, $m_1 = m_2$, are parallel. <Term id="perpendicular-lines"/> meet at a right angle when $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$, which applies to two non-vertical lines, so the gradient of a perpendicular line is the negative reciprocal $-\dfrac{1}{m}$. A horizontal line is perpendicular to a vertical line.

## Worked example

A straight line passes through $P(-3, 4)$ and $Q(5, -2)$.

<Steps>
<Step>Gradient: $m = \dfrac{-2 - 4}{5 - (-3)} = -0.75$.</Step>
<Step>Use the point $Q(5, -2)$ in point–gradient form: $y + 2 = -0.75(x - 5)$, so $y = -0.75x + 1.75$. The y-intercept is $c = 1.75$.</Step>
<Step>The x-intercept: put $y = 0$, so $0 = -0.75x + 1.75$ and $x = 2.33$ (to 2 decimal places). A line perpendicular to it has gradient $-\dfrac{1}{-0.75} = 1.33$ (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does the x-intercept use division by the gradient?">Putting $y = 0$ in $y = mx + c$ gives $mx = -c$. Dividing by $m$ is allowed because $m \neq 0$. A horizontal line $y = c$ crosses the x-axis only when $c = 0$, and then it is the x-axis itself.</Reveal>

## Visual

Picture the line through $(0, 1.75)$ and $(2.33, 0)$. It starts on the y-axis at height 1.75 and slopes down: each step of 1 to the right drops the line by 0.75. A sketch needs only two points, so plot the intercepts, join them with a ruler, and check the gradient by counting rise over run between them.

<Callout kind="tip">Before you sketch, check the sign. A negative gradient means the line must fall from left to right, so confirm that direction first.</Callout>

## How it is examined

Find and Write down questions reward the correct form and the correct numbers. A question on two points usually gives one mark for the gradient, one for the equation and one for the requested value. A perpendicular question gives marks for the gradient of the given line, for the perpendicular gradient and for the final equation. In Show that and Determine questions, write out each substitution, so that a method mark can still be awarded after an arithmetic slip.

<Callout kind="exam">Rewrite the equation as $y = mx + c$ before you read off the gradient. The line $3x - 4y + 8 = 0$ has gradient $\dfrac{3}{4}$, not $-\dfrac{3}{4}$ and not $3$.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Taking the gradient of a perpendicular line as $-m$. The correct gradient is $-\dfrac{1}{m}$.</Callout>

- Inverting the gradient: writing $\dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1}$ instead of $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
- Dropping a sign with a negative coordinate: $5 - (-3)$ is $8$, not $2$.
- Reading the wrong intercept: $c$ is the y-intercept, and the x-intercept is $-\dfrac{c}{m}$.

## Summary

- The gradient is $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$: rise over run.
- Use $y = mx + c$, where $c$ is the y-intercept, or $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ when a point is given.
- Put $x = 0$ for the y-intercept and $y = 0$ for the x-intercept.
- Parallel lines have equal gradients: $m_1 = m_2$.
- Perpendicular lines satisfy $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\dfrac{1}{m}$.
- Give the answer in the form the question asks for, such as 2 decimal places.
