## Big idea

A graph shows every input–output pair of a function at once. A sketch shows only what matters — the shape, the intercepts and the turning points — and can be built from a formula, from a context, or from a graph you already know.

## Explanation

The <Term id="graph-points-of-function"/> is the set of points $(x, f(x))$ for $y = f(x)$. A curve is the graph of a function when every vertical line meets it at most once; this is the vertical line test.

A <Term id="sketch-key-features"/> need not be drawn to scale. Label what the question asks for: the intercepts with the axes, the coordinates of the maximum and minimum points, and any end of the domain. A context limits the domain, so a count of individual items is a nonnegative integer. Elapsed time from the stated start is nonnegative; a signed time coordinate may use another reference.

For a quadratic $y=ax^2+bx+c$ with $a\neq0$, the turning point has x-coordinate $-\dfrac{b}{2a}$, the axis of symmetry of the parabola.

<Formula id="quadratic-vertex-x-coordinate"/>

<Term id="translating-graph-vector"/> is done by replacing $x$ by $x - a$ and adding $b$. The graph $y = f(x - a) + b$ takes every point $(p, q)$ on $y = f(x)$ to $(p + a, q + b)$: $a$ units to the right and $b$ units up. If $(1, 3)$ lies on $y = f(x)$, then $(3, 2)$ lies on $y = f(x - 2) - 1$.

To sketch $y = f(x) + g(x)$, add the heights of $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$ at each $x$ belonging to both domains; where $g(x) = 0$ the sum equals $f(x)$. The <Term id="graph-sum-difference"/> is built this way, and the difference $y = f(x) - g(x)$ is found by subtracting heights.

## Worked example

Let $f(x) = x^2 - 4$ and $g(x) = 6 - 2x$, and let $h(x) = f(x) + g(x)$.

<Steps>
<Step>Add the two functions: $h(x) = x^2 - 4 + 6 - 2x = x^2 - 2x + 2$.</Step>
<Step>The turning point is at $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$, with $h(1) = 1$, so the minimum point is $(1, 1)$. The y-intercept is $h(0) = 2$, and $h(2) = 2$ by symmetry about $x = 1$.</Step>
<Step>The discriminant is $(-2)^2 - 4(1)(2) = -4$, which is negative, so $h(x) = 0$ has no solutions. The graph has no x-intercepts and lies entirely above the x-axis.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="How can I check the sketch?">Substitute a few values, such as $x = 0, 1, 2$, into $h(x)$ and plot them. The points $(0, 2)$, $(1, 1)$ and $(2, 2)$ must lie on your curve.</Reveal>

## Visual

Sketch $y = f(x)$, a parabola whose lowest point is at $x = 0$ and lies below the x-axis, and $y = g(x)$, a straight line falling from left to right. At each $x$ the height of the sum is the height of $f$ plus the height of $g$. Where $g$ is above the x-axis the sum is higher than $f$, and where $g$ is below it the sum is lower. Opposite signs alone do not determine the sign of a sum: $-4+3=-1$ while $-4+6=2$. In this example, completing the square gives $h(x)=(x-1)^2+1>0$ for every real $x$; that is why the sum has no zeros.

<Callout kind="tip">Mark the key points first, the intercepts and turning points, then add a few heights from a table of values and join the points with a smooth curve.</Callout>

## How it is examined

Sketch, State and Write down questions reward labelled key features more than a perfect curve. Label the requested coordinates and features, and show the combined expression when working with a sum or difference. The precise allocation of marks depends on the question.

<Callout kind="exam">Label the axes and state the coordinates of every feature the question names. A point that is only drawn, without its coordinates, may not earn the mark.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Shifting the graph the wrong way: $y = f(x - 2)$ moves the graph 2 units to the right, not to the left.</Callout>

- Adding x-coordinates instead of heights when forming $f(x) + g(x)$.
- Sketching the curve from two or three points and missing a turning point or an x-intercept.
- Ignoring the context: drawing a profit curve for a negative number of items, or a time before the start.

## Summary

- The graph of $y = f(x)$ is the set of points $(x, f(x))$, and a function's graph passes the vertical line test.
- A sketch shows labelled key features: intercepts, turning points and the ends of the domain.
- For $a\neq0$, the turning point of $y=ax^2+bx+c$ has x-coordinate $-\dfrac{b}{2a}$.
- $y = f(x - a) + b$ moves the graph $a$ units right and $b$ units up.
- The graph of $f(x) + g(x)$ is found by adding the heights of $f$ and $g$ at each $x$.
- Use the context to limit the domain before reading off key values.
