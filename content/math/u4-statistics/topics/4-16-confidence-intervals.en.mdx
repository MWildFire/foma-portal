## Big idea

A confidence interval turns a sample mean into a range of plausible values for the population mean $\mu$. Its width comes from the standard error and a critical value, which depend on the confidence level and on whether $\sigma$ is known. The confidence level describes the method: a 95% interval comes from a method that captures $\mu$ in 95% of repeated samples.

## Explanation

Assume independent identically distributed observations, $n>1$ for a t interval, and a population with positive finite variance. Exact z and t coverage below uses a normal population; large-sample approximations need separate justification.

When the population standard deviation $\sigma$ is known and the population is normal, a <Term id="confidence-interval-mean"/> for $\mu$ has the z form. The critical value $z^{*}$ leaves the tail area that matches the confidence level (for 95%, 2.5% in each tail), and the standard error is $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$:

## Worked example

A machine fills cans of cola. The volume of a can, in ml, is normally distributed with known standard deviation $\sigma = 4.5$ ml. A random sample of $n = 36$ cans has mean $\bar{x} = 329.6$ ml. Find a 95% confidence interval for $\mu$.

<Steps>
<Step>The standard error is $\sigma/\sqrt{n} = 4.5/\sqrt{36} = 0.75$ ml, and $z^{*} = 1.960$ (to 3 decimal places) for 95%.</Step>
<Step>The margin of error is $E = 1.960 \times 0.75 = 1.47$ ml (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>The 95% confidence interval is $(329.6 - 1.47,\ 329.6 + 1.47) = (328.13,\ 331.07)$ ml.</Step>
</Steps>

A random sample of $n = 12$ tomatoes from a large farm has masses that are normally distributed with unknown $\sigma$. The sample mean is $\bar{x} = 58.3$ g and the sample standard deviation with divisor $n-1$ is $s_{n-1} = 4.3$ g. Find a 95% confidence interval for $\mu$ and interpret it.

<Steps>
<Step>Use the t-distribution with $\nu = 11$ degrees of freedom: $t^{*} = 2.201$ (to 3 decimal places). The standard error is $s_{n-1}/\sqrt{n} = 4.3/\sqrt{12} = 1.241$ (to 3 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>The margin of error is $E = 2.201 \times 1.241 = 2.73$ g (to 2 decimal places), so the 95% confidence interval is $(58.3 - 2.73,\ 58.3 + 2.73) = (55.57,\ 61.03)$ g.</Step>
<Step>Interpretation: we are 95% confident that the mean mass of tomatoes from this farm lies between 55.57 g and 61.03 g. The method captures the mean in 95% of repeated samples; it says nothing about the mass of one particular tomato.</Step>
</Steps>

## Visual

<Callout kind="tip">Draw the interval as a bar centred on $\bar{x}$, with the margin of error on each side. Holding spread and other settings fixed, a larger sample or a lower confidence level makes the bar shorter; a higher confidence level makes it longer. The bar moves from sample to sample because $\bar{x}$ changes, while $\mu$ stays fixed.</Callout>

## How it is examined

Name z or t and justify the model, known or estimated SD and independence. For t, state n−1 degrees of freedom. Give both interval endpoints at the requested accuracy and interpret the population mean in context. For sample-size planning, solve the margin inequality and round upwards.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using $z^{*}$ when $\sigma$ is unknown: the interval comes out too narrow. Use $t^{*}$ with $n-1$ degrees of freedom and $s_{n-1}$.</Callout>

- Writing "there is a 95% probability that $\mu$ lies between $a$ and $b$". The interval is random and $\mu$ is fixed.
- Giving only the margin of error (plus or minus 1.47) when the question asks for an interval, or forgetting to divide $\sigma$ by $\sqrt{n}$.
- Entering the population standard deviation (divisor $n$) into the GDC where the sample value with divisor $n-1$ is needed.

## Summary

- A confidence interval for $\mu$ is the sample mean plus or minus a critical value times the standard error.
- With $\sigma$ known, use $z^{*}$ and $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$; with $\sigma$ unknown, use $t^{*}$ with $n-1$ degrees of freedom and $s_{n-1}/\sqrt{n}$.
- A 95% level means the method captures $\mu$ in 95% of repeated samples, not that one interval has a 95% chance.
- Holding other quantities fixed, higher confidence widens the interval and larger sample size narrows it.
- Interpret the interval in context: we are 95% confident that the mean lies between $a$ and $b$.
- To keep the margin of error at most $E$ with known $\sigma$, use $n \geq (z^{*}\sigma/E)^{2}$.
