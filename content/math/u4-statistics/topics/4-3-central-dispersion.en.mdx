## Big idea

One average hides how far the values spread. Central tendency (mean, median, mode) says where the data centre; dispersion (range, interquartile range, standard deviation) says how tightly they cluster. Choose the pair that suits the data, and be especially careful when there are extreme values.

## Explanation

The mean, the median and the mode measure the centre of a data set. The mean uses every value, so one extreme value moves it; the median is the middle value of the ordered data (or the average of the two middle values for even n). It depends mainly on order and is generally less sensitive to extreme magnitudes than the mean; it is not guaranteed unchanged. For grouped data, the <Term id="estimated-mean-grouped"/> uses the class midpoints and frequencies: <Formula id="grouped-mean-estimate"/>

With equal-width classes, the <Term id="modal-class"/> has the highest frequency. For unequal widths, use frequency density to identify the histogram peak; the class with the most observations and the class with the highest density need not be the same. Follow the quantity requested in the question.

The range depends on two values only. The <Term id="interquartile-range"/> is the spread of the middle half of the data: <Formula id="iqr-formula"/> The <Term id="standard-deviation"/> measures the typical distance of the values from the mean and uses every value: <Formula id="sd-formula"/>

<Callout kind="define">Standard deviation: the square root of the mean of the squared deviations from the mean, measured in the same units as the data.</Callout>

If each value $x$ is replaced by $y = ax + b$, the mean becomes $a\bar{x} + b$ and the standard deviation becomes $|a|\sigma$. Adding $b$ moves the mean but does not change the spread. <Formula id="linear-change-effect"/>

<Callout kind="gdc">Enter the values in a Lists & Spreadsheet column, then on a Calculator page choose `Menu → Statistics → Stat Calculations → One-Variable Statistics` and read the mean `x̄`, the median `MedianX`, the quartiles `Q1X` and `Q3X`, and the standard deviation. For grouped data, enter the class midpoints in one column and the frequencies in another, and use the second as the Frequency List. `σx` divides by $n$ and is the one for a complete data set; `sx` divides by $n-1$.</Callout>

## Worked example

The weekly study hours of 8 students are: 2, 4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6, 9.

<Steps>
<Step>Mean: the sum of the values is 42, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{42}{8} = 5.25$ hours. Median: the middle two values are 5 and 6, so the median is $\dfrac{5 + 6}{2} = 5.5$ hours. Mode: 6 hours, which occurs three times.</Step>
<Step>Standard deviation: the squared deviations from the mean add up to $29.5$, so $\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{29.5}{8}} = 1.92$ hours (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Quartiles: the lower half 2, 4, 4, 5 has median 4 and the upper half 6, 6, 6, 9 has median 6, so the interquartile range is $6 - 4 = 2$ hours.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does one extreme value move the mean but barely move the median?">Adding a value of 30 to these eight values pulls the mean well above 5.25. The median only shifts to the next value in the ordered list, because it depends on position, not on size.</Reveal>

## Visual

Sketch the values as dots on a number line and mark the mean as a balance point. An extreme dot pulls the mean towards it, while the median and the interquartile range hardly move.

<Callout kind="tip">Mark the mean, the median and the quartiles on the same sketch. A mean–median difference may reflect skewness or unusual values; inspect the data and the purpose of the summary. It does not prove the presence of an outlier or make the median universally preferable.</Callout>

## How it is examined

For Calculate, distinguish raw values from class midpoints, show frequency weights and choose the requested SD divisor. For Comment, compare mean and median or SD and IQR using the actual distribution. Identify an outlier using the stated rule; an isolated large value is not automatically erroneous.

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Estimating the mean of grouped data with the class boundaries instead of the class midpoints.</Callout>

- Dividing by $n - 1$ for the standard deviation of a complete data set; that version is for estimating a population from a sample.
- Quoting the variance as the standard deviation, or forgetting the square root.
- Multiplying the standard deviation by $b$ when the constant $b$ is added; adding a constant leaves the spread unchanged.

## Summary

- The mean uses every value; the median and IQR are generally less sensitive to extreme magnitudes, but their values can change with ranks, sample size and quartile convention.
- For grouped data, estimate the mean with the class midpoints and the frequencies.
- For equal-width classes the modal class has the highest frequency. With unequal widths distinguish highest frequency from the histogram peak by density.
- The standard deviation is the square root of the mean squared deviation from the mean, in the units of the data.
- The interquartile range is $Q_3 - Q_1$ and measures the spread of the middle half of the data.
- If $y = ax + b$, then $\bar{y} = a\bar{x} + b$ and $\sigma_y = |a|\sigma_x$; adding a constant does not change the spread.
