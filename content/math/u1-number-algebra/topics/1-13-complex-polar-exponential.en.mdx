## Big idea

Polar and exponential forms describe a complex number by its distance from the origin and its angle. In these forms, multiplication becomes stretching and turning, so powers and sums of sinusoids of the same frequency become simple.

## Explanation

Unless the question specifies degrees, give the principal argument in radians, with $-\pi<\arg z\leq\pi$ (Oxford AI HL, ch. 8.4, p. 357). The argument of zero is undefined. The argument-based forms and operations below use nonzero complex numbers. Angles differing by $2\pi k$, integer $k$, represent the same direction; reduce the final angle to $(-\pi,\pi]$ when a principal argument is requested.

The <Term id="modulus-argument-form"/> of a complex number is $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$, written $r\,\text{cis}\,\theta$, where $r = |z|$ and $\theta = \arg z$.

<Formula id="complex-polar-form"/>

The <Term id="complex-argument"/> must lie in the right quadrant. When $a < 0$, the arctan of $b/a$ lands in the wrong half-plane, so add $\pi$ for a point in the second quadrant and subtract $\pi$ for one in the third.

The <Term id="exponential-form"/> is $z = re^{i\theta}$, with $\theta$ in radians. It follows from Euler’s relation $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$, and it is the most compact form for products and powers.

<Formula id="complex-exponential-form"/>

<Callout kind="define">cis θ is shorthand for cos θ + i sin θ, so r cis θ is the modulus–argument form of the same number.</Callout>

To multiply, multiply the moduli and add the arguments. To divide by a nonzero number, divide the moduli and subtract the arguments. For an integer power $n$ of a nonzero number, raise the modulus to the power $n$ and multiply the argument by $n$. This rule is <Term id="de-moivre-theorem"/>:

<Formula id="complex-de-moivre"/>

Adding sinusoids of the same frequency uses the complex number $a - bi$. When $(a,b)\neq(0,0)$, its modulus is the amplitude $R$ and its argument is $-\varphi$ modulo $2\pi$, so $a\cos\omega t + b\sin\omega t = R\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$:

<Formula id="sinusoid-sum-equal-frequency"/>

If $a=b=0$, the signal is identically zero: $R=0$ and the phase is not determined. For a nonzero signal, choose the phase using both coefficient signs, not just $\arctan(b/a)$.

Geometrically, multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ rotates a point anticlockwise about the origin through $\theta$, and multiplying by a positive real number scales its distance from the origin (contracts for a factor below 1).

## Worked example

Let $z = -2 + 2i$.

<Steps>
<Step>Modulus and argument: $r = |z| = 2.83$ and $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = 2.356$ radians (to 3 significant figures and 3 decimal places), so $z = 2\sqrt{2}\,e^{3\pi i/4} \approx 2.83\,e^{2.356i}$.</Step>
<Step>Fourth power: $z^{4} = r^{4}e^{4i\theta} = 64\,e^{3\pi i} = -64$, using $r^{4} = 64$.</Step>
<Step>Sum of sinusoids: $6\cos\omega t + 8\sin\omega t = R\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$ with $R = \sqrt{6^{2}+8^{2}} = 10.0$ and $\varphi = \arctan(8/6) = 0.927$ radians (3 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

## How it is examined

Find and Express questions award marks for the modulus, for the argument with the correct quadrant, and for the form requested. When a question asks for a sum of sinusoids, give $R$ and $\varphi$ as separate answers, with $\varphi$ in the unit the question asks for. Polar form often simplifies powers and quotients; show a valid method and follow any method or form specified in the question.

<Callout kind="exam">Check the unit of $\theta$. The exponential form $re^{i\theta}$ takes $\theta$ in radians; if a question asks for degrees, say so in the answer.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Writing $|z^{3}| = 3|z|$ instead of $|z|^{3}$. The modulus is raised to the power, and the argument is multiplied by the power.</Callout>

- Taking $\arg z = \arctan(b/a)$ without checking the quadrant, so that $-2 + 2i$ is given the angle $-\pi/4$ instead of $3\pi/4$.
- Mixing degrees and radians, for example writing $e^{30i}$ when the angle is 30 degrees.
- Writing $R = a + b$ for the amplitude of $a\cos\omega t + b\sin\omega t$, instead of $R = \sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2}}$.

## Summary

- $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) = r\,\text{cis}\,\theta = re^{i\theta}$, with $r = |z|$ and $\theta$ in radians in the exponential form.
- Check the quadrant of $\theta$: after an arctan, add or subtract $\pi$ for points in the second or third quadrant.
- Multiply moduli and add arguments; divide moduli and subtract arguments.
- de Moivre: $(r\,\text{cis}\,\theta)^{n} = r^{n}\,\text{cis}\,(n\theta)$ for an integer $n$ and a nonzero base.
- $a\cos\omega t + b\sin\omega t = R\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$, with $R = \sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$, $R\cos\varphi = a$ and $R\sin\varphi = b$.
- Multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ rotates the Argand diagram anticlockwise through $\theta$.
