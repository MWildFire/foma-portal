## Big idea

A loan is repaid by equal payments: each payment covers the interest for that period, and the rest reduces the balance. Savings made by equal regular deposits form an annuity. Both use the same compound factor, so one rate per period and one number of periods describe either case.

## Worked example

A loan of AED 60,000 is repaid by 60 equal monthly instalments. The interest rate is 6% a year, compounded monthly. Find the instalment, the interest and balance after the first payment, and the balance after 24 payments.

<Steps>
<Step>Rate per month $i = \dfrac{6}{1200}$ and $N = 60$. The instalment is $M = \dfrac{60000\,i}{1-(1+i)^{-60}} = 1159.97$ (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Interest in the first month is $60000 \times \frac{6}{1200} = 300.00$. The rest of the first instalment repays $1159.97 - 300.00 = 859.97$, so the balance after one payment is $60000 - 859.97 = 59140.03$.</Step>
<Step>After 24 instalments the balance is $B_{24} = 60000(1+i)^{24} - 1159.97 \times \dfrac{(1+i)^{24}-1}{i} = 38129.33$ (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does the balance fall slowly at first?">In the early months most of each instalment pays interest on a large balance, so only a small part of it reduces the debt. Later instalments reduce the balance faster, because less interest is charged each month.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

Find and Calculate questions give a mark for the rate per period and the number of periods, a mark for the method (the instalment, the balance or the future value), and an accuracy mark for the rounding asked. Comment questions ask how the balance or the share of interest changes over time, and expect a reason based on the interest charged on the balance.

<Callout kind="exam">Before you use the solver, say whether you are finding an instalment, a balance or a future value. The number of periods and the sign of the payment depend on that choice.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using the annual rate with monthly instalments. The rate per month is 6 divided by 12 per cent, and the number of instalments is 12 times the number of years.</Callout>

- Subtracting the instalment from the balance before adding that period's interest. Interest is charged on the balance first.
- Using the savings formula, where deposits build up, for a loan being repaid, or the reverse.
- Rounding the instalment to a whole number too early. Keep full accuracy in the working.

## Summary

- An amortised loan has equal instalments; each one pays that period's interest first and reduces the balance by the rest.
- Match the rate per period $i = \frac{r}{100k}$ and the number of periods $N = kn$ to the payment frequency.
- The instalment is $M = \dfrac{PV\,i}{1-(1+i)^{-N}}$, and the balance after $j$ payments is $B_j = PV(1+i)^j - M\dfrac{(1+i)^j-1}{i}$.
- A savings annuity with deposits $M$ has future value $FV = M\dfrac{(1+i)^N-1}{i}$.
- Balances fall slowly at first because most of each early payment is interest.
