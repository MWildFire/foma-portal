## Big idea

In this model a loan is repaid by equal payments: with positive interest and a fully amortising payment, each payment covers that period’s interest and reduces the balance by the rest. Savings made by equal regular deposits form an annuity. Both use the same compound factor, so one rate per period and one number of periods describe either case.

## Explanation

An <Term id="amortisation-loan"/> repays a loan with equal instalments over a fixed term. Each instalment first pays the interest on the balance for that period, and the rest reduces the balance. For nominal annual rate $r\%$ compounded $k$ times a year, with a payment at each period end, use the decimal rate $i = \frac{r}{100k}$ and $N = kn$ whole periods over $n$ years. These formulas use a constant rate and matching compounding/payment periods. At zero interest, divide the loan by $N$ for the payment and use $MN$ for savings. ## Worked example

A loan of AED 60,000 is repaid by 60 equal monthly instalments. The interest rate is 6% a year, compounded monthly. Find the instalment, the interest and balance after the first payment, and the balance after 24 payments.

<Steps>
<Step>Rate per month $i = \dfrac{6}{1200}$ and $N = 60$. The instalment is $M = \dfrac{60000\,i}{1-(1+i)^{-60}} = 1159.97$ (to 2 decimal places).</Step>
<Step>Interest in the first month is $60000 \times \frac{6}{1200} = 300.00$. The rest of the first instalment repays $1159.97 - 300.00 = 859.97$, so the balance after one payment is $60000 - 859.97 = 59140.03$.</Step>
<Step>Retain the unrounded payment $M = 1159.9680917657\ldots$ in the algebra: $B_{24} = 60000(1+i)^{24} - M\dfrac{(1+i)^{24}-1}{i} = 38129.33$ to 2 decimal places. If the actual monthly payment is rounded to 1159.97 instead, the balance is 38129.28. State which convention is used.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does the balance fall slowly at first?">For a fully amortising fixed-rate loan, interest takes a larger share of an early payment than of a later one. It need not be most of the payment: here the first interest charge is 300 out of about 1159.97 (25.9%). As the balance falls, interest falls and more of the equal payment reduces principal.</Reveal>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using the annual rate with monthly instalments. The rate per month is 6 divided by 12 per cent, and the number of instalments is 12 times the number of years.</Callout>

- Subtracting the instalment from the balance before adding that period's interest. Interest is charged on the balance first.
- Using the savings formula, where deposits build up, for a loan being repaid, or the reverse.
- Rounding the instalment to a whole number too early. Keep full accuracy in the working.

## Summary

- The fully amortising model here uses equal instalments; each one pays that period's interest first and reduces the balance by the rest.
- Match the rate per period $i = \frac{r}{100k}$ and the number of periods $N = kn$ to the payment frequency.
- The instalment is $M = \dfrac{PV\,i}{1-(1+i)^{-N}}$, and the balance after $j$ payments is $B_j = PV(1+i)^j - M\dfrac{(1+i)^j-1}{i}$.
- A savings annuity with deposits $M$ has future value $FV = M\dfrac{(1+i)^N-1}{i}$.
- On this fully amortising fixed-rate model the interest share falls over time; it is not necessarily a majority even in the first payment.
