## Big idea

Powers and logarithms are two sides of one idea. A power with an integer exponent follows a few simple laws, and a logarithm reads the exponent back out of a power: if $10^{y}=x$, then $y=\log_{10}x$.

## Explanation

Integer exponents follow the same laws for every power of one non-zero base $a$. <Term id="exponent-laws-integer"/> let you multiply, divide and raise powers by working only with the exponents.

<Formula id="exponent-zero-negative"/>

A zero exponent gives 1, and a negative exponent gives a reciprocal. So $5^{0}=1$ and $4^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{16}$.

A logarithm is an exponent. For $x>0$, <Term id="logarithm-base-10"/> is written $\log_{10}x$, and <Term id="logarithm-base-e"/> is written $\ln x$, so $\ln x=\log_{e}x$.

<Callout kind="define">Logarithm base 10: for $x>0$, $\log_{10}x=y$ means $10^{y}=x$. Natural logarithm: for $x>0$, $\ln x=y$ means $e^{y}=x$.</Callout>

Each logarithm undoes its power:

<Formula id="log-inverse-10-e"/>

So if $10^{y}=250$, then $y=\log_{10}250$, and if $e^{y}=20$, then $y=\ln 20$.

<Callout kind="gdc">On a Calculator page, `log(250)` gives $\log_{10}250$, `ln(20)` gives $\ln 20$ (`ctrl` then `eˣ` types `ln(`), and `log(8,2)` gives $\log_{2}8$, because the base is the second argument. Enter the whole argument, keep the full value during the working, and round only at the end. Check that the base in your command is the base the question names.</Callout>

## Worked example

Apply the laws of exponents, then use the logarithm functions to solve two equations.

<Steps>
<Step>Use the laws of exponents on the exponents: $\dfrac{2^{5}\times 2^{-3}}{2^{-1}}=2^{5+(-3)-(-1)}=2^{3}=8$.</Step>
<Step>Evaluate with technology: $\log_{10}250=2.398$ (3 decimal places). The exact exponent is $\log_{10}250$; 2.398 is its rounded approximation, so $10^{2.398}$ is only approximately 250.</Step>
<Step>Solve $e^{y}=20$ for $y$: $y=\ln 20=2.996$ (3 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why take ln of both sides?">Taking the natural logarithm of both sides of $e^{y}=20$ removes the exponential, because $\ln(e^{y})=y$. The exact answer is $y=\ln 20$, and you round it only at the end.</Reveal>

## Visual

<Callout kind="tip">Sketch $y=10^{x}$ and $y=\log_{10}x$ on the same axes, with the line $y=x$ for reference. The two curves are reflections of each other in the line $y=x$: the point $(0,1)$ on the first curve becomes the point $(1,0)$ on the second. The same picture works for $e^{x}$ and $\ln x$.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Treating $2^{3}+2^{4}$ as $2^{7}$. Exponents add only when powers are multiplied.</Callout>

- Writing $4^{-2}$ as $-16$ instead of $\dfrac{1}{16}$.
- Using $\log_{10}$ when the question asks for $\ln$, or the other way round.
- Rounding a value too early and then using the rounded value in the next step.

## Summary

- For nonzero $a$ and integer $m,n$, the exponent laws are: $a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}$, $a^{m}\div a^{n}=a^{m-n}$ and $(a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}$.
- For $a\neq 0$: $a^{0}=1$ and $a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^{n}}$.
- For $x>0$: $\log_{10}x=y$ means $10^{y}=x$, and $\ln x=y$ means $e^{y}=x$.
- A logarithm undoes its power: $10^{\log_{10}x}=x$ and $e^{\ln x}=x$.
- Evaluate $\log_{10}$ and $\ln$ with technology, and round only at the end.
