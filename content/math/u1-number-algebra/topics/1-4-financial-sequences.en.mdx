## Big idea

Compound interest and reducing-balance depreciation are both geometric sequences. Each period the amount is multiplied by the same factor, so the whole method is to find that factor and count the periods.

## Explanation

When interest is added to a balance, the next period's interest is earned on the interest as well. For a positive principal and a positive fixed rate per period, <Term id="compound-interest"/> gives a larger balance than simple interest at the same periodic rate after more than one period; after one period they agree. Here $r\%$ is a nominal annual rate compounded $k$ times per year, with no fees or additional cash flows. For principal $PV$, after $n$ years (a whole number $kn$ of periods), the value is:

<Formula id="compound-interest-periods"/>

Each <Term id="compounding-period"/> uses the rate $\frac{r}{k}\%$, and there are $kn$ periods in $n$ years. Dividing by $k$ applies to this nominal-rate convention. An effective annual rate $j\%$ instead gives the periodic rate $(1+j/100)^{1/k}-1$. Annual compounding is the case $k = 1$. Monthly compounding is $k = 12$, which gives $12n$ periods at $\frac{r}{12}\%$ each.

<Callout kind="define">Compound interest: interest added to the balance at the end of each period, so that the balance is multiplied by a fixed growth factor $1 + \frac{r}{100k}$ in each period.</Callout>

Depreciation can also be a geometric sequence. In <Term id="reducing-balance-depreciation"/>, the value falls by a fixed percentage of its value at the start of each year. For $V_0>0$ and $0<r<100$, if $r\%$ is lost each year, the value after $n$ years is:

## Worked example

A deposit of AED 20,000 earns 3.5% a year, compounded annually. A car bought for AED 85,000 loses 12% of its value each year on a reducing-balance basis.

<Steps>
<Step>Compound interest, annual: $FV = 20000 \times 1.035^{5} = 23\,753.73$ (to 2 decimal places). The common ratio is 1.035 and there are 5 periods, so the deposit is worth AED 23,753.73.</Step>
<Step>Depreciation: $V_4 = 85000 \times 0.88^{4} = 50\,974.11$ (to 2 decimal places). The car keeps 88% of its value each year, so after 4 years it is worth AED 50,974.11.</Step>
<Step>First year with the car below AED 40,000: after 5 years the value is $85000 \times 0.88^{5} = 44\,857.21$, and after 6 years it is $85000 \times 0.88^{6} = 39\,474.35$. The value first drops below AED 40,000 in year 6, so the least value of $n$ is 6.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why is the factor less than 1?">The value goes down each year, so the factor is one minus the percentage lost. Using one plus the percentage would model growth, not depreciation.</Reveal>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using the annual rate in a monthly calculation. At 4% a year, the rate per month is 4 divided by 12 per cent, and the number of periods is 12 times the number of years.</Callout>

- Using $\frac{r}{100}$ as the growth factor instead of $1 + \frac{r}{100}$, or using $1 + \frac{r}{100}$ for depreciation instead of $1 - \frac{r}{100}$.
- Treating reducing-balance depreciation as a fixed amount lost each year. The loss shrinks each year, because it is a percentage of a smaller value.
- Rounding the common ratio early, which moves the final answer.

## Summary

- Compound interest multiplies the balance by $1 + \frac{r}{100k}$ in each period, and there are $kn$ periods in $n$ years.
- Reducing-balance depreciation multiplies the value by $1 - \frac{r}{100}$ each year, so the yearly values form a geometric sequence.
- To find an unknown rate, solve for the common ratio, then take the required root.
- Match the rate and the number of periods to the compounding frequency: for monthly interest, divide the rate by 12 and multiply the years by 12.
- Round only the final answer, and give it in the form the question asks for.
- Check a result with a financial solver or a table of values on the GDC.
