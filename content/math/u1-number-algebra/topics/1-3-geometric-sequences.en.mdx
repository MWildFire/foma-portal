## Big idea

A geometric sequence multiplies by the same factor at every step. For a positive first term, that factor, the common ratio, describes growth when it is above 1 and decay when it is between 0 and 1. The nth term and the sum of the first n terms follow from two formulas.

## Explanation

A <Term id="geometric-sequence"/> multiplies by the same non-zero number at each step, the <Term id="common-ratio"/> $r$. Its terms are $u_1, u_1 r, u_1 r^2, \dots$ For $u_1>0$, $r>1$ gives increasing terms and $0<r<1$ gives decreasing terms. With a negative first term the numerical direction reverses: $-2,-4,-8$ decreases although the magnitudes grow. The ratio formula assumes a nonzero previous term; the usual nonzero-term convention takes $u_1\ne0$ and $r\ne0$. A negative $r$ makes the signs alternate.

<Formula id="geometric-common-ratio"/>

The Oxford 2019 course book, printed p.291, reserves the name “geometric sequence” for $r\ne1$. Under the recurrence definition, $r=1$ is the constant special case. In either convention $u_n=u_1r^{n-1}$ still calculates the terms at $r=1$, while the quotient formula for the sum must be replaced by $S_n=nu_1$.

To find the $n$th term, start from $u_1$ and multiply by $r$ exactly $n - 1$ times.

<Formula id="geometric-nth-term"/>

<Callout kind="define">Geometric sequence: a sequence in which each term is the previous term multiplied by a constant non-zero ratio $r$.</Callout>

The <Term id="geometric-series"/> is the sum of the first $n$ terms. When $r \ne 1$ it is found from the formula below; when $r = 1$ all terms are equal and the sum is $n u_1$.

<Formula id="geometric-sum"/>

<Term id="sigma-notation-geometric"/> writes the same sum compactly, so $\sum_{k=1}^{n} u_1 r^{k-1}$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the series.

Geometric models describe growth or decay at a fixed percentage per period. Check the model against the data, and remember that a percentage change applies to the current amount, not to the starting amount.

## Worked example

Let $u_1 = 8$ and $r = 0.5$ for a geometric sequence. Find $u_4$ and $S_4$.

<Steps>
<Step>Find the 4th term: $u_4 = u_1 r^3 = 8 \times 0.5^3 = 1$.</Step>
<Step>Find the sum of the first 4 terms: $S_4 = \dfrac{8(1 - 0.5^4)}{1 - 0.5} = 15$. This agrees with adding the four terms directly.</Step>
</Steps>

Next, evaluate $\sum_{k=1}^{4} 3 \times 2^{k-1}$ by writing out the terms.

<Steps>
<Step>Substitute $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$: the terms are 3, 6, 12 and 24.</Step>
<Step>Add them: $3 + 6 + 12 + 24 = 45$, which agrees with $S_4 = \dfrac{3(2^4 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 45$.</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why does the formula need r not equal to 1?">When $r = 1$ the denominator $1 - r$ is zero, so the formula cannot be used. The sum is then simply $n u_1$, because every term equals $u_1$.</Reveal>

## Visual

<Callout kind="tip">Plot the terms against the term number. For a positive first term, a geometric sequence with $r > 1$ gives a curve that rises faster and faster, while $0 < r < 1$ gives a curve that falls and levels off near zero. The pattern is identified by equal ratios between neighbouring points, not by equal differences.</Callout>

## How it is examined

Marks depend on the particular task, not a universal ratio/formula/substitution pattern. Show the common ratio, term indexing and substitutions relevant to the requested output. Context questions need the model stated, with $u_1$ and $r$ taken from the wording, and an interpretation of the answer, such as whether the quantity is growing or decaying.

<Callout kind="exam">Check whether the question asks for the sum of $n$ terms or for the $n$th term, and whether the starting value counts as one of the terms. A drop of 40% per period gives $r = 0.6$, not $0.4$.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Using $r = 0.4$ for a 40% decrease. The common ratio is $1 - 0.4 = 0.6$.</Callout>

- Adding $r$ instead of multiplying by it, which turns the sequence into an arithmetic one.
- Using $u_1 r^{n}$ for the nth term instead of $u_1 r^{n-1}$, which shifts every term by one step.
- Applying the sum formula when $r = 1$, where the denominator is zero; the sum is $n u_1$ instead.

## Summary

- A geometric sequence multiplies by a constant non-zero ratio $r$ each step: $u_{n+1} = r u_n$.
- $u_n = u_1 r^{n-1}$ gives any term, and $r = u_{n+1}/u_n$ identifies the common ratio.
- $S_n = \dfrac{u_1(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ for $r \ne 1$ gives the sum of the first $n$ terms.
- For a positive first term, $r>1$ means growth and $0<r<1$ means decay; a negative nonzero $r$ alternates signs when terms are nonzero.
- Sigma notation $\sum_{k=1}^{n} u_1 r^{k-1}$ writes a geometric sum compactly.
- For growth or decay at rate $p$ per period, the ratio is $1 + p$ or $1 - p$, with $p$ as a decimal.
