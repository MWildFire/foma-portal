## Big idea

The second derivative measures how the gradient itself changes. Its sign tells you whether a curve bends upwards or downwards, and at a stationary point it tells you whether that point is a maximum or a minimum.

## Explanation

The derivative $f'(x)$ is the gradient function. Differentiate it once more and you get the <Term id="second-derivative"/>, written $f''(x)$, or $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ when $y = f(x)$.

<Formula id="second-derivative-notation"/>

Because $f''(x)$ is the rate of change of the gradient, its sign describes the bend of the curve, its <Term id="concavity"/>: the curve is concave up where $f''(x) > 0$, so the gradient is increasing, and concave down where $f''(x) < 0$, so the gradient is decreasing.

At a stationary point $x = a$, where $f'(a) = 0$, the <Term id="second-derivative-test"/> decides the type of point from the sign of $f''(a)$.

<Formula id="second-derivative-test-rule"/>

<Callout kind="define">Second derivative test: at a point where $f'(a) = 0$, a positive $f''(a)$ gives a local minimum, a negative $f''(a)$ gives a local maximum, and $f''(a) = 0$ settles nothing.</Callout>

<Callout kind="gdc">On a Calculator page, type `nDerivative(x^3-6*x^2+9*x+1,x=1,2)`: the last number is the order, so this is $f''(1)$, and it should agree with your hand value $-6$. Use your own function and $a$, then read the sign. Check with a table of $f'(x)$ either side of $a$ in `Menu → Table → Split-screen Table`: the gradient changes from positive to negative at a maximum and from negative to positive at a minimum.</Callout>

For a power of $x$ the second derivative follows a short rule: each differentiation lowers the power by one.

<Formula id="second-derivative-power"/>

## Worked example

<Steps>
<Step>Take $f(x) = x^{3} - 6x^{2} + 9x + 1$. Then $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)$, so the stationary points are at $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.</Step>
<Step>Differentiate again: $f''(x) = 6x - 12$. At $x = 1$, $f''(1) = -6 < 0$, so there is a local maximum with $f(1) = 5$. At $x = 3$, $f''(3) = 6 > 0$, so there is a local minimum with $f(3) = 1$.</Step>
<Step>For $g(x) = x e^{-x}$: $g'(x) = (1 - x)e^{-x}$, so $x = 1$ is stationary. Then $g''(1) = -e^{-1} = -0.368$ (3 decimal places), so this is a local maximum with $g(1) = e^{-1} = 0.368$ (3 decimal places).</Step>
</Steps>

<Reveal title="Why the test can fail">For $f(x) = x^{4}$ at the origin, the gradient and the second derivative are both zero, so the test gives no conclusion. The point is still a minimum, because the gradient is negative on the left and positive on the right. In that case read the sign of $f'$ on either side instead.</Reveal>

## How it is examined

Find and Determine questions give marks for differentiating twice (M1), for a correct second derivative (A1) and for a reasoned conclusion (R1). The reasoning mark needs both the sign of $f''(a)$ and the word maximum or minimum: a bare answer gets no reasoning mark. Show that questions expect the stationary condition $f'(a) = 0$ to be stated before the test is applied.

<Callout kind="exam">Write the stationary condition $f'(a) = 0$ first, then the sign of $f''(a)$, then the conclusion. That order is what the reasoning mark looks for.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout kind="mistake">Applying the second derivative test where $f'(a) \neq 0$. The test needs a stationary point first.</Callout>

- Classifying a point with $f'(a)$ instead of $f''(a)$.
- Concluding a maximum or minimum from $f''(a) = 0$. The test is inconclusive there.
- Differentiating once when the question asks for $f''$: $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x + 9$ is the gradient, not the second derivative.

## Summary

- The second derivative $f''(x)$ is the derivative of $f'(x)$, the rate of change of the gradient.
- Its sign gives concavity: $f''(x) > 0$ means concave up, and $f''(x) < 0$ means concave down.
- At a stationary point $x = a$, $f''(a) > 0$ gives a local minimum and $f''(a) < 0$ gives a local maximum.
- If $f''(a) = 0$ the test is inconclusive; check the sign of $f'$ on either side of $a$.
- In exam answers, state the stationary condition, the sign of the second derivative and the conclusion.
