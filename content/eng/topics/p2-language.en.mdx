## Big idea

Criterion D concerns clear, accurate and appropriate language. In a closed-book comparative essay, detailed references to the works can support the argument without memorised quotations.

## Explanation

The school materials allocate 5 of the 25 Paper 2 marks to language. Use appropriate vocabulary, grammar, sentence construction and register. A clear explanation with ordinary words can be stronger than inaccurate literary terminology. “Shows” is not banned; decide whether you mean a direct presentation, an implication, a contrast or a judgement.

The literary present is a useful convention for analysis: “the narrator hides”. Other tenses are appropriate for earlier events or historical context. Keep the relationships clear. Sentence length depends on the thought and syntax, not a three-line limit or a rule that every sentence must contain just one idea.

<Term id="diction"/> concerns word choice. Explain a word's <Term id="connotation"/> in its particular context instead of assuming that every occurrence has the same associations. Use a formal, readable <Term id="register"/> that suits an analytical essay.

<Callout kind="exam">Paper 2 is closed book. IB examiner instructions do not expect quotations: precise, detailed references to a scene, image, narrative choice or development can demonstrate knowledge. An accurately remembered quotation can help, but inventing or changing wording to fit an argument cannot.</Callout>

When you do quote, preserve the words. Integrate a short quotation grammatically or introduce a longer one clearly, then explain its significance. There is no quotation quota. A paraphrase should not be put in quotation marks.

## Worked example

This miniature passage is invented for practice, not quoted from a studied work: “I was, I suppose, alone. In every room I listened for her key.”

One supported reading: The narrator's hesitant admission, “I was, I suppose, alone”, understates a loss that the listening in “every room” makes difficult to escape. The qualification “I suppose” can suggest a reluctance to acknowledge that loss.

<Steps>
<Step>**Keep the evidence accurate.** Do not rewrite the source as “I was completely alone” and present it as a quotation.</Step>
<Step>**Connect the choice to its context.** “I suppose” is hesitant here; the following sentence supports an interpretation of painful absence. The phrase alone would not prove grief.</Step>
<Step>**Offer a quotation-free reference.** The narrator hesitates to admit being alone and continues to listen for another person's arrival in every room. This precise reference can support the same analysis without claiming to reproduce exact words.</Step>
<Step>**Compare in a real essay.** Set this choice beside a specific choice in the second studied work and explain how the comparison answers the actual prompt. These invented lines cannot substitute for knowledge of your selected works.</Step>
</Steps>

## Visual

| Evidence | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Exact remembered wording | Quote it accurately and explain its significance |
| A precisely remembered scene | Describe it in your own words and analyse the author's choice |
| An uncertain phrase | Paraphrase the supported detail; do not invent a quotation |
| A vague claim such as “the language is good” | Specify the language choice and its effect |

## How it is examined

Criterion D is assessed across the response. The examiner needs to follow a coherent comparison expressed in clear, accurate, appropriate language. There is no automatic mark for using a particular verb, a quotation or a literary term.

## Common mistakes

- Treating memorised quotations as a requirement for Paper 2.
- Altering source words while keeping quotation marks.
- Giving an isolated quotation or plot detail with no analysis.
- Using a term whose meaning does not fit the example.
- Claiming an interpretation is certain when the evidence supports alternatives.

## Summary

- Express the comparison clearly and use accurate, appropriate language.
- Support analysis with specific references; quotations are optional in Paper 2.
- Preserve quoted wording and distinguish a paraphrase from a quotation.
- Explain the significance of a word or choice in its context.
- Use tense and sentence structure to make the argument easy to follow.
